Welcome to Western Australia

A warm welcome, you’re in good hands

Deciding to move your life and career to the other side of the world is a big step, and an exciting one. Whether Western Australia is already home to people you know or a brand-new chapter, I want you to feel supported from the very first conversation.

WA is a place of remarkable space and lifestyle: white-sand beaches, the Swan and Margaret River wine regions, and some of the cleanest air and biggest skies anywhere. The pace is relaxed, the outdoors is everything, and the healthcare job market, across Perth and the regional centres, is strong and genuinely welcoming to international staff.

This guide walks you through healthcare, visas and registration, finances, housing, schooling and cost of living across the state, then breaks down the main places you might live so you can picture daily life in each. Think of it as the conversation we’d have over coffee, in your pocket, whenever you need it.

Welcome to Western Australia. We’re so glad you’re here, and we’ll be alongside you every step of the way.

Sophie Careem
Founder & Director

"A good move is measured in years, not weeks, so we stay with you long after you land."

Warm regards,

Ethicare insight

Candidates often picture Australia as one job market. It isn’t, WA registers nationally through Ahpra but hires locally, and demand in the regions frequently outpaces Perth. That’s where the strongest packages tend to be.

Overview · Culture

Ancient country, modern lifestyle

The Whadjuk Noongar people are the traditional custodians of the Perth region, part of one of the oldest living cultures on earth, a heritage increasingly woven into WA life.

WA blends a deep Aboriginal heritage with a young, multicultural population drawn by mining, medicine and the good life. Perth is one of the most isolated major cities in the world, closer to Bali than to Sydney, which gives it a distinct, self-reliant character. Festivals like Perth Festival and Fringe World light up the summer, and the Margaret River region has become a byword for food, wine and surf.

Sophie’s tip

Learn the dual place names early, Boorloo (Perth), Walyalup (Fremantle). Using them warmly is genuinely appreciated and helps you feel part of the community faster.

Overview · Transport

Getting around

Perth has a genuinely good public-transport network, but WA’s scale means a car is useful for the regions. Here’s how people get around day to day.

Driving

Australians drive on the left. Perth is easy to drive and park; a car is near-essential in Mandurah, Bunbury and the regional centres.

Transperth

Trains, buses and ferries run on the tap-on SmartRider card, with fast rail reaching Mandurah and the northern suburbs.

Flying

Perth Airport links to the eastern capitals (~4–5 hrs) and regional WA; Qantas, Virgin and Jetstar are the main carriers.

Cycling

Perth’s flat terrain and extensive riverside paths make it one of Australia’s most bike-friendly cities.

Sophie’s tip

You can usually drive on a valid overseas licence for a period after arriving, but you’ll need to transfer to a WA licence once you’re a resident. Keep your licence and any official translation handy, and start a SmartRider from day one.

Sophie’s View

Why I like Western Australia

One of the things I love most about Western Australia is the sense of space it gives you. Whether you’re drawn to Perth’s easy city life, a coastal town or a close-knit regional community, there’s usually somewhere that fits your priorities. For healthcare professionals, that flexibility means you rarely have to choose between career progression and quality of life, you can often have both.

Having spent many years in the NHS, I know how easily work can take over. What stands out to me about WA is how much people value the hours outside it, a swim before a shift, a weekend among the vines. If I could give one piece of advice, it would be to think about your commute, your community and what you’ll enjoy on your days off. Those things shape your happiness more than people expect.

Sophie

Section two · Healthcare

Healthcare in Western Australia

WA’s health system combines universal Medicare cover with a strong network of public hospitals, led by Perth’s tertiary centres, and a substantial private sector.

Go deeperUnderstanding healthcare in Australia→

Fiona Stanley Hospital

WA’s flagship tertiary hospital in Murdoch, south of Perth, a major trauma, surgical and specialist centre.

Royal Perth & Sir Charles Gairdner

Long-established tertiary hospitals serving central and northern Perth, with major emergency and specialist services.

Regional hospitals

Bunbury, Geraldton and Kalgoorlie each have a regional hospital; Mandurah is served by Peel Health Campus.

Medicare & private

Medicare funds public care for eligible residents; private hospitals offer elective procedures and shorter waits.

Good to know

Eligibility for Medicare usually begins once you hold an appropriate visa. Reciprocal Health Care Agreements give some countries limited Medicare access. Ambulance cover is not automatic in WA, St John Ambulance runs a subscription scheme.

Registration · Ahpra

Professional registration

Most healthcare professions are regulated nationally through Ahpra, the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency, and its profession-specific boards.

One national register

Once registered with Ahpra you can work anywhere in Australia, including all of WA, registration is national, not state-by-state.

Qualification assessment

Overseas qualifications are assessed against Australian standards; some roles require bridging programs or exams.

English language

You’ll usually need to evidence English proficiency through an approved test or a recognised pathway.

Start early

Registration takes time, begin gathering documents and references well before you plan to travel.

Ethicare insight

In our experience, WA start dates slip for one reason above all: documents gathered too late. Certified copies of your qualifications, references and ID take longer to pull together than anyone expects, so start the day you’re seriously weighing the move.

Section four · Banking & Finance

Setting up your finances

Getting your finances in order early makes day-to-day life much easier. Most of this can be started before you arrive.

Opening a bank account

The major banks are CommBank, Westpac, NAB and ANZ, plus WA’s own Bankwest. Many let you open online before you arrive, then verify ID in branch with your passport.

Tax File Number (TFN)

Every worker needs a TFN from the ATO. Apply online once you arrive and give the number to payroll so you’re taxed correctly. Without it, you’ll be taxed at the highest rate.

Sophie’s tip

Open your bank account before you fly where you can, having an account ready makes your payroll setup and first rent payment far smoother in those hectic first weeks.

The money set-up, in order

1Activate your bank account, verify ID in branch, order a debit card and online banking.
2Apply for your TFN, online via the ATO, then give it to payroll immediately.
3Choose your super fund, or accept the employer default, then review later.
4Set up salary packaging, ask payroll or your provider on day one if you’re in public health.
5Register for Medicare, if eligible under your visa or a reciprocal agreement.
6Get a local SIM & number, many banking and government steps need an Australian mobile.
Sophie’s tip

Salary is only half the story. The people who settle into Perth most smoothly are the ones who budget for the bridge, the few weeks between arriving and your first full pay clearing. Think about temporary accommodation, a rental bond (typically four weeks’ rent), a first car, and setting up a home. None of it is dramatic, but together it’s real, and planning for it removes almost all of the early stress.

Sophie Careem · Founder & Director

Section seven · Housing & Areas

Finding a place to live

From Perth apartments to coastal family homes and regional towns, WA offers space and value, and where you live shapes your commute, so think about transport links to work.

Most rentals are advertised online and move quickly in popular areas. You’ll usually apply with ID, references and proof of income, then pay a bond (typically four weeks’ rent) lodged with the Bond Administrator. Many newcomers rent for a few months first to get to know an area before committing. The main sites are realestate.com.au (largest), domain.com.au and WA-local reiwa.com.au.

Choosing your area, the order that saves the most stress

1Fix your work site first. Everything flows from your commute, decide it before you fall for a suburb.
2Check school catchments if you have children. Local intake is guaranteed; out-of-area isn’t.
3Shortlist two or three areas and rent short-term in one before committing.
4Confirm the exact address is in-catchment before you sign, boundaries can split a street.

Utilities & getting set up

Power

Synergy (Perth) / Horizon Power (regional). Set up on move-in.

Water

Water Corporation; if renting, rates are usually the landlord’s.

Internet

NBN via Telstra, Optus, TPG or Aussie Broadband.

Setting up home

Kmart, IKEA and Bunnings furnish a first home fast; Marketplace for second-hand.

Sophie’s tip

No Australian rental references? Bring landlord references from home, proof of income and savings, and your job offer. A strong, complete application often beats a longer local history.

Housing & family · Choosing where to live

Choosing where to live

For many healthcare professionals, the job is only half the decision. The other half is where your family will settle well. Schools, commute, housing cost and lifestyle are best weighed together. Here’s a practical way to think it through.

Schools often come first

Access to a government school is usually tied to where you live. Popular schools can be pinned to an exact address, catchments can change street by street, and out-of-area places aren’t guaranteed. If you have children, check the catchment before you sign a long-term lease. Independent, Catholic and faith schools offer more flexibility, but usually charge fees.

Your commute still matters

In Perth, driving is easy and parking is usually manageable, but a car is near-essential in Mandurah, Bunbury and the regional centres. Think about your shift start and finish times, whether the Transperth trains and buses realistically reach your site, and how you’ll feel travelling early or late.

The order that usually works best

A simple sequence that keeps the big decisions in the right order.

  1. 1Confirm your likely work site.
  2. 2Shortlist suitable school options if you have children.
  3. 3Check the catchment maps for those schools.
  4. 4Compare commute times to work at shift hours.
  5. 5Review rental availability and cost.
  6. 6Rent short-term first where you can.
  7. 7Choose a longer-term suburb once you know the area.

Section eight · Shopping & Food

Groceries, food & community

The essentials in one place, the big chains, the budget picks, and where different communities find the flavours of home.

The big chains

Woolworths & Coles for full-range everywhere; ALDI for the cheapest weekly shop; IGA and independents for neighbourhood top-ups, vital in regional towns.

Flavours of home

Specialty importers stock South African and British staples; South Asian & Halal in Mirrabooka, Cannington and Victoria Park; Asian groceries in Northbridge (Perth’s Chinatown) and the suburbs.

Sophie’s tip

Bring your own bags, single-use plastic bags are banned across WA, and most people keep a few reusable ones in the car for exactly this reason.

Good to know

In Perth, halal butchers and grocers are easy to find (Mirrabooka, Cannington, Victoria Park) and kosher goods come through the Perth Hebrew Congregation and specialty importers. In the regional centres it’s more limited: Bunbury, Geraldton and Kalgoorlie have some halal-certified outlets and the major supermarkets carry a growing halal range, but dedicated kosher supplies are largely Perth-based, many regional families stock up on a Perth trip or order online.

Faith & community · Places of worship

Religion & places of worship

Australia has no state religion and law protects freedom of belief. WA is religiously diverse: at the last census a little under half of residents reported no religion, Christianity remained the largest faith, and Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism and Judaism are all established and growing, most strongly across the Perth metropolitan area, with active communities in the regional centres too.

Most communities list service and prayer times online and warmly welcome newcomers, often one of the quickest ways to build a support network after you arrive.

The main faiths & where to find them

Christianity

Catholic, Anglican, Uniting, Baptist, Pentecostal and Orthodox churches statewide. Landmark cathedrals include St Mary’s (Catholic) and St George’s (Anglican) in Perth and St Francis Xavier in Geraldton.

Islam

Perth Mosque (William St, Northbridge, WA’s oldest, 1906) plus mosques and musallas across the suburbs, and in Bunbury, Geraldton, Kalgoorlie and Katanning. See the Islamic Council of WA directory.

Hinduism

Perth Hindu Temple (Canning Vale) and the Shree Swaminarayan Mandir serve a large community, with cultural and festival programmes through the year.

Buddhism

Several temples and meditation centres, including Fo Guang Shan Buddhist Temple WA and forest monasteries in the hills east of Perth.

Sikhism

Gurdwaras in the Perth metropolitan area welcome worshippers and visitors, with community kitchens (langar) open to all.

Judaism

The Perth Hebrew Congregation and Progressive congregations serve WA’s Jewish community, mostly in Perth’s northern suburbs, alongside kosher supplies and community services.

By location, what you’ll find near you

A guide to the main faith communities in each city in this guide. Perth metro has the fullest range; the regional centres focus on Christian churches and a local mosque.

Section ten · Education & Family

Schooling & family life

WA offers a full range of early childhood education, schools and universities, run by the state’s Department of Education. The school year runs late January to mid-December across four terms, a flip from the Northern Hemisphere calendar worth planning around.

Go deeperEducation & schools in Australia→

Early childhood

Long daycare, kindergarten and family day care for ages 0–5, with subsidies. Kindergarten begins the year a child turns 4.

Primary

From Pre-primary (first compulsory year) through Year 6, across public, Catholic and independent schools.

Secondary

Years 7–12, ending with the WA Certificate of Education (WACE), leading to university or training.

Tertiary

UWA, Curtin, Murdoch and Edith Cowan universities, plus TAFE (vocational) colleges across the state.

Public school fees for visa-holder families

WA-specific and important to budget for: families on skilled work visas pay a public school tuition fee, managed by TAFE International WA (TIWA).

Ethicare insight

Families on a 457 or 482 visa typically pay around A$4,000 per family per year (indicative, confirm current TIWA rates), regardless of how many children are enrolled. No fee applies while your oldest child is still in Kindergarten. You can pay upfront or by instalments, and your employer can sometimes pay it for you.

School catchments come first

For WA public schools, your home address determines your local-intake school, children inside the area are guaranteed a place, while out-of-area places depend on capacity. Boundaries can run street-by-street, so always check the exact address before you sign.

For families, the school decision usually leads the housing decision here, deciding it first prevents an expensive mid-year move.

Education & Family · Partners & work

Employment for your partner

A move only works if it works for the whole family. The good news: on most skilled and employer-sponsored visas your partner is included with full work rights, free to take any job, full or part-time, with any employer.

Work rights come as standard

Partners added to a 482, 186, or skilled 189/190/491 visa generally have unrestricted work rights, no separate employer sponsorship needed. Always confirm the exact conditions on your grant letter.

A broad, healthy job market

WA has low unemployment and demand across healthcare, education, mining & resources, construction, trades, retail, hospitality and professional services, strongest in Perth, with real opportunities in the regional centres too.

Helping your partner land work

1Reformat the CV to Australian style, concise, achievement-led, no photo or date of birth. Set up a strong LinkedIn with an Australian location.
2Search the main boards, SEEK, Indeed and Jora, plus WA Health’s careers site and local council pages for public-sector roles.
3Check if the profession is regulated, teaching, nursing, trades and others need registration or a skills assessment. Start it before you arrive.
4Register with recruitment agencies, many WA roles are filled through agencies, who can also advise on local expectations and pay.
Ethicare insight

Partner employment is one of the biggest factors in a move succeeding, so we treat it as part of the plan, not an afterthought. Tell us early what your partner does; we can point them to the right boards, agencies and registration steps, and factor their prospects into which city or region we recommend. If your partner is also in healthcare, we can often help them directly.

Section twelve · Your Ethicare Journey

We guide you through every stage

Relocating overseas can feel overwhelming. From the first conversation to long after you arrive, we’re here to support you, the same six-step journey you’ll find on our website.

Stage 1 of six

Discovery call

Understanding your goals, your family situation and whether Australia or New Zealand may be the right fit.

Stage 2 of six

Eligibility & registration

Assessing registration requirements and helping you understand your options clearly.

Stage 3 of six

Opportunities & interviews

Discussing locations, employers and salaries, then preparing you for interviews and what employers look for.

Stage 4 of six

Offer & decision support

Helping you evaluate opportunities and make the decision that’s right for you.

Stage 5 of six

Visa & relocation guidance

Supporting you through the practicalities of moving overseas.

Stage 6 of six

Settling in

Ongoing support as you begin your new life and career, long after you arrive.
