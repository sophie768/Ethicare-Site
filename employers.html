Whether you can

Can we hire internationally at all?

Almost always yes, if it is a role a board will register and the timeline is workable. Four things decide it.

01 · The gate

Registration decides everything after it

Whether the clinician can register with the relevant board sets the visa, the start date and the offer. We check it before anyone’s time is spent.

See the registration checker →
02 · The route

The visa follows the pathway

Once the board route is clear, the work visa follows a known process. We sequence it and keep the file moving; the decisions stay with the immigration authority. This is coordination, not immigration advice.

03 · The clock

A likely timeline

For the professions we cover, offer to arrival is typically five to nine months. We tell you where your role is likely to sit before you commit.

04 · The fine print

Supervision and accredited posts

Some boards expect a supervised period, or a post that meets an accreditation standard, before a clinician can work unsupervised. Knowing this before you advertise avoids an appointment that cannot start.

Who's out there

Who’s out there?

Ask us. We can tell you who we are already talking to in your profession and region, and exactly where each person stands on registration.

How candidates are assessed

  • Verified against the board — profession, registration route and stage, so “available” means a date.
  • A start date read from the process, not from optimism.
  • The regions they would move to, so a shortlist fits the posting.
  • The household — a partner’s work, children’s schooling, the questions that decide whether a family settles.

How introductions work

  • One named person at a time, with their consent.
  • Contact details released only on acceptance.
  • Nothing is shared beyond the conversation, and every tool stays free to the clinician, however they got their job.

Tell us the role and the region and we will answer personally. A registration-verified availability view for employers is in development; until it is live, that answer comes from us.

Whether they stay

Will they stay?

Retention is a relocation question, and it is the work we are built around.

“Sophie always puts candidate care first, and has invested heavily in preparing candidates for a successful move — ensuring they have as much support as possible when relocating to a new country.”

Maree LoynesFormer Allied Health Recruitment Specialist, Health New Zealand MidCentral

A regional allied health role that had stayed open

The challenge

A regional allied health service, one role open long enough that the gap had started to feel normal. Local advertising hadn’t moved it, and the location was never going to sell itself from a job description alone.

The search

A small, targeted search. The decisive conversations weren’t about the role: where the candidate stood with the board, what their partner would do, and whether the timeline was realistic for a family.

The outcome

Registered, relocated and still in post — so the service filled a role it had stopped expecting to fill, and didn’t have to run the whole process again a year later.

Why it holds: we don’t stop at arrival

The first year is where a placement is won or lost, so it’s where we stay — personal coordination of registration, visa and the move itself, then contact through settling in: the bank account, the GP, the school place, the fortnight when a family wonders whether they got it right. For clinicians we place it’s included as standard; for your direct hires it’s available by arrangement.
