Psychologists moving to Australia

One national register, and a route that starts the same way for everyone who trained overseas: provisional registration first, then a supervised period inside an Australian job. Here is what that actually asks of you, in order.

The Psychology Board of Australia, through Ahpra. One register for every state and territory.

Can I work there?

Yes — through provisional registration first. Which of these is you?

Australia does not look at your training country so much as the shape of your training: how many years, at what level, with how much supervised practice. Choose the one that fits and the route below rewrites itself.

The Board has already read your accreditation

The Psychology Board has approved certain accredited qualifications as substantially equivalent to its own sequence. From the United Kingdom, a BPS-accredited qualification that fulfils eligibility for Chartered Membership counts as a fifth-and-sixth year of study; one that gives Graduate Membership, or fulfils Stage One towards Chartered status, counts as the four-year sequence. From Ireland, a PSI-accredited honours or conversion course at 2:2 or above counts as the four-year sequence. From the United States, any programme on the APA accredited list counts as a fifth-and-sixth year. The Board describes the BPS route as streamlined.

What it settles, and what it doesn’t. It answers the qualification criteria. The Board still looks at your supervised practice, English, recency of practice, criminal history and insurance before it says which outcome applies to you. We don’t name individual UK degrees here, because the Board points at the BPS course list rather than naming them — check your own award against it.

Board: Meeting the registration requirements, page reviewed 18 Aug 2025; read 9 Sep 2026.

The route, in the Board’s order

  1. Ask your institution to send your original transcripts direct to Ahpra — it often takes several weeks, so start here.
  2. Apply for provisional registration as an internationally qualified applicant: overseas assessment fee A$718, registration fee A$473.
  3. Answer any request for more within 30 days, or the file closes. Ask for more time if you need it.
  4. Receive your outcome — one of the four below. Meeting the fifth-and-sixth-year criteria is what makes the transitional program the one to expect, but the Board decides case by case.
  5. Find an Australian psychology role whose workplace will host a supervision plan with a Board-approved supervisor; the plan is approved before you start.
  6. At least three continuous months at 17.5 hours a week or more. Sit the national exam (A$450) during it.
  7. Apply for general registration with your supervisor’s assessment: A$580 application, A$473 registration.

Eleven criteria, and you don’t need all of them

Your qualifications are assessed against the Board’s eleven criteria. They test the level of your award against the Australian sequence (a four-year sequence at AQF 8, a fifth year, or a fifth and sixth year at AQF 9), whether your programme was externally accredited and your institution registered to teach, whether what you were taught matches the APAC competencies at each level, and your supervised practice — at least 1,500 hours, or at least 3,000, with competence shown in eight named areas from assessment and intervention to working across the lifespan and with diverse groups. The pattern you meet decides the requirement you are given; the Board is explicit that the purpose of the assessment is to set your supervised-practice period, not to say yes or no.

Two things to check before you pay. The only discipline name in your qualification’s title must be psychology. And the Board may ask for the syllabus of every unit you studied — learning outcomes, assessments, textbooks — translated at your cost.

Board: Assessment of applications, page reviewed 22 Aug 2025; read 9 Sep 2026.

The route, in the Board’s order

  1. Ask your institution to send your original transcripts direct to Ahpra; gather supervision logs, reports and position descriptions for your internship.
  2. Apply for provisional registration: overseas assessment fee A$718, registration fee A$473.
  3. Answer any request — syllabus, translations, evidence of hours — within 30 days.
  4. Receive your outcome, one of the four below, and re-plan around it before you resign from anything.
  5. If it is the transitional program: find a role whose workplace will host your approved supervision plan.
  6. Three continuous months or more at 17.5 hours a week; the exam (A$450) during it.
  7. General registration: A$580 application, A$473 registration.

Two conditions open the Trans-Tasman door

Current New Zealand registration and a current practising certificate that allows you to practise as a psychologist in New Zealand. With both, you apply online under the Trans-Tasman Mutual Recognition Act 1997, and the assessment on the other tabs does not apply to you. Without the current right to practise in New Zealand — registration that has lapsed, or New Zealand training that never led to registration — you take the overseas route like everyone else.

The Board’s registration standards, code of conduct and guidelines apply to you from the day you are registered, so read them before you apply rather than after.

Board: Overseas applicants, page reviewed 13 Jul 2026; read 9 Sep 2026.

What changes for you

  1. Confirm both conditions are current on the day you apply — the practising certificate is the one people let lapse.
  2. Apply online for registration under mutual recognition.
  3. Read the Board’s registration standards and code of conduct; they bind you on registration.
  4. The visa is separate and unaffected by mutual recognition. Visas and residence

A title is an endorsement, decided separately

Australia has no specialist registration in psychology. “Clinical psychologist” and the other approved areas — nine in all — are endorsements added to general registration, assessed on their own. You can hold general registration and practise within your competence without one; what you cannot do is use the endorsed title. The usual route is an approved postgraduate qualification in the area followed by a registrar program of supervised practice, and its length is set by your qualification, not your experience: 88 weeks and 3,000 hours after a Masters, 66 weeks and 2,250 hours after a combined Masters/PhD, 44 weeks and 1,500 hours after a Doctorate. The Board is explicit that experience gained before the qualification, in another profession, or outside the program does not shorten it.

If you have already done the equivalent. Comparable post-qualification supervised practice completed elsewhere can be put forward for endorsement on its own basis, on a separate form (AEAE-76). That is the application an experienced overseas clinical psychologist should ask about first.

Board: Registrar program, page reviewed 8 Jul 2025; fees schedule effective 18 Sep 2025; read 9 Sep 2026.

In order

  1. General registration first — the endorsement cannot be applied for without it.
  2. Decide whether you need the title now. Roles that name it in the advert are the reason to; practising without it is not a failure state.
  3. Comparable supervised practice already completed: apply on that basis (AEAE-76). Otherwise: a Board-approved supervisor who holds the endorsement, a role that meets the practice requirements, a program approved before you start.
  4. Fees: A$291 to apply for endorsement, A$372 for the qualification assessment, A$291 to have a registrar program approved.

The four possible outcomes

Provisional registration is granted so that you can complete one of these. Three of the four are a year or more of Australian study or internship — a different move. Budget for the outcome, not the hope.

Outcome 1

The transitional program

At least three continuous months of supervised practice, plus the national exam if required. The outcome a fully trained, internship-complete clinician is aiming for.

Outcome 2

Postgraduate study

A Board-approved qualification accredited as a fifth, or fifth and sixth, year of study (AQF 9 Masters or higher).

Outcome 3

A fifth year, then an internship

A fifth-year qualification followed by a 5+1 internship program and the national exam.

Outcome 4

The 5+1 internship

A Board-approved 5+1 internship program and the national exam.

If the Board proposes to refuse you, you have 30 days to make a submission before the decision is final. A refusal refunds the registration fee; the A$718 assessment fee is never refunded. An application that does not supply what is asked is withdrawn, not refused — which is why the 30-day notices matter.

Registration isn’t the only check

Seven other gates, decided by different people. Settle the slow ones first.

  • English. The Board’s English language skills standard — an exemption or a test, per-component minimums, results that expire. The requirement, plainly
  • Criminal history. Police certificates from every country you have lived in.
  • Recency of practice. A gap is not a barrier, but it means more evidence.
  • Professional indemnity insurance. A registration standard, arranged before you practise.
  • The visa, and health. Home Affairs, not the Board; medicals for everyone on the application. Visas and residence
  • Employer credentialling and references. Referees who directly supervised your clinical work.
  • A migration skills assessment. The Australian Psychological Society, only if your visa route needs it — it never permits practice.

Salary and working life

Two layers of pay, and no single national number

Australia sets a national minimum and then lets each state, and each funder, build on it. The honest figure for you depends on the state, your level and who is paying for the work — so we don’t print one. We confirm the instrument, the level and the pay point in writing before you decide anything.

Layer one · the floor

A national award

A legal minimum, the same in every state, published by the Fair Work Ombudsman. A floor, not a forecast — if an offer sits near it, that tells you something.

Layer two · public services

State and territory agreements

Public health services pay under their own enterprise agreements, so the same title genuinely pays differently in Perth and in Hobart. Level and pay point rest on evidenced experience, including overseas experience.

Layer three · the market

Private practice and everything else

Medicare-subsidised referrals and private fees, disability-scheme funding, employer-funded workplace services — each a different arrangement, and none of them a salary scale.

The funder decides the door — and therefore the week

The week

Large multidisciplinary teams, defined referral pathways, and the full weight of the system around complex care — adult community, acute and inpatient, child and adolescent, older persons, forensic. Caseload and the balance of crisis and continuing care vary by state and between metropolitan and regional services; ask rather than assume.

The pay instrument

The enterprise agreement for that state or territory’s public health service. Ask for the classification and pay point in writing, not just the salary.

For your supervised period

The setting most likely to hold Board-approved supervisors and a structure that can host a plan — which is the question to ask at interview.

The week

Independent or group practice on Medicare-subsidised referrals and private fees. A caseload you shape, an appointment structure you set, and the setting a large share of Australian psychologists work in.

The pay instrument

None — income follows fees, referrals and provider eligibility. Whether an endorsement changes what you can charge or claim is a question we check for you, not one we answer here.

For your supervised period

Possible, if the practice can meet the Board’s plan and supervision requirements — and you hold a role there for the whole period.

The week

Disability-scheme work built around function and capacity rather than episodic treatment; employer-funded workplace and EAP services; community-managed alcohol and drug services; rural, remote and telehealth roles with generalist scope over very large areas.

The pay instrument

Set by the organisation and the funding stream, above the award floor. The same qualification can be worth a different amount under a different funder.

For your supervised period

Check that the role is a psychological position in the Board’s sense and that supervision can be arranged before you count on it.

Where could I live?

Four states, not four suburbs of one

Because public pay and service structure are set state by state, where you land changes the job as well as the life. These four are a start, written about the place — none of our destination chapters is written for psychologists yet, and we would rather say so than pretend.

Victoria. The deepest hospital system in the country, suburbs for every budget and a cultural life that never runs dry — and the widest choice of public, private and community mental-health settings in one city.

Queensland. Capital-city medicine, winter sun and housing that undercuts Sydney and Melbourne — with the state’s regional and remote services a real part of what a psychologist is asked to cover.

Western Australia. Beaches better than the east coast’s, an easier big-city life and a system that needs you — at a distance from the rest of the country that suits some households and not others.

Where people get stuck

Ten things that cost psychologists months, and none of them is the exam

Every one of these comes from the Board’s own pages or from the questions we are asked. None is a reason not to go; each is a reason to know it now.

Working out your pathway

  1. Starting with the APS. The Australian Psychological Society assesses qualifications for migration. It is not the registration gateway, and a positive assessment does not let you practise.
  2. Reading “provisional” as a verdict. It is the category you hold while completing the supervised period, not the Board’s view of your career. You keep practising under it while general registration is decided.
  3. Planning for one route. There are four outcomes, and three of them mean a year or more of Australian study or internship. Wait for the outcome before you resign from anything.
  4. The 30-day notice. Miss it and the application is withdrawn, assessment fee gone. Ask for more time before it runs out.
  5. Trans-Tasman without the certificate. New Zealand registration on its own is not enough; the practising certificate has to be current too.

Provisionally registered, and finding it hard

  1. A workplace that will host the plan. The supervised period needs an Australian psychology role and a supervision plan the Board approves before you start. Ask at interview, in those words.
  2. A Board-approved supervisor before day one. The Board publishes a supervisor register; find the person before you sign the contract.
  3. General registration without the title the advert names. You can practise; you cannot call yourself a clinical psychologist until the endorsement is granted, and it is decided separately.
  4. Expecting experience to shorten the registrar program. It doesn’t. Comparable supervised practice already completed is a different application — make that one.
  5. The visa and the three months not overlapping. You cannot finish the pathway from abroad, so the right to work has to cover the whole supervised period.

If you are in the second column now, tell us where it has stuck — that conversation is free, and it is the one we are most useful in.

From Sophie Draft — her own words to follow

The psychologists who write to us are rarely asking whether Australia will have them. They are asking why a fifteen-year career arrives as “provisional”. It is a fair question, and the honest answer is that the word describes a stage, not a judgement — three months of supervised practice inside a real job, with the exam sat along the way.

What I would ask you to do first is not the paperwork. It is to read the four outcomes and be honest about which one your training points to. Everything else — the role, the state, the school run — follows from that answer.

Sophie Careem · Founder, Ethicare Resourcing
