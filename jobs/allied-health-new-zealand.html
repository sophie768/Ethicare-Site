Career guide · Allied health · New Zealand

The professions that get
people back to their lives.

Five disciplines, one shared job: function, independence and participation — in a country whose accident scheme funds more of that work than almost anywhere else. This page is here to help you understand the career before you go looking for a role; live vacancies sit further down.

One shared job

Different professions, the same question

Medicine asks what is wrong. Allied health asks what this person needs to be able to do — walk, speak, work, cook, get home. The disciplines answer it differently, and in New Zealand they usually answer it together.

Physiotherapy

Movement, function and rehabilitation, from ICU to sports field — with direct access and ACC funding shaping the private side.

Occupational therapy

Daily living, home and workplace, equipment, cognition and participation — across physical and mental health.

Speech-language therapy

Communication and swallowing, from stroke wards to school classrooms.

Podiatry

Foot health, diabetes-related risk, biomechanics and keeping people mobile.

Dietetics

Clinical nutrition across acute care, community health and long-term conditions.

Worth knowing before you search

ACC is why this family’s job market looks different here. New Zealand’s no-fault accident scheme funds treatment and rehabilitation directly — which is why a large private and community sector exists alongside the public one, why physiotherapists see accident patients without a referral, and why “private practice” here often means publicly funded work in a private clinic.

It also means more of the week is rehabilitation: funded to get someone back to work and life, not just discharged. How the funding works — worth ten minutes before any interview.

Private & ACC-funded

Clinics, providers & contracts

ACC-contracted rehabilitation, private clinics, aged care, schools and occupational health. Public or private is not the same job with a different employer — the funder changes your caseload, your autonomy and how long you have with each person.

Where the work happens

Where might that week take you?

Less of it in a hospital than you might expect. Allied health here works wherever the person’s life happens — and the further from a main centre, the broader the caseload.

Group one

Hospital & rehabilitation

The clinical core, and where most overseas clinicians start.

Acute wards & ICU

Respiratory physio, early mobilisation, acute swallowing and nutrition — the sharpest end of the family’s work.

Inpatient rehab units

Stroke, neuro, orthopaedic and older-person rehabilitation, run as goal-based MDT programmes.

Outpatient clinics

Booked lists across musculoskeletal, neuro, paediatric, hand therapy and specialist clinics.

Across the family

What applies across allied health

The things that are the same whichever of these professions you hold.

Who regulates this work?+

Each registered profession has its own board: the Physiotherapy Board, the Occupational Therapy Board, the Podiatrists Board and the Dietitians Board. Speech-language therapy is the exception — there is no statutory register, so employers rely on your qualification and New Zealand Speech-language Therapists’ Association membership.

Registration is the first gate, and it runs on its own timeline. How registration works · Check your pathway

What does the pay look like?+

Public-system pay is set by collective agreement rather than negotiated role by role, so your base salary comes from a national scale rather than a negotiation with the hospital. Your step on that scale, your hours and your on-call commitment are what vary. ACC-funded and private work sits outside that and varies with the contract.

Indicative bands sit on each profession’s own guide above. Pay in New Zealand

Which visa would I need?+

Most clinicians move on a work visa tied to an accredited employer. Physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech-language therapists, dietitians and social workers all sit in Tier 1 of the Green List — Straight to Residence — which changes the route considerably. Checked against Immigration New Zealand’s Green List (Appendix 13, in force from 9 March 2026); the list changes, so confirm it against your own occupation before you plan around it.

The visa can usually run alongside registration. Visas and residence
