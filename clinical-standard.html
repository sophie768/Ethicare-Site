Clinical governance

The Ethicare Clinical Standard

What we establish before a clinician is put forward, what we verify ourselves, what the professional boards verify, and the circumstances in which we advise someone not to move yet.

StatusApproved by the clinical board · 8 September 2026
Versionv1.0
Held byEthicare clinical board
Next reviewAnnually, or on regulatory change
Why we wrote this down

Recruitment has an incentive problem. This is our answer to it.

Agencies are paid when someone takes a job. That is a poor foundation for advice, and clinicians know it — which is why a recruiter saying “this is the right move for you” carries so little weight.

We cannot remove the incentive. What we can do is publish the standard we hold ourselves to, put it in front of senior clinicians who have no commercial interest in any placement, and let it be checked. A standard you can read is worth more than a promise you cannot.

This document is written for two readers. If you are a clinician, it tells you what we will ask of you and what we will not pretend to have checked. If you are an employer, it tells you exactly what has happened before a CV reaches you, and where each stage of verification actually sits.

The standard

Seven things we establish before anyone is put forward

Screening a CV takes minutes. Establishing whether someone will still be in post in two years takes conversations.

01

Registration

Where this clinician genuinely stands with the relevant board — not whether they intend to apply, but what has been submitted, what is outstanding and what the realistic timeline is. Registration is the longest lead time in any move, and the most common cause of a placement collapsing late.

Not yet: documents unobtainable, or a pathway that has not been confirmed.
02

Motivation

Why this move, why now, and whether the reason will still hold in eighteen months. A clinician moving towards something stays. A clinician moving away from something often does not.

Not yet: a decision made in the middle of a crisis at home or at work.
03

Salary

What they need, what the relevant agreement or scale actually pays, and whether those two numbers meet. We name the framework and let the employer confirm the package. If the gap cannot close, we say so before anyone interviews.

Not yet: an expectation the market cannot meet.
04

Family

Who else is moving, and whether they are moving willingly. Partner employment, children’s schooling and, where relevant, the health requirements that apply to every member of an application. The most common reason a placement fails in year one has nothing to do with the job.

Not yet: a partner who has not agreed, or a health question unresolved.
05

Location

Whether they have understood the place, not just the country. Isolation, distance from a base hospital, the cost of the housing market, what a weekend actually looks like. Where another region is the better answer for them, we say that too.

Not yet: an expectation formed from photographs.
06

Timeline

When they can realistically start, tested against notice periods, registration, visa processing and the school year. A start date that only works if nothing goes wrong is not a start date.

Not yet: a timeline with no room in it.
07

Relocation

Whether the practical move is fundable and deliverable — shipping, pets, housing at the other end, the first months before a first salary. Readiness here is what separates an accepted offer from an arrival.

Not yet: no plan for the first eight weeks.

The verification boundary

What we check ourselves, and what we do not claim as ours

Recruiters routinely describe board and immigration checks as though they carried them out. We do not. The distinction matters to an employer relying on it, so here it is in full.

Ethicare verifies

  • Professional references, taken up directly and in writing.
  • Current good standing, confirmed with the clinician’s existing regulator.
  • Employment history and the reasons behind each move.
  • The seven dimensions above, through interview rather than paperwork.
  • Where the registration and visa application actually stand, at any moment, and what is outstanding.
  • That the clinician has read the honest version of the destination, including what is difficult about it.

The boards and the immigration authority verify

  • Qualifications and eligibility to register.
  • Scope of practice and any conditions on registration.
  • Identity.
  • Police and criminal record checks.
  • Health, including the acceptable standard of health that applies to every member of an application.
  • The right to work.
The ‘not yet’ standard

Sometimes the honest answer is that this is not the right year. Registration is not ready, a partner is not ready, or a health question needs resolving before anyone should be making plans.

When that is the case we say so, to the clinician and to the employer, and we accept the lost placement. An agency that cannot afford to say “not yet” is not giving advice — and an employer who is handed that risk pays for it in year one, not us.

Who holds this standard

Two senior clinicians hold this standard

Ethicare’s board includes two practising senior clinicians. They do not work on placements and are not paid for any that we make. Their role is to hold this standard, review it, and tell us when it is not good enough.

Both are shareholders in Ethicare. We state that plainly rather than describe them as independent, because a standard that depends on a reader not knowing something is not a standard. What matters for the advice on this page is the narrower point: neither has any interest in whether one particular clinician takes one particular job.

What the board does
Reviews and approves this standard. Tests it against how clinical practice and regulation actually work. Provides a senior clinical view on unusual cases where we ask for one.
What the board does not do
The board does not screen candidates, review CVs, sit on interviews or approve individual placements. Presenting them as operational would be untrue, and would make everything else on this page harder to believe.
Declared interest
Both board members hold shares in Ethicare Resourcing. Neither is remunerated per placement, and neither has a financial interest in whether any individual clinician accepts any individual role. Ethicare does not recruit into the NHS.
Founder accountability
Sophie Careem, our founder, is accountable for applying this standard day to day. She spent her clinical-services career in NHS service improvement and transformation at the Royal Free London, and relocated to New Zealand herself in 2023. More on why Ethicare exists →
Keeping it current

A standard nobody reviews is a marketing claim

Scheduled review

Annually, by the clinical board. The review date and version are published at the top of this page so you can see when it was last tested.

Triggered review

Any material change to registration requirements, immigration policy or the health thresholds that apply to applicants brings the relevant section forward for review immediately.
