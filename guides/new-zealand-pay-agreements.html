Which agreement sets your pay

Pay in New Zealand’s public health system is set by collective agreement, not negotiated case by case. So the useful question is not what the job pays. It is which agreement covers you, and whether it is current.

Kettle on. A longer read — about 10 min

Why this page exists

Everything here is public. Almost nobody reads it.

Every one of these agreements is published in full — the scales, the steps, the progression rules, the penal rates. You are entitled to read the document that will govern your salary before you accept a job.

What people struggle with is knowing which one. There are more than a dozen in circulation, several professions sit under agreements with names that do not match their job title, and a few of the documents you will find first have expired. So this is the register: what covers each profession, how long it runs, and where to read it yourself.

This page is deliberately a register, not an explainer. How pay actually works. Steps, progression, what sits on top of base salary, tax and take-home, is explained once, in Salary & pay, explained honestly.

General practice is the one gap. There is no single collective agreement for GPs. Pay depends on whether you are salaried by a practice, employed through a primary health organisation, or contracting, and it is negotiated individually. It is the one area where the number really does depend on the conversation.

The thing that surprises people

One scale, six professions

Open the physiotherapy, occupational therapy, dietetics, social work, radiation therapy and anaesthetic technology agreements side by side and you find the same ten steps, to the dollar. That is the allied pay equity settlement: your step sets your salary, not your profession.

Steps 1 to 7 are automatic, one a year on your anniversary date. Step 8 depends on objectives you set with your manager when you reach step 7. Steps 9 and 10 are recognition steps for experienced practitioners who are not in a designated role.

Designated roles sit above this. Team leader, section head and professional advisor positions run on a separate six-grade scale reaching into the $150,000s.

The figures above are the rates payable from the 2026 anniversary in the radiation therapy, occupational therapy, dietetics, social work and anaesthetic technology agreements. Physiotherapy is one increase round behind on the calendar and runs a few hundred dollars lower until its next anniversary; every agreement steps up again during 2027.

Where you land on this ladder is the single biggest decision in your offer, and it is set by how your overseas experience is counted. That is worth getting right rather than accepting.

Imaging, sonography, nursing and midwifery run on their own scales, not this one. Sonography in particular sits well above it, and reporting sonographers higher again, on a separate scale in the same agreement.

Read the documents yourself

Where the agreements are published

We are not the source. Both of these are, they are free, and between them they hold every agreement in the register above. Worth bookmarking rather than taking anyone’s summary on trust, including ours.

Primary source

APEX collective agreements

The union that negotiates for most allied health, scientific and technical professions. Its register is the most current of the two and usually the fastest to publish a newly ratified agreement.

apex.org.nz The employer’s own list

Health New Zealand employment agreements

Every agreement Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora is party to, including nursing and the senior medical agreements. The salary scales live in the appendices at the back of each document.

tewhatuora.govt.nz

The two lists do not always agree. Where a newly settled agreement appears on one and not the other, the more recent document is usually the operative one, but if a figure matters to a decision you are making, ask us or ask the union rather than guessing between them.

The honest limits

What a collective agreement will not tell you

It sets the scale. It does not settle your salary, and reading it will leave four questions open.

Which step you start on

The agreement says the employer will take your years of experience in the occupation into account, and may go higher where recruitment is difficult. That is discretion, and discretion is where an offer is actually won or lost.

What sits on top

Penal rates, on-call, call-back, allowances and overtime are all in the agreement, but what you will actually earn depends on the roster you are given. Two people on the same step can take home materially different amounts.

Whether the role is designated

Designated positions run on their own grades and are graded by the employer against the responsibilities of the post. Whether a job is designated is a question about that specific job, not about your profession.

What it means where you are going to live

Clinical pay is national. The cost of living is not. The same step is a comfortable salary in one region and a stretch in another, and no agreement addresses that.

All four of these are things we work out with you before you decide, against the agreement and in writing. Ask us for the real number for your role. Profession, years of experience, and where you are thinking of going is enough to start.

Common questions

Questions we get about the agreements

Do I have to join the union to be paid on the agreement?

No. Your terms are set by the agreement that covers your role, and you do not have to be a member to be employed on it. Membership is your decision. Some agreements do provide that a new member is placed on the scale from the point they join, so if you are weighing it up, ask the relevant union directly rather than your employer.

My agreement has expired. Has my pay stopped rising?

Your existing terms continue to apply until a replacement is ratified, so nothing lapses and your annual step progression continues. What pauses is the across-the-board increase that a new agreement usually brings. Back pay to an agreed date is common when one settles, but it is negotiated rather than guaranteed.

Why will you not quote me a salary until you have checked?

Because a figure from the wrong column of the right document is still wrong, and it is the sort of wrong that surfaces on someone’s first payslip in a new country. Most of these agreements carry three dated columns and a separate designated scale. We would rather take a day and give you the number you will actually be paid.

Does my overseas experience count towards my step?

Experience in the occupation is what the agreements direct the employer to take into account, and that is not limited to New Zealand. How much of it is recognised in practice varies, and it is one of the few negotiable parts of a clinical offer. Come to that conversation with your years set out clearly and evidenced.

Is the private sector paid on these agreements?

Some independent providers hold their own collective agreements, and several appear in the register above. Many do not, in which case pay is individually negotiated. If you are considering a private role, ask at the outset whether a collective agreement applies. The answer changes what there is to discuss.
