Registering with the Physiotherapy Board

For internationally qualified physiotherapists: which of the three pathways is yours, what the Practice Thresholds ask of you, the two cultural competency courses you can start today — and the checklist built into the page so nothing you tick goes out of date.

Section 01

Understanding the Board

The Physiotherapy Board of New Zealand registers and oversees physiotherapists under the Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act. Its purpose is to protect the public by making sure everyone practising as a physiotherapist is qualified, competent and fit to do so. The title and practice of physiotherapy are legally protected: you cannot practise, or call yourself a physiotherapist, without registration and a current Annual Practising Certificate.

The Practice Thresholds

What sets physiotherapy apart is what you are assessed against. Every application is measured against the Physiotherapy Practice Thresholds in Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand — a shared set of competencies describing what a physiotherapist must be able to do, safely and effectively, to register and practise. Get to know them early; Section 04 covers them in detail.

Registration, the APC and your HPI number

Registration confirms you have met the standard and does not expire. The Annual Practising Certificate (APC) is what permits you to work, renewed each year; the physiotherapy APC year runs to 31 March and is managed through the Board’s MyPBNZ portal. Once registered you are added to the public Register of Physiotherapists and the Ministry of Health issues your Health Practitioner Index (HPI) number. Registration is an ongoing commitment: competence, ethical practice and annual CPD the Board can audit.

Section 02

Scopes of practice

Three scopes exist. For anyone applying from overseas, one matters to begin with: every physiotherapist must first be registered in the General Scope.

Your application is for this

General Scope

Where every physiotherapist begins. The full breadth of autonomous practice — assessment, diagnosis and treatment — across musculoskeletal, neurological, cardiorespiratory and other areas.

Later

Advanced Practice Physiotherapist

For advanced skills in areas such as musculoskeletal, neurology or cardiorespiratory practice. Not an entry point: it needs unrestricted General Scope registration, a current APC, a relevant postgraduate qualification and experience including time practising in New Zealand.

Later

Specialist Scope

The most advanced scope, for physiotherapists recognised as specialists in their field. Built on General Scope registration and New Zealand practice — something to progress toward, not a route in.

What this means for you.

Apply for the General Scope. The other two are progression options once you are registered, hold an APC and have built experience here. Nothing to think about yet — but reassuring that the career pathway exists.

Section 03

Which registration pathway is yours

The single most important thing to get right at the start. PBNZ has three distinct pathways, and which one you use depends entirely on where you trained and where you are registered.

Pathway 1

International Express Pathway

Who: physiotherapists connected to the UK, Ireland, Canada or South Africa — either unrestricted registration there (HCPC, CORU, HPCSA or a Canadian physiotherapy regulator) or a physiotherapy qualification from one of them recognised as pre-registration by that regulator.

Why it exists: the Board treats those four jurisdictions as closely aligned with New Zealand, so the Express Pathway removes duplication of what you have already proven.

Pathway 2

Trans-Tasman Mutual Recognition

Who: physiotherapists registered with Ahpra in Australia.

Why it exists: under the Trans-Tasman arrangement, registration in one country is recognised in the other. You apply under TTMR, not as an overseas-trained applicant.

Pathway 3

International General Pathway

Who: everyone else — no recognised qualification or unrestricted registration in the UK, Ireland, Canada, South Africa or Australia.

Why it exists: it assesses your qualification and competence directly against the Practice Thresholds, with a structured way to demonstrate any competency your curriculum does not already evidence (Section 04).

  1. 1

    Registered with Ahpra in Australia?

    Trans-Tasman Mutual Recognition.

  2. 2

    Qualified or registered in the UK, Ireland, Canada or South Africa?

    International Express Pathway.

  3. 3

    Anywhere else?

    International General Pathway.

The wrong pathway costs time.

Applying under General when you qualify for Express, or the reverse, is one of the most common avoidable delays. If you are unsure where you fall, check before you start.

Section 04

The Practice Thresholds and how assessment works

The Practice Thresholds describe the threshold competence required for initial registration and continuing practice — what you must be able to do, safely and effectively, across assessment, clinical reasoning, intervention, communication, professional behaviour and safety. They are shared across Australia and New Zealand, which is why Australian-registered physiotherapists move so smoothly between the two systems.

On the General Pathway, assessment runs through five phases. Knowing them in advance removes most of the anxiety, because you can see exactly where you are at any point.

  1. 1

    Document check

    The Board reviews your application for completeness. If anything is missing or eligibility is in question, you are emailed before it proceeds.

  2. 2

    Qualification assessment

    An assessor reviews how well the curriculum of your primary physiotherapy degree aligns with each key competency. Two outcomes: every competency met, and the application goes straight forward; or gaps, and it moves to Phase 3. Neither is unusual — curricula differ between countries.

  3. 3

    Request for supporting evidence — only if needed

    You are told exactly which competencies to demonstrate and have three months to provide evidence. Competencies already shown do not need proving again.

  4. 4

    Assessment of supporting evidence

    If it meets the criteria you proceed; if not, you can submit more based on the assessor’s feedback.

  5. 5

    Registration

    Applications that meet the requirement go to the Registrar for approval, and you receive email confirmation.

Demonstrating competencies, if you reach Phase 3

The Board’s request sets out precisely which competencies need evidencing, and it publishes a Guidance on Providing Supporting Evidence document with templates — a Reflections template and a Validation of Work History form. Read them; they help you choose high-quality evidence. Give specific examples and detail from your training or work, clearly named and well organised.

Read the Thresholds before you apply.

Map your experience to each competency. If you can already see where your curriculum might be light, start gathering specific, detailed examples now — the evidence phase, if it comes, is then far quicker.

Section 05

Eligibility requirements

Far easier to satisfy once you understand why each one exists — so that is how they are explained here.

A recognised physiotherapy qualification
Why: your qualification is the foundation of safe practice. The Board needs confidence your training covered the Practice Thresholds — which is why course content can matter as much as the certificate, particularly on the General Pathway.
Registration and good standing
Why: if you are, or have been, registered as a physiotherapist anywhere, the Board needs to know you are in good standing with no unresolved concerns. This underpins the Express and TTMR pathways and supports the General Pathway too.
Recency of practice
Why: skills fade when unused. If you have had a significant break, flag it early — depending on the gap, a Return to Practice process may apply.
English language
Why: patient safety. Taking histories, explaining treatment, communicating precisely with patients and colleagues. Section 08.
Fitness for registration
Why: your physical and mental fitness to practise safely, and your professional conduct history. Full, honest disclosure — even of matters you think minor or resolved — is always right. Non-disclosure is treated far more seriously than the underlying matter usually is.
Cultural competency
Why: practising safely in New Zealand means understanding its cultural context, including Te Tiriti o Waitangi and Māori health. PBNZ requires evidence of cultural competency training — specific enough to have its own section, 07.
Read every requirement as a question.

The Board is asking whether you are safe to practise. Frame each document as the answer and your application becomes far more persuasive.

Section 06

Preparing your application

A strong application is rarely about doing anything clever. It is clean, complete, correctly certified documents, presented well.

The Board is forming a picture of you as a professional, and a folder of crooked, half-legible photocopies undermines it even when every document is genuine. Clean colour scans, pages straight and square, nothing cut off, consistent file names, certification done properly the first time.

Identity

Why
Confirms who you are; underpins your visa and right to work.
Accepted
A passport or birth certificate, plus a name-change document (marriage certificate, deed poll) if your name differs across documents.
Tip
Scan flat, in colour, with all edges visible.

Your qualification documents

Why
Your primary physiotherapy qualification is the basis of registration; on the General Pathway these are what the curriculum assessment works from.
Required
General Pathway: your degree, academic transcript, curriculum and a clinical hours profile — all four. Express Pathway: the degree or transcript from a UK, Ireland, Canada or South Africa institution, or proof of registration with HCPC, CORU, HPCSA or a Canadian regulator.
Mistake
Sending only the degree and transcript. The curriculum and clinical hours profile are required and frequently forgotten.

Certificates of Good Standing

Why
Confirm good standing with every physiotherapy regulator you have registered with.
Timing
Each must be less than three months old on the date you submit. Requesting too early is a frequent, avoidable reason to start again.
Tip
Line them up to be fresh at submission, not months before.

Police or criminal history certificates

Why
The Board must protect patients, so it checks your background.
Required
From any country you have lived in for more than twelve months in the last ten years since age eighteen — and, like Good Standing, each less than three months old at submission.
Tip
They can take time to obtain, so balance starting early against the three-month rule.

CV — on the Board’s template

Why
Evidences your experience and recency of practice.
Mistake
Submitting your own CV format. The Board requires its own downloadable template and will send yours back.
Tip
Complete it fully, accounting for every period including gaps. Build your CV helps you assemble the content before you transfer it.

Certification and translations

Why
Certification confirms a true copy of the original.
The rule
Each required document is either certified, or sent directly to the Board by the issuing authority. Translations of any non-English document must also be provided.
Tip
Confirm who may certify and the wording before you spend money. Getting it wrong means doing it twice.

Section 07

Cultural competency requirements

A requirement that surprises many international applicants — and the one you can begin today, before you have gathered a single other document.

PBNZ requires evidence of cultural competency training as part of registration, typically certificates from two recognised courses. They cover cultural safety, Te Tiriti o Waitangi and its place in healthcare, and working effectively with Māori and New Zealand’s diverse communities. Safe physiotherapy here is not only clinical skill; Te Tiriti is foundational to the health system and cultural safety is treated as a core part of competent practice, not an optional extra.

Mauri Ora Associates

Foundations in Cultural Competency

Counts as one of your two.

Mauri Ora Associates

Foundations Course on Healthcare and the Treaty of Waitangi

Counts as one of your two.

Ministry of Health, via Ko Awatea LEARN

Ngā Paerewa Te Tiriti eLearning, Modules One and Two

You will need to request an account and provide your international registration number. Both modules together count as one course.

An easy mistake.

Ngā Paerewa Modules One and Two combined count as one of the two courses you need. Completing Ngā Paerewa alone is not enough: pair it with a Mauri Ora course, or complete both Mauri Ora courses. Plan for two distinct courses.

Start these first.

They are online and entirely within your control, so they are the easiest thing to finish ahead of time — and a genuine introduction to the healthcare culture you are joining, not just a box to tick.

Section 08

English language requirements

This exists for one reason: patient safety. The Board needs objective assurance of your ability.

Several routes

Check which applies before assuming you need a test

Depending on your background: evidence that English was your first language or the language of your education, or a recognised test. The Board’s English language policy sets out exactly which routes apply.

Test

IELTS (Academic)

Minimum scores in each component. A strong overall score does not help if one component falls short — prepare evenly.

Test

OET

The healthcare-specific test many clinicians prefer because its tasks are set in medical contexts. Minimum grades in each component.

Results expire.

Typically around two years from the test date. Sitting the test early and then taking many months to assemble the rest is a frequent reason for paying to sit it again. Time it to remain valid through to assessment. Sit a timed practice test first, find your weakest component, and book early.

Section 09

Completing and submitting your application

  1. 1

    Confirm your pathway

    Express, TTMR or General — it determines what you submit. Section 03.

  2. 2

    Complete your cultural competency courses

    Ideally already done; they are within your control.

  3. 3

    Create your application on MyPBNZ

    Work through each section methodically and answer the fitness-for-registration questions accurately.

  4. 4

    Upload your documents

    Clean colour scans, certified where required, CV on the Board’s template, clearly named.

  5. 5

    Pay the fee and submit

    Review everything one final time. Keep your payment confirmation.

  6. 6

    Respond to any requests

    On the General Pathway you may be asked for competency evidence — reply promptly and completely.

Understanding the fees

Depending on your pathway and circumstances the Board’s charges include the registration or application fee and, once registered, your Annual Practising Certificate. On top sit third-party costs: police certificates, the cultural competency courses, any English test, and document certification or translation.

Amounts are deliberately not printed here. The Board publishes its prescribed fees on its website and revises them; confirm the current figures before you budget or pay. Where an Ethicare package applies to your placement, APC costs are reimbursed — ask us what your package includes before you rely on it.

Section 10

After you submit, and your APC

Submitting is a milestone, not the finish line.

1

Assessment

Against the Practice Thresholds. Generally quicker on the Express and TTMR pathways; on the General Pathway it may include the evidence phase in Section 04.

2

Requests for further information

Routine, not a red flag. A fast, complete response is the biggest thing within your control.

3

Registration decision

Applications that meet the requirements go to the Registrar. Once approved you are registered in the General Scope, added to the public Register, and issued your HPI number.

4

Apply for your APC

Registration alone does not let you work. Apply for your Annual Practising Certificate through MyPBNZ, confirming when you last practised. The APC year runs to 31 March.

5

Employment and relocation

With registration, or clear registrability, you are in a strong position to secure a role. This is where planning the move takes over from registration.

Where people get stuck

Almost every avoidable delay traces back to one of these

The wrong pathway

General when you qualify for Express, or the reverse. Confirm your pathway first.

CV not on the Board template

Your own format is sent back.

Cultural competency left late

Within your control — leaving it to the end needlessly delays everything.

Expired Good Standing

Requested too early, over three months old at submission, reissued.

Poor-quality scans

Cropped, blurry or greyscale. If the assessor cannot read it, it does not count.

Incorrect certification

Wrong person or wrong wording — redone from scratch.

Thin competency evidence

On the General Pathway, vague evidence without specific examples mapped to each competency.

The fix for all of these

Confirm your pathway, prepare everything together, and have it read by someone else before you submit. Most of these are invisible to the person making them.

Section 12

The questions we are asked most

Which pathway should I use?

Ahpra-registered in Australia: Trans-Tasman Mutual Recognition. Trained or registered in the UK, Ireland, Canada or South Africa: the International Express Pathway. Everyone else: the International General Pathway. When in doubt, check before you start.

Can I apply for registration before finding a job?

Yes — and in most cases you should. Registration usually comes before employment, so starting early puts you in a far stronger position when roles arise.

What scope will I be registered in?

The General Scope, where every physiotherapist begins. Advanced Practice and Specialist are progression options once you are registered, hold an APC and have New Zealand experience.

What are the Practice Thresholds?

The shared Australia–New Zealand set of competencies your application is assessed against: what a physiotherapist must be able to do to register and practise. Read them before you apply.

Do I really need the cultural competency courses?

Yes. Evidence of cultural competency training, typically two courses, is part of registration. Online and within your control — one of the easiest things to complete early.

What if my curriculum does not cover every competency?

On the General Pathway the assessment runs in phases. If the curriculum review finds gaps, you are told the specific competencies to demonstrate and have around three months to provide detailed evidence from your training or work. Competencies already shown do not need proving again, and the Board provides guidance and templates.

Can I work while my application is being assessed?

You cannot practise until you are registered and hold a current APC. Plan your move around that fact rather than assuming you can begin and sort registration out afterwards.

Can Ethicare review my application?

Yes. We review documents and competency evidence before submission against what the Board looks for and flag likely issues while they are easy to fix. The registration decision always rests with the Board.
