Welcome to the NT

A warm welcome, you’re in good hands

Deciding to move your life and career to the other side of the world is a big step, and an exciting one. Whether the Northern Territory is already home to people you know or a brand-new chapter, I want you to feel supported from the very first conversation.

The Northern Territory is like nowhere else in Australia: the tropical Top End around Darwin, the red-centre outback around Alice Springs, and the world’s oldest living cultures at the heart of it all. It offers extraordinary experiences, fast career progression and some of the most generous packages in the country.

This guide walks you through healthcare, visas and registration, finances, housing, schooling and cost of living across the state, then breaks down the main places you might live so you can picture daily life in each. Think of it as the conversation we’d have over coffee, in your pocket, whenever you need it.

Welcome to the Northern Territory. We’re so glad you’re here, and we’ll be alongside you every step of the way.

Ethicare insight

Candidates often picture Australia as one job market. It isn’t. The Territory registers nationally through Ahpra but hires locally through NT Health, and demand is high across a huge, remote catchment, which is exactly why the packages and the experience can be exceptional.

Overview · Culture

The world’s oldest cultures

The Larrakia people are the traditional custodians of the Darwin region, known as Garramilla, and the Arrernte people of Mparntwe (Alice Springs); across the Territory, First Nations peoples make up around a quarter of the population and sustain some of the oldest living cultures on earth.

The Territory is Australia’s most Aboriginal region, and its art, languages and communities are central to daily life, from the galleries of Darwin to the world-famous art centres of the centre and Arnhem Land. Darwin is also strikingly multicultural and Asian-facing, with a laid-back, open-air culture built around markets, festivals and the harbour.

Sophie’s tip

Learn the local names early, Garramilla (Darwin) and Mparntwe (Alice Springs), and take time to understand the Country you’re on. It’s genuinely appreciated and central to life here.

Overview · Transport

Getting around

The Territory is huge, and a car is essential for life beyond town. Darwin and Alice Springs have local buses, but distances between towns are immense, this is genuine outback.

Driving

Australians drive on the left. A reliable car is essential; distances are vast, fuel up often and carry water on long drives.

Buses

Darwin and Alice Springs have local bus networks with tap-and-ride cards; services are limited compared with the big cities.

Remote travel

Beyond the highways, roads can be unsealed and seasonal, always check conditions and tell someone your plans.

Flying

Darwin and Alice Springs airports link to the capitals and to remote communities, flying is often the practical way to cover the distances.

Sophie’s tip

You can usually drive on a valid overseas licence for a period after arriving, but you’ll need to transfer to an NT licence once you’re a resident. Get a well-maintained car, carry water, and check road and weather conditions before any long or remote drive.

Overview · Climate

A year in the Top End & Centre climate

The Territory has two very different climates: the tropical Top End (Darwin) runs on a wet and a dry season rather than four, while the centre (Alice Springs) is desert, hot days and cold nights. The seasons are flipped from the Northern Hemisphere.

In the Top End, the Dry is warm, sunny and glorious; the humid Build-up and the Wet bring monsoon storms and spectacular skies from about November to April. The centre is arid, with hot summers, warm winter days and genuinely cold desert nights, pack for both.

Ethicare insight

Newcomers to the Top End underestimate the Wet, the heat, humidity and monsoon storms are intense, and cyclone season runs roughly November to April, so learn the emergency plans early. In the centre, the shock is the opposite: freezing desert nights in winter. Either way, the UV is extreme year-round, sun protection is essential.

Sophie’s View

Why I like the Northern Territory

One of the things I love most about the Territory is what it offers a clinician early in a career: real responsibility, extraordinary breadth of practice, and a genuinely welcoming, multicultural community. Whether you’re drawn to Darwin’s tropical harbour life or the red-centre magic of Alice Springs, few places grow you faster or reward you more generously. The packages here, remote allowances, subsidised housing, relocation support, are unlike anywhere else in the country.

Having spent many years in the NHS, I know how easily work can take over. What stands out to me about the Territory is the sense of adventure, a sunset market at Mindil, Uluru at dawn, Kakadu in the Dry, and a community that pulls together. If I could give one piece of advice, it would be to ask exactly what your package includes, especially housing, and to go in open to the experience. It changes people, in the best way.

Section two · Healthcare

Healthcare in the NT

The Northern Territory runs a public health system built for distance, universal Medicare cover, a small number of hospitals and a vast remote-health network, centred on the Royal Darwin Hospital and Alice Springs Hospital.

Go deeperUnderstanding healthcare in Australia→

Royal Darwin (RDH)

The Territory’s only tertiary referral hospital, in Tiwi, home to the National Critical Care and Trauma Response Centre and the base for Top End services.

Alice Springs Hospital

The referral hospital for the whole of Central Australia, offering exceptionally broad, hands-on practice across a vast region.

Palmerston & Katherine

Palmerston Regional Hospital serves Darwin’s growing south; Katherine Hospital anchors a huge region three hours south.

Remote health & Gove

Gove District Hospital serves east Arnhem Land, backed by a vast network of remote clinics across the Territory.

Good to know

Eligibility for Medicare usually begins once you hold an appropriate visa. Reciprocal Health Care Agreements give some countries limited Medicare access. St John Ambulance NT provides ambulance services; check whether cover is included in your package or add it to your private health insurance.

Registration · Ahpra

Professional registration

Most healthcare professions are regulated nationally through Ahpra, the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency, and its profession-specific boards.

One national register

Once registered with Ahpra you can work anywhere in Australia, including all of the NT, registration is national, not state-by-state.

Qualification assessment

Overseas qualifications are assessed against Australian standards; some roles require bridging programs or exams.

English language

You’ll usually need to evidence English proficiency through an approved test or a recognised pathway.

Start early

Registration takes time, begin gathering documents and references well before you plan to travel.

Ethicare insight

For a Territory role, the one thing that delays a start is paperwork left too late. Certified qualifications, references and ID take time to assemble, so begin early, remote roles especially reward being ready to go.

Section four · Banking & Finance

Setting up your finances

Getting your finances in order early makes day-to-day life much easier. Most of this can be started before you arrive.

Opening a bank account

The major banks are CommBank, Westpac, NAB and ANZ, plus Territory-focused options. Many let you open online before you arrive, then verify ID in branch with your passport.

Tax File Number (TFN)

Every worker needs a TFN from the ATO. Apply online once you arrive and give the number to payroll so you’re taxed correctly. Without it, you’ll be taxed at the highest rate.

Sophie’s tip

Open your bank account before you fly where you can, having an account ready makes your payroll setup and first rent payment far smoother in those hectic first weeks.

The money set-up, in order

1Activate your bank account, verify ID in branch, order a debit card and online banking.
2Apply for your TFN, online via the ATO, then give it to payroll immediately.
3Choose your super fund, or accept the employer default, then review later.
4Set up salary packaging, ask payroll or your provider on day one if you’re in public health.
5Register for Medicare, if eligible under your visa or a reciprocal agreement.
6Get a local SIM & number, many banking and government steps need an Australian mobile.
Sophie’s tip

The salary is only half the picture, and here the packages often include the good stuff: remote allowances, subsidised housing and relocation support are common, so ask what’s included. Still budget for the bridge, temporary accommodation, a bond, and a reliable car (essential here), and planning for it removes almost all of the early stress.

Sophie Careem · Founder & Director

Section seven · Housing & Areas

Finding a place to live

From Darwin’s tropical suburbs to remote-town housing, where you live in the Territory is often tied to your job, many roles come with subsidised or provided accommodation, so ask first.

Many Territory health roles, especially remote ones, come with subsidised or provided housing, so check what your package includes before you rent. On the open market you’ll apply with ID, references and proof of income, then pay a bond (typically four weeks’ rent). The main sites are realestate.com.au (largest) and domain.com.au.

Choosing your area, the order that saves the most stress

1Fix your work site first. Everything flows from your commute, decide it before you fall for a suburb.
2Check school catchments if you have children. Local intake is guaranteed; out-of-area isn’t.
3Shortlist two or three areas and rent short-term in one before committing.
4Understand the climate. In the Top End, look for airflow, screens and air-conditioning.

Utilities & getting set up

Power & water

Jacana Energy and the Power and Water Corporation supply most of the Territory. Set up on move-in.

Water

Power and Water Corporation; usage matters in the Dry.

Internet

NBN and wireless via the major providers; coverage thins in remote areas.

Setting up home

Kmart, IKEA and Bunnings furnish a first home fast; Marketplace for second-hand.

Sophie’s tip

Ask your employer about housing before anything else, subsidised or provided accommodation is common in the Territory, especially outside Darwin, and it can transform the maths of a move.

Housing & family · Choosing where to live

Choosing where to live

For many healthcare professionals, the job is only half the decision. The other half is where your family will settle well. Schools, commute, housing cost and lifestyle are best weighed together. Here’s a practical way to think it through.

Schools often come first

Access to a government school is usually tied to where you live. Popular schools can be pinned to an exact address, catchments can change street by street, and out-of-area places aren’t guaranteed. If you have children, check the catchment before you sign a long-term lease. Independent, Catholic and faith schools offer more flexibility, but usually charge fees.

Your commute still matters

The Territory is car country, and outside Darwin distances are vast, so a reliable vehicle is essential. But the single biggest factor here is your package: many roles, especially remote ones, come with subsidised or provided housing, so confirm that first. Then think about climate (airflow and air-con in the Top End) and how close you are to your hospital.

The order that usually works best

A simple sequence that keeps the big decisions in the right order.

  1. 1Confirm your likely work site.
  2. 2Shortlist suitable school options if you have children.
  3. 3Check the catchment maps for those schools.
  4. 4Compare commute times to work at shift hours.
  5. 5Review rental availability and cost.
  6. 6Rent short-term first where you can.
  7. 7Choose a longer-term suburb once you know the area.

Section eight · Shopping & Food

Groceries, food & community

The essentials in one place, the big chains, the budget picks, and the Territory’s genuinely multicultural, Asian-influenced food culture, which is a real highlight.

The big chains

Woolworths & Coles for full-range everywhere; ALDI for the cheapest weekly shop; IGA and independents for neighbourhood top-ups, vital in regional towns.

Flavours of home

Darwin’s markets (Mindil, Parap, Rapid Creek) are the heart of its Asian and multicultural food; the major supermarkets carry a good halal and world range; and specialty grocers cover South-East Asian, Indian and other staples.

Sophie’s tip

Bring your own bags, single-use plastic bags are banned in the Territory, and in the heat a cooler bag in the car is genuinely useful for the drive home.

Good to know

Darwin’s Asian and multicultural food culture is a real highlight, halal outlets and South-East Asian, Indian and Chinese groceries are easy to find, and the markets are superb. Dedicated kosher supplies are very limited and usually ordered in. In remote towns, ranges are basic and prices higher, so many people buy non-perishables in bulk on city trips.

Faith & community · Places of worship

Religion & places of worship

Australia has no state religion and law protects freedom of belief. The Territory is strikingly diverse for its size: alongside a large First Nations population with their own spiritual traditions, Christianity is the largest faith, and Islam, Buddhism, Hinduism and others are established in Darwin and the towns.

Most communities list service and prayer times online and warmly welcome newcomers, often one of the quickest ways to build a support network after you arrive.

The main faiths & where to find them

Christianity

Catholic, Anglican, Uniting and many other churches across Darwin and the towns, including strong Aboriginal Christian communities.

Islam

The Darwin Mosque and community centres serve a well-established Muslim community, reflecting the Territory’s Asian and African links.

Buddhism

Temples and centres in Darwin reflect the city’s large South-East Asian communities.

Hinduism

A growing community with temple and cultural activities in Darwin.

First Nations traditions

Aboriginal spiritual and cultural life is central across the Territory, grounded in connection to Country.

Community networks

Multicultural and migrant services in Darwin help newcomers find community and places of worship.

If regular worship matters to you, factor it into where you settle. Communities and places of worship are concentrated in Darwin and, to a lesser extent, Alice Springs. In remote postings they may be a long way off, worth confirming as part of your decision.

Faith & community · Diversity & inclusion

Diversity, inclusion & belonging

The Northern Territory is one of the most diverse places in Australia, and the law protects everyone’s right to be treated equally. Whoever you are, and whoever your family is, you’re welcome here.

Go deeperReligion, community & belonging in Australia→

A multicultural home

The Northern Territory is one of the most diverse places in Australia, with a very high First Nations population and long-established Asian, Greek and other communities, especially in Darwin. Multicultural and migrant services help newcomers connect, and the community is famously welcoming.

Your rights in law

The Northern Territory Anti-Discrimination Act 1992 and federal law protect people from discrimination on the basis of race, religion, age, sex, disability, sexuality and gender identity. The NT Anti-Discrimination Commission handles complaints.

LGBTQ+ inclusion

Same-sex marriage has been legal Australia-wide since 2017, and sexuality and gender identity are protected attributes. Darwin Pride brings the community together in the Dry, and QLife (1800 184 527) offers free, anonymous peer support across the Territory.

Inclusive workplaces & care

A growing number of health services hold Rainbow Tick accreditation or run active inclusion networks. Interpreters are free through TIS National (131 450), and the NDIS supports people with disability.

Ethicare insight

NT Health and most employers have active diversity and inclusion commitments, and many run staff networks for culturally diverse, First Nations and LGBTQ+ workers. If belonging at work matters to you, tell us, we can factor a team’s culture and inclusion record into where we suggest you land.

Section ten · Education & Family

Schooling & family life

The Northern Territory offers a full range of early childhood education, schools and universities, run by the Department of Education and Training. The school year runs late January to mid-December across four terms, a flip from the Northern Hemisphere calendar worth planning around.

Go deeperEducation & schools in Australia→

Early childhood

Long daycare, kindergarten and family day care for ages 0–5, with subsidies.

Primary

The first years of school through to the end of primary, across government, Catholic and independent schools.

Secondary

The senior years, ending with the NT Certificate of Education and Training (NTCET), leading to university or training.

Tertiary

Charles Darwin University (CDU), with campuses in Darwin, Palmerston and Alice Springs, plus vocational training.

Public school fees for visa-holder families

The Territory is the simplest of all: dependants of 482 visa holders attend government schools with no tuition fees.

Ethicare insight

The Territory is the most generous jurisdiction on school fees: dependants of 482 visa holders attend government schools with no tuition fees at all, exactly like local families. You may still meet standard voluntary contributions and materials or excursion charges, and non-government schools set their own fees, but there’s no international-student tuition to budget for. It’s one more way the Territory makes a family move easier.

School catchments come first

For Northern Territory government schools, your home address usually determines your local-intake (catchment) school, children inside the zone are generally guaranteed a place, while out-of-area places depend on capacity, and popular schools can be oversubscribed. Boundaries can run street-by-street, so always check the exact address before you sign.

For families, the school decision usually leads the housing decision here, deciding it first prevents an expensive mid-year move.

Education & Family · Partners & work

Employment for your partner

A move only works if it works for the whole family. The good news: on most skilled and employer-sponsored visas your partner is included with full work rights, free to take any job, full or part-time, with any employer.

Work rights come as standard

Partners added to a 482, 186, or skilled 189/190/491 visa generally have unrestricted work rights, no separate employer sponsorship needed. Always confirm the exact conditions on your grant letter.

A broad job market

The Territory has a tight labour market and genuine demand, especially in healthcare, education, government, construction, defence, and mining and resources, with real opportunities for partners in Darwin, Palmerston and Alice Springs.

Helping your partner land work

1Reformat the CV to Australian style, concise, achievement-led, no photo or date of birth. Set up a strong LinkedIn with an Australian location.
2Search the main boards, SEEK, Indeed and Jora, plus the NT Government jobs site and health-service careers pages for public-sector roles.
3Check if the profession is regulated, teaching, nursing, trades and others need registration or a skills assessment. Start it before you arrive.
4Register with recruitment agencies, many Territory roles are filled through agencies, who can also advise on local expectations and pay.
Ethicare insight

In the Territory, a partner settling well matters enormously to whether a move lasts, so we plan for it from the outset. Tell us early what your partner does; if they’re also in healthcare, we can often help them directly in Darwin or Alice Springs.
