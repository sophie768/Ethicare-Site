# NSW photography — audit

## Added 23 Aug 2026

- `nsw-sydney-harbour-bridge-luna-park.jpg` — the Harbour Bridge from the north shore, Luna Park's
  face and ferris wheel below it, the Opera House sails visible through the span, moored yachts in
  Lavender Bay. Sydney, confirmed — about as unambiguous as an Australian photograph gets.
- `nsw-newcastle-nobbys-beach-summer.jpg` — a busy Newcastle beach on a hot day looking to Nobbys
  Head and its lighthouse. Newcastle, confirmed by the headland.
- `nsw-newcastle-ocean-baths-aerial.jpg` — Newcastle from the air: the Ocean Baths and Canoe Pool,
  Newcastle Beach, the CBD and the harbour beyond. Newcastle, confirmed.

- `nsw-sydney-circular-quay-station.jpg` — the Harbour Bridge framed from the platform at Circular
  Quay station: the station canopy in silhouette, the bridge and the north shore beyond the glass,
  two people with prams stopped at the barrier. **Sydney, confirmed twice over** — the platform sign
  in frame reads "Circular Quay", and the bridge and Kirribilli behind it corroborate it.

  The most useful Sydney file we hold, and not for the bridge. It is a **commute** photograph: an
  ordinary weekday moment, on the way somewhere, with the landmark incidental behind the glass. Every
  other Sydney image in the library is the postcard — bridge, Opera House, Bondi. This one answers
  "what does getting to work actually look like", which is the question a relocating clinician
  actually has. Pair it with `brisbane-cbd-street-buses-dusk.jpg`; both are transport-chapter shots
  rather than tourism shots.

- `nsw-sydney-qvb-interior.jpg` — the Queen Victoria Building from an upper gallery: the Royal
  Clock hanging under the glass barrel roof, tiled floor, cast-iron balustrades, shoppers on three
  levels. **Sydney, confirmed** — the QVB interior and its clock are unmistakable. Tall portrait
  crop (1500×2249), so it suits a narrow column or a phone, not a banner.

  Use with some care. It is a genuine heritage landmark and good evidence that the city has grand
  Victorian architecture, but it is also a **luxury shopping centre**, and the frame carries a dozen
  legible brand fascias. That makes it weak for a relocation guide, where the useful message is
  everyday life rather than retail, and it should not be used anywhere the surrounding copy is about
  cost of living or affordability — the juxtaposition would read badly. Best fit is a heritage or
  city-architecture note, not a lifestyle or money section.

- `nsw-sydney-qvb-facade.jpg` — the Queen Victoria Building from Market Street: the full sandstone
  Romanesque frontage, stained glass under the awnings, people walking past on the footpath.
  **Sydney, confirmed** — the building is named in carved stone above the entrance.

  The better of the two QVB files for general use, and it should be preferred over
  `nsw-sydney-qvb-interior.jpg`. Landscape rather than a tall portrait, so it actually fits a
  banner; it is architecture seen from the street rather than a retail hall; and the frame carries
  far fewer legible brand names — the two that show are "SALE" posters, which is a different problem
  but a smaller one. The same caution still applies: keep it away from cost-of-living copy, and use
  it for heritage or city-architecture, not lifestyle.

Newcastle's nine guide chapters previously shared two images between them; two Newcastle files added
23 Aug are still unplaced — worth using.

- `nsw-bondi-beach-crowded-summer.jpg` — the full Bondi crescent on a hot day from the south end,
  the North Bondi headland and Ben Buckler beyond, the sand densely occupied. Sydney, confirmed.

  **This is the third Bondi file** (with `nsw-bondi-beach-sydney-australia-3-2627055435.jpg` and
  `nsw-icebergs-pool-bondi-beach-sydney-2694538809.jpg`), which makes Bondi the single most
  photographed place in the library. Kept anyway, for one honest reason the other two do not carry:
  it shows how **busy** it is. Everywhere else in the Australian set the coast looks empty, and a
  clinician weighing Sydney against a regional posting should see that the famous beach is shared
  with several thousand people. Use it where the point is the crowd, not the water.

  If a fourth Bondi arrives, decline it. None of the eleven outstanding placeholders is a beach.
