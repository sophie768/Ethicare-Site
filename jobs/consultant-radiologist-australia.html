Why AustraliaA deep market, real sub-speciality scope, and a country built for living

Consultant radiologists are in strong, sustained demand across Australia — in public hospital services and one of the world's largest private radiology markets, metro and regional. The case mix is broad, sub-speciality work is well supported, and the lifestyle around the role is, for many, the real draw.

  • Strong demand — public and a very large private market
  • Genuine sub-speciality scope and modern RIS/PACS
  • A specialist pathway built for internationally trained consultants
"Exceptional earning potential and an exceptional lifestyle, together."

Specialists move here from across the world — the UK, South Africa, Ireland, the UAE and beyond — and many tell us the move gives back real time without compromising the work.

Getting registeredSpecialist registration overview

Reviewed July 2026 · checked against the Medical Board of Australia’s current published guidance

You hold specialist registration with the Medical Board of Australia, through Ahpra. For an internationally trained specialist that runs through the specialist pathway: RANZCR assesses how comparable your training is to an Australian-trained radiologist, and the Board makes the registration decision on that advice.

The RANZCR assessment finds you substantially comparable, partially comparable or not comparable — which shapes whether you start in a supervised role on provisional (or limited) registration before full specialist recognition. If you hold the Canadian Royal College certificate in diagnostic radiology, the Expedited Specialist pathway applies instead. You'll typically be assembling:

  • Your specialist medical qualifications and training record
  • Detailed logbook / case evidence and a structured CV
  • Registration history and a current Certificate of Good Standing
  • English-language evidence, where the standard requires it

After your comparability outcome

What the RANZCR finding means for how you begin practising:

Substantially comparable

The most direct route — leads to specialist registration, usually after a short period of supervised practice (peer review) before full specialist recognition.

Partially comparable

A longer period of supervised practice and/or some top-up training or exams, after which you progress to full specialist recognition.

Not comparable

The specialist pathway isn't the right fit on the evidence provided — we'll talk through the realistic alternatives with you.

Period of supervised practice

Where required, you practise under a College-approved supervisor while completing requirements — usually easy to arrange in a market this short of radiologists.

Medical Board specialist registration

The Medical Board of Australia, through Ahpra, makes the registration decision and grants specialist registration once requirements are met.

Compared · Australia and New ZealandOne college, two quite different working lives

For radiologists both countries are strong options: sophisticated imaging services, real demand for experienced specialists, and a shared training tradition through RANZCR. The experience of working in each is quite different.

Australia has a much larger radiology market, significant private-sector opportunity and potentially very high earning potential. New Zealand has a smaller, more connected system, interesting public and private roles, and suits radiologists looking for a long-term change rather than a different job. Your subspecialty, and how you feel about public against private practice, matter more than the country name.

Registration is a separate process in each country — never assume one follows from the other. The full comparison sits in Australia or New Zealand?, and the money side in Where will your salary go further?

Common questions

Frequently asked

How does specialist registration work?

You hold specialist registration with the Medical Board of Australia, through Ahpra. For internationally trained radiologists that runs via the specialist pathway, where RANZCR assesses how comparable your training and experience are to an Australian-trained radiologist, and the Board registers you on that advice.

What does the RANZCR assessment look at?

Your qualifications, training and clinical experience — resulting in a finding of substantially, partially or not comparable. “Substantially comparable” is the most direct route; “partially comparable” usually means a period of supervised practice and/or top-up requirements before full specialist recognition.

Will I start under supervision?

Often, yes — many specialists begin in a supervised role on provisional (or limited) registration while completing any College requirements, then move to full specialist recognition. In a market this short of radiologists, suitable supervised posts are usually easy to arrange.

What about the visa?

Most consultants relocate on an employer-sponsored visa, with skilled routes also available. The College assessment and the visa are separate workstreams; we help line them up so they don't hold each other up.

How long does the RANZCR assessment usually take?

It varies with your training, your paperwork and the College's current workload, so we avoid firm promises. The biggest thing in your control is a complete, well-evidenced application — we help you assemble it so nothing stalls, and we keep the role and visa moving in parallel so the timelines don't hold each other up.

What does “substantially comparable” mean?

It's RANZCR's finding that your training and experience are close enough to an Australian-trained radiologist's to lead, most directly, to specialist registration — usually after a short period of supervised practice (peer review) before full specialist recognition.

Can I work privately?

Yes. Australia has one of the world's largest private radiology markets, and many consultants work privately, in public services, or in a mix of both. Any conditions on your registration while you complete College requirements shape the timing — we'll set that out clearly for your situation.

Will my fellowship be recognised?

That's exactly what the RANZCR comparability assessment determines. Fellowships from comparable training systems are often found substantially comparable; we'll give you a clear read on your likely outcome before you commit time and money.

Can I relocate with my family?

Yes. The common visa routes carry partner work rights and schooling for children, and relocation support is frequently part of the package. We help line up the move for the whole family, not just the role.
