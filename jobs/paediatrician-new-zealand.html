What the work is actually like

The whole of child health, in one job.

The medicine will feel familiar within a fortnight. What changes is how broad your list is, and how much child health happens away from the ward.

Breadth

General paediatrics is the job itself

Outside the tertiary centres you carry acute paediatrics, neonatal cover, clinics and community work in the same week. Consultants who enjoy the whole of child health thrive; those who came to sub-specialise can find it a bigger change than expected.

The team around you

Small departments, and you are at the cot side

Departments are small enough to know the registrars, nurses and community team by name. Consultants are expected on the floor, and most describe the hierarchy as flatter than the one they left.

Equity

Child health here is measured on equity

Services are measured on outcomes for tamariki Māori and Pacific children, which shapes clinics, outreach and service design. It is part of the consultant role, not a line in an induction pack.

Worth knowing early

Neonatal cover is the thing to check first.

Most general paediatric posts include neonatal cover, and the level of the unit decides what you’ll be doing at three in the morning. It’s the detail people most often find out after accepting, so we get the job description and roster before you fall for a town.

Sub-specialty practice sits on a different pathway. The quicker route covers general paediatrics only. Other paediatric specialties, including dual specialties, are assessed individually with the RACP advising, which takes considerably longer. If you mainly practise in a sub-specialty, plan for that from the start.

Sub-specialty services such as oncology, cardiology, complex neurology and intensive care sit in a handful of tertiary centres. Elsewhere you carry a broad general list with a special interest attached. That suits some people exactly and frustrates others, so be clear with yourself early.

Getting registeredYour specialty is one of the quicker ones

Reviewed August 2026 · checked against the Medical Council of New Zealand’s current published guidance

You register with the Medical Council of New Zealand in the vocational scope of paediatrics. The route is the same for every specialist; three details are specific to you.

General paediatrics is on the fast-track list. A UK Fellowship or Membership of the relevant Royal College, with a CCT from the GMC or PMETB or a CCST from the STA, is on the Council’s approved list, as is an Irish Membership or Fellowship with a CSCST. The Council publishes twenty working days for a complete application.

A FRACP needs no assessment. If you trained through the RACP’s Paediatrics & Child Health Division, your fellowship is an approved Australasian qualification. No comparability exercise, no college advice to wait for.

Sub-specialists, dual specialties and other training take the longer route. Anything other than general paediatrics is assessed individually, with the RACP advising the Council against the FRACP standard. The same applies if you trained outside the approved countries. Allow considerably longer, and start the college paperwork early.

What the Council will want to see, whichever route you are on:

  • A consultant-level job offer — a listed prerequisite on the fast-track pathway
  • Primary-source verification of your medical degree and specialist qualification
  • Evidence of at least 24 months’ recent practice in the specialty
  • A certificate of good standing from every authority you have registered with
  • Identity, criminal-history and English-language evidence where required

Order matters more than speed. Verification through EPIC or MyIntealth is usually the slowest step, so it starts first. The job offer comes next, because the fast track requires one. The Council application is built around both, with the visa running alongside.

Which vocational pathway fits you

Almost every consultant paediatrician we move arrives by one of four MCNZ vocational pathways. They reflect what you already hold, not how good a doctor you are, and yours decides your timeline more than anything else.

VOC4 · fast track

A UK or Irish award, with a CCT, CCST or CSCST

General paediatrics is one of nine areas on the Council’s fast-track list. A UK Fellowship or Membership with a CCT or CCST, or an Irish award with a CSCST, qualifies. A FRACP is already an approved Australasian qualification, so it goes to VOC1 or VOC2 instead.

20 working daysCouncil’s published time for a complete application
VOC1

FRACP, and already registered here

An approved Australasian postgraduate qualification plus general registration in New Zealand. Nothing is assessed for comparability; your fellowship is the standard.

10 working daysThe quickest route the Council publishes
VOC2

FRACP, but no NZ general registration

The same qualification, without New Zealand general registration yet. Most RACP fellows moving from Australia come this way.

20 working daysFrom a complete application
VOC3

A sub-specialty, a dual specialty, or training elsewhere

Paediatric specialties other than general paediatrics sit here, alongside anyone trained outside the approved countries. Your training is assessed individually, with the RACP advising. Early college paperwork saves the most time on this route.

Around three monthsIn most cases, once the application is complete

What the fast track actually asks of you

VOC4 has prerequisites the other routes don’t, and one surprises people: a consultant-level job offer in Aotearoa New Zealand is required, so the role comes first. You also need 24 months’ practice in paediatrics at 0.5 FTE or more in the past five years (12 of them in the 18 months before you apply) in a comparable health system, three verified references, and your qualifications already moving through EPIC / MyIntealth. Verification is usually the slowest step, which is why we start it first.

And what follows approval

VOC3 and VOC4 both lead to provisional vocational registration. You practise under a named supervisor in an approved position; that is a condition of the scope, not a judgement on you. If your job, location or supervisor changes, apply to vary your scope at least 20 days ahead.

Two dates to plan for. Your registration meeting takes place in New Zealand within two weeks of your start date, and you can’t practise until it’s done and your practising certificate is issued, which takes about three days. Moving to the full vocational scope is an application you make once the Council’s requirements are met; it doesn’t happen automatically.

Where this comes from. Pathways, prerequisites, processing times and approved areas above are from the Medical Council of New Zealand’s published guidance, read in August 2026 against its approved-qualifications list dated 13 August 2026. Times are for complete applications; an incomplete one restarts the clock. Check your own case with the Council before deciding.

Life in New ZealandA short commute, and the coast or the hills at the end of it.

Paediatricians tend to arrive with the family decision already made, and it is usually what settles the move. School is close, the commute is short, and the outdoors is on your doorstep.

What people mention next is time. Rosters are built to last a career, and departments are small enough that leave is a conversation, not a contest. For families, that combination usually decides it.

Registration sits with the Medical Council and visas with Immigration New Zealand. We give you the realistic range, keep your file moving and chase what can be chased, but we won’t quote a faster number to win you over.

Common questions

Frequently asked

I’m a paediatric sub-specialist. Does the fast track apply to me?

No. The Council’s approved list names general paediatrics only. Other paediatric specialties, including dual specialties, take the longer route with the college advising.

That affects your timeline, not how you’re judged. It’s better to know before you apply, because it changes when you can realistically start.

How much neonatal work comes with a general paediatric post?

More than most consultants expect, and it varies by hospital. Some posts carry a defined neonatal commitment; others share cover across a small consultant group.

We get the job description and roster in writing, because this is the detail people most often discover late.

Would I be expected to cover adult medicine?

No. Paediatrics is its own vocational scope. Where a small hospital runs a combined out-of-hours arrangement, the paediatric commitment is defined, and we read it before you commit.

What is on-call actually like?

Main centre: a larger roster with registrar cover in front of you. Regional: a thinner rota, called more often, and you are the one who drives in.

Paid at penalty rates plus an availability allowance, and we show you the actual rota before you decide.
