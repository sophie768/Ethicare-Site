About Ethicare

This move is about far more than your career.

It’s your family, your registration, the money, the people you’re leaving behind. That’s the conversation we’re here for.

Why we exist

We didn’t start Ethicare because there was a gap in the market. We started it because people making this move deserved better than they were getting.

Finding the right role is one part of a successful move. It’s everything around it that keeps people awake at night: the registration, the schools, the money, the distance from family, the friends you’re saying goodbye to.

So we start there. We help you look at the whole picture honestly, before you commit to anything. Sometimes that means saying yes, this is right, let’s go. Sometimes it means saying not yet: wait a while, sort one thing out first.

Telling someone to wait almost never wins us the placement. It’s usually the right thing to say anyway. That, far more than any list of company values, is who we are.

A note from Sophie

Why I started Ethicare

I never set out to build a traditional recruitment company — I came to it from inside the NHS. Most of a decade in workforce transformation at one of the country’s largest teaching hospitals, and my own move to the other side of the world and back, taught me the same thing: this is about people staying, not gaps being filled. Ethicare grew from both.

Read my story ▾

I never set out to build a traditional recruitment company. For years I worked inside the NHS, most recently as Senior Service Improvement/Transformation Manager at the Royal Free London NHS Foundation Trust, one of the country’s largest teaching hospitals. I led workforce improvement programmes there: reducing temporary staffing spend, the trust’s patient engagement strategy, waste reduction. It taught me how much depends on people staying, and how often that comes down to the human things no job description ever mentions.

I know what it’s like to pack up your life and start again somewhere new: a year travelling around Australia, then several years living in Wellington, where I met my partner.

What bothered me was watching international recruitment treat people like a gap on a rota. Someone gets sold a role, lands on the other side of the world, and the phone goes quiet exactly when the hard part begins. That always felt backwards to me. This isn’t a vacancy being filled. It’s a person, often a whole family, changing their life.

So I built Ethicare the way I wish someone had done it for me. We tell people the truth, even when it costs us the placement. We stay small on purpose, so you speak to the same people the whole way through. And we don’t disappear once you’ve landed, because that’s usually when you need us most.

If you’re weighing this up, I’d be glad to help you think it through. No pressure — just an honest conversation.

Open by default

Candidates deserve better than that.

Recruitment agencies traditionally share information only when there is a vacancy to fill.

A move to Australia or New Zealand can affect your career, your finances, your family and your everyday life. You should be able to understand what you are getting into before anyone asks you to apply for a job.

So Ethicare does two things, and both of them are the business: we recruit into carefully chosen roles across Australia and New Zealand, and we get the people who take them, and the families who come with them, through registration, visas, arrival and the first year. But we don’t believe good information should only reach the people we place. Our registration guides, comparison tools and relocation resources are free to use whether you find your role through Ethicare, apply directly to an employer, or work with another agency entirely.

Already found your job?

You don’t need to have been placed by Ethicare to use our relocation support. If you have secured a role directly with an employer or through another agency, Ethicare Move is still yours to use: our free planning tool covering registration, the visa, the budget, schools, housing and what to do when.

Healthcare first, recruitment second

Our background is NHS leadership: planning and reshaping the clinical workforce from the inside. That order matters. We think about staffing the way the people who run services do, as something that has to hold up over years rather than weeks.

Done well, this is workforce transformation, not vacancy filling. A clinician who arrives prepared and stays gives a service far more than one who covers a gap for a few months and leaves, so retention is what we design for. No health system has a workforce large enough to do this alone, and the range of experience international clinicians bring makes services stronger.

What it’s actually like to work with us

Less about what we offer, more about how it feels. This is what people tend to say once the move is behind them.

01

We’ve done this ourselves

We know how the move goes, because we have spent years guiding it and because we have lived it. Not much surprises us anymore, which means far less of it has to surprise you.

02

We tell you the truth

Even when it isn’t what you were hoping to hear. If the timing is wrong, or a move doesn’t add up for your family, we will say so. You can trust our yes because we are willing to say no.

03

We’re deliberately small

We take on fewer people and give each of them far more. You will deal with the same faces from the first conversation onward, never passed around, never a number in a pipeline.

The honest bit

What we can’t promise

A move this size deserves the limits as well as the pitch. Four things you will never hear us guarantee — and what we do instead.

01

Timelines that aren’t ours to give

Registration sits with your professional regulator and visas with the immigration authority. We give you the realistic range, keep your file complete and moving, and chase what can be chased — but we will not quote a faster number to win you over.

02

A specific job in a specific suburb

Vacancies move. If your heart is set on one workplace in one corner of one city, we will tell you honestly how long that wait could be — before you resign anything, not after.

03

A supervisor waiting at the other end

Some registrations are granted subject to a period of supervised practice, and that only works if the department has a supervisor with the capacity to take you. We ask the question before you accept and we pass on exactly what we are told — but the answer belongs to the employer, and departments lose supervisors at short notice.

04

That the move is right for you

Some moves should not happen. If the scope, the pay maths or the family logistics do not stack up, we will say so plainly. We would rather lose a placement than see you land in the wrong life.

After you land

The part most agencies skip

Getting someone hired is the easy half. The months after the plane lands are the ones that decide whether the move lasts, so that is where most of our work happens.

01

When you arrive

We know your arrival date, and you have a mobile number a person answers. The same people you have dealt with all along, who know your name and your situation. We call again once the adrenaline wears off and the admin lands all at once: tax number, bank account, a GP, school forms.

02

Once life settles down

A deliberate check-in a few months in, timed for the dip, because we know it is coming. It is the call people are most surprised to get and most glad of. Not a survey, just an honest conversation.

03

For the long term

There is no end date. A contract question, a move to another region, work for your partner, a reference years later. We measure this by how you are living a year on, not by the placement.

We’re a small team, not a concierge service. We won’t organise your weekends, and the settling in is still yours to do. What we promise: you’ll always know who to call, and they’ll already know your story.

Some questions a guide can’t answer

Once you have accepted a role, Sophie introduces you to a clinician who made the same move recently, matched on profession where we can, and on household where that matters more. Some things you can only hear from someone who has already done it. We also sit down once a quarter to answer whatever is being asked.

Community often matters more than salary.

The people who stay are the ones who found somewhere to belong. People rarely regret a move they prepared for. The regret comes from rushing it.
