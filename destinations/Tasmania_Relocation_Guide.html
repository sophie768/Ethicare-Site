Welcome to Tasmania

A warm welcome, you’re in good hands

Deciding to move your life and career to the other side of the world is a big step, and an exciting one. Whether Tasmania is already home to people you know or a brand-new chapter, I want you to feel supported from the very first conversation.

Tasmania is Australia’s island state: wild, beautiful and small enough that everything feels close. Hobart pairs a historic harbour with remarkable food, art and mountain-and-sea living, and the whole island is a playground of wilderness, produce and clean air. It’s a lifestyle move as much as a career one.

This guide walks you through healthcare, visas and registration, finances, housing, schooling and cost of living across the state, then breaks down the main places you might live so you can picture daily life in each. Think of it as the conversation we’d have over coffee, in your pocket, whenever you need it.

Welcome to Tasmania. We’re so glad you’re here, and we’ll be alongside you every step of the way.

Section one · Overview

The island state

Tasmania is Australia’s only island state, small and wild, where about 570,000 people share some of the cleanest air and most spectacular wilderness on earth, split between the south around Hobart and the north around Launceston.

Ethicare insight

Candidates often picture Australia as one job market. It isn’t. Tasmania registers nationally through Ahpra but hires locally through the Tasmanian Health Service, and as a smaller system it often values, and looks after, the people it recruits.

Overview · Culture

Ancient country, island life

The muwinina people were the traditional custodians of the Hobart area, known as nipaluna, and today’s Tasmanian Aboriginal community (palawa/pakana) continues one of the oldest living cultures on earth, a heritage increasingly recognised across the island.

Tasmania blends a deep Aboriginal heritage and a layered colonial history with a thriving modern culture, anchored by MONA, the boldest art museum in the country, and festivals like Dark Mofo that light up the winter. Its food, whisky and wine scene punches far above the island’s size, and the wilderness is always close.

Sophie’s tip

Learn the local names early, nipaluna (Hobart) and kunanyi (Mount Wellington). Using them warmly is genuinely appreciated and helps you feel part of the community faster.

Overview · Transport

Getting around

Tasmania is small, and a car is the main way to get around, distances are short but public transport is limited outside the cities. Hobart and Launceston have local bus networks.

Driving

Australians drive on the left. A car is near-essential here, but distances are short, most of the island is within a few hours, and the drives are spectacular.

Buses

Metro Tasmania runs buses in Hobart, Launceston and Burnie on the tap-on Greencard; services are lighter than on the mainland.

The ferry

The Spirit of Tasmania sails from Devonport to Geelong, handy for bringing a car and belongings from the mainland.

Flying

Hobart and Launceston airports link to Melbourne, Sydney, Brisbane and beyond, the quickest way on and off the island.

Sophie’s tip

You can usually drive on a valid overseas licence for a period after arriving, but you’ll need to transfer to a Tasmanian licence once you’re a resident. Plan to have a car here, and consider the Spirit of Tasmania if you’re bringing belongings from the mainland.

Overview · Climate

A year in the Tasmanian climate

Tasmania has a cool-temperate climate, four gentle seasons, mild summers and crisp, sometimes snowy winters, closer to England or New Zealand than to mainland Australia. The seasons are flipped from the Northern Hemisphere.

Ethicare insight

Two things surprise newcomers. Tasmania’s summer daylight is long and glorious, but the sun is fierce, the UV is strong despite the cool air, so sun protection is a year-round habit. And winters are genuinely cold, so factor in heating and warm bedding, a point worth making to family before they arrive.

Sophie’s View

Why I like Tasmania

One of the things I love most about Tasmania is how close everything feels, the wilderness, the coast and a genuinely warm community are all on your doorstep. Whether you’re drawn to Hobart’s harbour and culture, the elegant north around Launceston, or the green south and the coasts, there’s a real sense of belonging here. For healthcare professionals, a smaller, close-knit system often means you’re valued and looked after in a way the big cities can’t match.

Having spent many years in the NHS, I know how easily work can take over. What stands out to me about Tasmania is how deliberately people make time for life here, a walk up kunanyi, a market on Saturday, a weekend among the vines or the wilderness. If I could give one piece of advice, it would be to think about your commute, your community and what you’ll enjoy on your days off. Those things shape your happiness more than people expect.

Section two · Healthcare

Healthcare in Tasmania

Tasmania runs a single statewide public health system, universal Medicare cover, four major hospitals and a private sector, delivered through the Tasmanian Health Service and centred on the Royal Hobart Hospital.

Go deeperUnderstanding healthcare in Australia→

Royal Hobart (RHH)

Tasmania’s largest hospital and principal referral centre, in central Hobart, and the state’s site for cardiothoracic surgery, neurosurgery and burns.

Launceston General

The major hospital for northern Tasmania, a busy teaching hospital serving the Tamar and the north-east.

North West Regional

In Burnie, the referral hospital for the north-west coast, working alongside the Mersey Community Hospital.

Mersey Community

At Latrobe near Devonport, providing elective surgery and sub-acute care for the north-west.

Good to know

Eligibility for Medicare usually begins once you hold an appropriate visa. Reciprocal Health Care Agreements give some countries limited Medicare access. Ambulance cover is not automatic in Tasmania, though free ambulance may apply in some circumstances, many people add ambulance cover to their private health insurance to be safe.

Visa rules change regularly. Always confirm current requirements with the Department of Home Affairs (homeaffairs.gov.au) and your Ethicare contact before making decisions. Registration · Ahpra

Professional registration

Most healthcare professions are regulated nationally through Ahpra, the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency, and its profession-specific boards.

One national register

Once registered with Ahpra you can work anywhere in Australia, including all of Tasmania, registration is national, not state-by-state.

Qualification assessment

Overseas qualifications are assessed against Australian standards; some roles require bridging programs or exams.

English language

You’ll usually need to evidence English proficiency through an approved test or a recognised pathway.

Start early

Registration takes time, begin gathering documents and references well before you plan to travel.

Ethicare insight

For a Tasmanian move, the thing that slows a start date is almost always late paperwork. Certified qualifications, references and ID take time, so begin the moment the island is on your shortlist, the smaller system rewards the well-prepared.

Section four · Banking & Finance

Setting up your finances

Getting your finances in order early makes day-to-day life much easier. Most of this can be started before you arrive.

Opening a bank account

The major banks are CommBank, Westpac, NAB and ANZ, plus Tasmania’s own Bank of us (a local mutual). Many let you open online before you arrive, then verify ID in branch with your passport.

Tax File Number (TFN)

Every worker needs a TFN from the ATO. Apply online once you arrive and give the number to payroll so you’re taxed correctly. Without it, you’ll be taxed at the highest rate.

Sophie’s tip

Open your bank account before you fly where you can, having an account ready makes your payroll setup and first rent payment far smoother in those hectic first weeks.

The money set-up, in order

1Activate your bank account, verify ID in branch, order a debit card and online banking.
2Apply for your TFN, online via the ATO, then give it to payroll immediately.
3Choose your super fund, or accept the employer default, then review later.
4Set up salary packaging, ask payroll or your provider on day one if you’re in public health.
5Register for Medicare, if eligible under your visa or a reciprocal agreement.
6Get a local SIM & number, many banking and government steps need an Australian mobile.
Sophie’s tip

The salary is only half the story on an island move. The people who settle into Tasmania most smoothly budget for the bridge, the few weeks between arriving and your first full pay clearing. Think about temporary accommodation, a rental bond (typically four weeks’ rent lodged with the Rental Deposit Authority), a car (near-essential here), and setting up a home. Planning for it removes almost all of the early stress.

Sophie Careem · Founder & Director

Section seven · Housing & Areas

Finding a place to live

From Hobart’s harbourside suburbs to coastal and valley towns, Tasmania offers beauty and value, but the rental market is tight, so preparation matters.

Rentals, especially in Hobart, move quickly and can be competitive. You’ll usually apply with ID, references and proof of income, then pay a bond (typically four weeks’ rent) lodged with the Rental Deposit Authority. Many newcomers rent short-term first to learn an area. The main sites are realestate.com.au (largest) and domain.com.au.

Choosing your area, the order that saves the most stress

1Fix your work site first. Everything flows from your commute, decide it before you fall for a suburb.
2Check school catchments if you have children. Local intake is guaranteed; out-of-area isn’t.
3Shortlist two or three areas and rent short-term in one before committing.
4Factor in heating. Ask how a home is heated before you sign, it matters in a Tasmanian winter.

Utilities & getting set up

Power

Aurora Energy is the main electricity retailer; Tasmania runs largely on hydro power. Set up on move-in.

Water

TasWater; if renting, most water charges are the landlord’s.

Internet

NBN via Telstra, Optus, TPG or Aussie Broadband; coverage is good in towns.

Setting up home

Kmart, IKEA and Bunnings furnish a first home fast; Marketplace for second-hand.

Sophie’s tip

No Australian rental references? Bring landlord references from home, proof of income and savings, and your job offer as clean PDFs. In Hobart’s tight market, a complete, instant application really stands out.

Housing & family · Choosing where to live

Choosing where to live

For many healthcare professionals, the job is only half the decision. The other half is where your family will settle well. Schools, commute, housing cost and lifestyle are best weighed together. Here’s a practical way to think it through.

Schools often come first

Access to a government school is usually tied to where you live. Popular schools can be pinned to an exact address, catchments can change street by street, and out-of-area places aren’t guaranteed. If you have children, check the catchment before you sign a long-term lease. Independent, Catholic and faith schools offer more flexibility, but usually charge fees.

Your commute still matters

Tasmania is a car island, public transport is light outside Hobart and Launceston, so plan to drive. The upside is short distances: most commutes are quick and the drives are lovely. Think about your shift times, whether the local buses reach your site, and parking, and remember that in winter you’ll value a warm, easy commute.

The order that usually works best

A simple sequence that keeps the big decisions in the right order.

  1. 1Confirm your likely work site.
  2. 2Shortlist suitable school options if you have children.
  3. 3Check the catchment maps for those schools.
  4. 4Compare commute times to work at shift hours.
  5. 5Review rental availability and cost.
  6. 6Rent short-term first where you can.
  7. 7Choose a longer-term suburb once you know the area.

Section eight · Shopping & Food

Groceries, food & community

The essentials in one place, the big chains, the budget picks, and the island’s extraordinary local produce, which is a big part of life here.

The big chains

Woolworths & Coles for full-range everywhere; ALDI for the cheapest weekly shop; IGA and independents for neighbourhood top-ups, vital in regional towns.

Flavours of home

Salamanca and the farm-gate markets for local produce; the major supermarkets carry a growing world range; and specialty importers and a handful of Asian and continental grocers in Hobart and Launceston cover most staples.

Sophie’s tip

Bring your own bags, single-use plastic bags are banned in Tasmania, and locals keep reusable ones and a cooler bag in the car for market runs.

Good to know

Tasmania’s specialist food range is smaller than the mainland’s, but growing. Hobart and Launceston have some halal outlets and the supermarkets carry a modest halal range; dedicated kosher and some specialist ingredients are limited and often ordered online. The upside is extraordinary local produce, seafood, cheese, berries, whisky and cool-climate wine, much of it straight from the farm gate.

Faith & community · Places of worship

Religion & places of worship

Australia has no state religion and law protects freedom of belief. Tasmania is less religiously diverse than the mainland capitals but welcoming: at the last census around half of residents reported no religion, Christianity remained the largest faith, and Islam, Buddhism, Hinduism and other communities are established and growing in Hobart and Launceston.

Most communities list service and prayer times online and warmly welcome newcomers, often one of the quickest ways to build a support network after you arrive.

The main faiths & where to find them

Christianity

Anglican, Catholic, Uniting and other churches statewide, including some of Australia’s oldest, such as St David’s Cathedral in Hobart.

Islam

Mosques in Hobart (the Hobart Mosque) and Launceston serve the Muslim community, with the Islamic Council of Tasmania as a point of contact.

Buddhism

Several temples and meditation centres in and around Hobart and Launceston, reflecting the island’s Asian communities.

Hinduism

A growing community with temple and cultural activities in Hobart, supported by the Hindu Society of Tasmania.

Other faiths

Sikh, Baháʼí and other communities are small but active, mainly in Hobart and Launceston.

Community networks

The Migrant Resource Centre and multicultural councils help newcomers find their community and places of worship.

By location, what you’ll find near you

Faith & community · Diversity & inclusion

Diversity, inclusion & belonging

Tasmania may be small, but it is warmly welcoming, and the law protects everyone’s right to be treated equally. Whoever you are, and whoever your family is, you’re welcome here.

Go deeperReligion, community & belonging in Australia→

A multicultural home

Tasmania is smaller and historically less diverse than the mainland, but its migrant communities are growing fast and the island is warmly welcoming. The Migrant Resource Centres in Hobart and Launceston, and multicultural councils, help newcomers settle, connect and belong.

Your rights in law

The Tasmanian Anti-Discrimination Act 1998 is one of the broadest in the country, and with federal law it protects people from discrimination on the basis of race, religion, age, sex, disability, sexuality and gender identity. Equal Opportunity Tasmania handles complaints.

LGBTQ+ inclusion

Same-sex marriage has been legal Australia-wide since 2017, and Tasmania, once the last state to decriminalise homosexuality, now has some of the most progressive gender-recognition laws in the country. TasPride and events across Hobart and Launceston feature, and QLife (1800 184 527) offers free peer support.

Inclusive workplaces & care

A growing number of health services hold Rainbow Tick accreditation or run active inclusion networks. Interpreters are free through TIS National (131 450), and the NDIS supports people with disability.

Ethicare insight

The Tasmanian Health Service and most employers have active diversity and inclusion commitments, and many run staff networks for culturally diverse, First Nations and LGBTQ+ workers. If belonging at work matters to you, tell us, we can factor a team’s culture and inclusion record into where we suggest you land.

Section ten · Education & Family

Schooling & family life

Tasmania offers a full range of early childhood education, schools and universities, run by the state’s Department for Education, Children and Young People. The school year runs late January to mid-December across four terms, a flip from the Northern Hemisphere calendar worth planning around.

Go deeperEducation & schools in Australia→

Early childhood

Long daycare, kindergarten and family day care for ages 0–5, with subsidies.

Primary

The first years of school through to the end of primary, across government, Catholic and independent schools.

Secondary

The senior years, ending with the Tasmanian Certificate of Education (TCE), leading to university or training.

Tertiary

The University of Tasmania (UTAS), with campuses in Hobart, Launceston and Burnie, plus TasTAFE.

Public school fees for visa-holder families

Tasmania is generous on tuition, dependants of 482 visa holders don’t pay tuition fees, only a modest school levy.

Ethicare insight

Tasmania is one of the kindest states on school costs. Dependants of 482 visa holders are exempt from tuition at government schools; instead you pay a modest annual school levy, typically somewhere between about $300 and $750, charged directly by the school. Low-income families can have the levy waived through the Student Assistance Scheme. Non-government schools set their own fees. It’s a small, predictable cost rather than a major budget item.

School catchments come first

For Tasmania government schools, your home address usually determines your local-intake (catchment) school, children inside the zone are generally guaranteed a place, while out-of-area places depend on capacity, and popular schools can be oversubscribed. Boundaries can run street-by-street, so always check the exact address before you sign.

For families, the school decision usually leads the housing decision here, deciding it first prevents an expensive mid-year move.

Education & Family · Partners & work

Employment for your partner

A move only works if it works for the whole family. The good news: on most skilled and employer-sponsored visas your partner is included with full work rights, free to take any job, full or part-time, with any employer.

Work rights come as standard

Partners added to a 482, 186, or skilled 189/190/491 visa generally have unrestricted work rights, no separate employer sponsorship needed. Always confirm the exact conditions on your grant letter.

A broad job market

Tasmania has a smaller but healthy job market, with demand across healthcare, education, tourism and hospitality, agriculture and aquaculture, construction and the public service, concentrated in Hobart and Launceston.

Helping your partner land work

1Reformat the CV to Australian style, concise, achievement-led, no photo or date of birth. Set up a strong LinkedIn with an Australian location.
2Search the main boards, SEEK, Indeed and Jora, plus the Tasmanian Government jobs site and health-service careers pages for public-sector roles.
3Check if the profession is regulated, teaching, nursing, trades and others need registration or a skills assessment. Start it before you arrive.
4Register with recruitment agencies, many Tasmanian roles are filled through agencies, who can also advise on local expectations and pay.
Ethicare insight

In a smaller market like Tasmania, a partner’s employment can make or break a move, so we plan for it early rather than hoping it falls into place. Tell us what your partner does; if they’re in healthcare too, we can often help them directly.
