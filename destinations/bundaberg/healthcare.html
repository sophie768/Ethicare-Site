Bundaberg’s health service is growing faster than its population, for one structural reason: the wider Wide Bay region is one of the fastest-growing corners of regional Queensland. Understanding that trajectory is the difference between reading this as a small-city posting and reading it accurately.

The system

A regional referral centre
The region’s public hospital anchors the Wide Bay Hospital and Health Service, covering Bundaberg, the Fraser Coast, North Burnett and the Discovery Coast.
A large, growing catchment
The wider service area covers more than 225,000 people, a number the state expects to keep growing for decades.
A hospital being rebuilt for growth
The public hospital is mid-way through one of regional Queensland’s largest health infrastructure projects, adding beds, theatres and diagnostic capacity.
A genuine private sector
Private hospital and specialist imaging services operate alongside the public system.
Brisbane is the tertiary backstop
The most complex cases are referred south to Brisbane or Sunshine Coast tertiary centres, a normal part of how regional Queensland operates.
A note on names
  • We don’t name hospitals, trusts or private providers as places of work anywhere on this site — a named employer implies a relationship, and the post and the package get confirmed with you directly.
  • Everything above is described structurally for that reason. If you want to know exactly where a role sits, ask us and we will tell you plainly.

What a growing region does to your work

A regional health service under active investment pulls in two directions at once, and both are true of the same job.

What the region gives you
Broader day-to-day scope than a metropolitan subspecialty post, plus the rare chance to help shape a service as its capacity expands, rather than joining one already at capacity.
What it asks of you
Comfort with a smaller specialist bench than Brisbane, and with a service still building out some of the capacity a bigger centre already has.

Imaging and radiation, specifically

This is where our Queensland work is concentrated, so it is the part we can speak to in most detail. A growing regional service under active capital investment is a specific kind of opportunity for imaging and radiation professionals: genuine breadth across modalities day to day, real prospects as new capacity comes online, and a case mix drawn from a large, largely rural and coastal catchment that a metropolitan department would not see in the same proportions.

How pay works here

Registration is national but pay is not. In the public system, salaries are set by the relevant Queensland health system industrial agreement, negotiated between the health service and the union covering your profession — so the same role can sit on a different scale in another state. In the private sector, pay is negotiated individually and is often higher, particularly where a service is hard to staff.

We do not publish figures we have not verified against the agreement that actually governs the role. For the indicative bands and how the frameworks work, see Salary in Australia, explained honestly.

What to ask at interview

  • How is the current hospital redevelopment expected to affect my role and team?
  • Which cases go to Brisbane or the Sunshine Coast, and how is that handled day to day?
  • How many colleagues sit alongside me in my specialty specifically?
  • What is the on-call pattern, and how often is it genuinely called upon?
  • Which modalities or sub-specialties would I be expected to cover?
  • What CPD time and funding is provided, and does it cover travel to Brisbane?
  • Is there scope to cross-train, and is that supported or just permitted?
The short version

Bundaberg is a regional health service investing heavily to keep pace with a growing catchment, which is why the day-to-day scope is wider than the city’s size suggests. The trade is a smaller bench and a genuine trip to Brisbane for tertiary care — and for imaging specifically, real breadth in a service that is actively expanding.
