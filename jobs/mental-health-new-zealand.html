One team

Different expertise, brought to the same person’s care

Mental health care in New Zealand is highly multidisciplinary. Depending on the service, the same vacancy may be open to professionals from several different clinical backgrounds — so this page starts with the ecosystem, and only then asks which profession you hold.

Psychiatry

Medical assessment, diagnosis, treatment and specialist leadership.

Psychology

Psychological assessment, formulation and evidence-based intervention.

Mental health nursing

Ongoing clinical care, assessment, medication, risk and recovery support.

Occupational therapy

Function, participation, routines, sensory needs, recovery and independence.

Social work

The wider social picture: whānau, housing, safeguarding, advocacy and community support.

Worth knowing before you search

Sometimes the role matters more than the profession. A New Zealand employer may advertise for a “mental health professional” and consider applicants from several registered professions — looking for the right combination of mental-health experience, clinical skills, cultural capability and registration rather than one specific title.

If you are an occupational therapist or social worker who searched your own job title and found little, this is often why. The role you are right for may not be advertised under your profession’s name.

Where the work happens

Where might that team work?

Mental health careers here don’t all sit inside a “mental health department”, and public specialist services are only part of the picture. A psychologist might be working in rehabilitation, persistent pain, dementia assessment or workplace health; a psychiatrist might specialise in old age, addiction, forensic, liaison, child and adolescent or general adult psychiatry.

The mix of professions changes with the setting too — an eating-disorder team might bring together psychiatry, psychology, nursing, dietetics and medicine, while a community team runs on psychiatry, nursing, psychology, occupational therapy and social work alongside peer and community support.

The job isn’t only what happens in the consulting room.

Imagine someone with severe mental illness who is also struggling to keep their tenancy and their job. Five professions, five contributions — and a system around them.

Psychiatrist

Manages diagnosis and treatment.

Mental health nurse

Coordinates ongoing care.

Psychologist

Provides psychological intervention.

Occupational therapist

Works on daily functioning and return to meaningful activity.

Social worker

Addresses housing, finances and family circumstances.

Around them, the team may be working with a GP, a housing provider, an employment service, an addiction service, a peer-support worker and the person’s whānau. Services are also in a period of change — rather than print a summary that dates, we would rather tell you the current state of it in conversation.

Across the family

What applies across mental health

The things that are the same whichever of these professions you hold.

Who regulates this work?+

Each profession has its own regulator, which is why this page ends at your profession rather than starting there. Psychiatrists register with the Medical Council of New Zealand; psychologists with the New Zealand Psychologists Board, across several scopes — and the scope you are granted matters more than the registration itself; nurses with the Nursing Council; occupational therapists with the Occupational Therapy Board; social workers with the Social Workers Registration Board.

Registration is the first gate, and it runs on its own timeline.

What does the pay look like?+

Public-system pay is set by collective agreement rather than negotiated role by role, so the same job pays the same wherever in the country you do it. Private and ACC-funded work sits outside that and varies.

Indicative bands sit on each profession’s own guide above.

Which visa would I need?+

Most clinicians move on a work visa tied to an accredited employer, and some roles in this family sit on the Green List, which changes the route to residence considerably.

The visa can usually run in parallel with registration rather than after it.

What about the rest of the move?+

Schools, housing, healthcare, banking, shipping and the first few weeks are the same for every profession, so we explain them once rather than on each page.
