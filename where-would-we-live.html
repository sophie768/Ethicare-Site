Choosing a region

Where could we live?

This is the one that gets decided by accident. A role comes up in a particular city, so that becomes the answer — and only later does anyone ask whether it was the right place for the people who came too. We understand why it happens that way. So our aim here is to give you the question back: start from what matters to you and your household, and see which places fit. Housing, the commute, schools and how far you are from a big hospital vary far more between regions than between the two countries.

What matters to you, before the map

Tell us what you would want from a place and what you would miss from home — a proper city or somewhere unhurried, the coast or the mountains, a big tertiary department or a broad regional scope, housing you can afford, and who is coming. We weigh the regions against your answers and show you three, with the trade-offs as well as the good bits.

Seven questions, about a minute. Change any answer and run it again. If you have already told us your country or who is coming, those are filled in.

Where people get stuck

The version of choosing a place

We are a recruitment consultancy, so you would expect us to say yes to wherever the role is. Here is what we think, including the parts that do not help us commercially.

Sometimes another city is the better answer

If the role is in the wrong place for your household, the move tends not to last — and a placement that unravels helps nobody. Several of our destination guides say plainly when somewhere else would suit you better.

The big cities are not automatically easier

They have the departments, the flights and the communities. They also have the housing costs and the commutes. Plenty of people are happier in a smaller place with a base hospital and a fifteen-minute drive.

“Near the beach” is doing a lot of work in a job advert

Near can mean ten minutes or ninety. So can “close to the city”. Our guides give real distances and real commutes, because that is what you will live with.

Visit if you possibly can

Not everyone can, and a good guide is the next best thing. But a week in a place tells you something no page can, and it is cheaper than moving twice.

If you are torn between two places, say so. We have had this conversation many times and we are happy to have it without a role attached.
