Before you decide

Why New Zealand?

A move this big is about far more than the job.

New Zealand is the second most peaceful country in the world, and the most peaceful in the Asia–Pacific. It has ranked first in the world for life–work balance three years running, measured across factors including statutory leave, working hours, healthcare and safety. Life outside work is taken seriously here.

If you have children, the schooling picture is reassuring: New Zealand’s 15-year-olds perform above the OECD average in maths, reading and science. And on happiness, New Zealand ranks 11th globally, ahead of Australia, the United States and the United Kingdom, and the highest-ranked English-speaking country in the world.

It is a long way from home — flights back to the UK, South Africa or elsewhere are a real cost to plan for — and housing, groceries and everyday essentials cost more than many people expect. What people say they love once they are here, what nobody puts in the brochure and how the distance feels are the same conversation in either country.

The day to day

Practising in New Zealand healthcare

Beyond the registration and the paperwork, the day-to-day culture of practising here is part of what draws people. A few things our clinicians consistently notice. Your profession guide goes deeper for your specific role.

Where you’ll work

Public, private or community

Work might be in a Health New Zealand hospital, private provider or community setting. The environment shapes the pace, variety and feel of your working week.

The culture

Less hierarchy, more team

Teams tend to be collaborative and relatively flat, with first-name relationships and multidisciplinary working part of everyday practice.

Equity at the centre

Care shaped by Te Tiriti

Te Tiriti o Waitangi influences how healthcare is designed and delivered, with a particular focus on equity and improving outcomes for Māori.

On pay

Most public healthcare roles here are paid under a national collective agreement rather than negotiated individually. Your step on the scale is the same in Invercargill as it is in Auckland, and the ladder is published. We can point you at the documents rather than asking you to trust a figure.

Bringing your family

Can my family come too?

For the healthcare professionals we work with, partners are usually eligible to work in New Zealand, children can generally come with you, and many healthcare roles offer a pathway to residence.

The exact visa route depends on your role and circumstances, but for most candidates this can be a family move, not something you do alone.
