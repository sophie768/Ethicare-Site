Moving to Australia

New South Wales is Australia’s front door: the harbour city of Sydney, the largest economy and healthcare job market in the country, and behind it a state of surf coasts, wine valleys, world-heritage mountains and genuine outback. More clinicians start their Australian lives here than anywhere else.

The state that has one of everything

Sydney is the picture most people carry of Australia — the harbour, the bridge, the beaches — and it holds around five and a half million of the state’s eight and a half million people. It is a true world city with the salaries, dining, culture and housing costs to match.

The part fewer people picture is everything else. An hour north, the Hunter Valley runs from surf beaches at Newcastle through horse and wine country. An hour south, Wollongong and the Illawarra sit between an escarpment and the Pacific. The coast runs seven hundred kilometres in each direction — the subtropical Northern Rivers one way, the quiet oyster towns of the South Coast the other. Inland: the Blue Mountains, the Riverina’s canola plains, and eventually the red dirt of the far west. In winter, the Snowy Mountains are actual snowfields.

For a clinician the state offers the widest choice in Australia — the biggest tertiary concentration in the southern hemisphere in Sydney, strong regional centres with genuine incentives, and rural medicine as remote as you want it. The honest cost is Sydney’s housing, which asks more of a clinical salary than any other Australian city.

Genuinely. Warm humid summers, mild autumns, frosty inland winters — and snow in the Snowies.

Six things you only learn once you’re here

There are several Sydneys
The eastern beaches, the leafy north, the inner west and the vast growing west are different cities with different price tags. Most of the clinical work is west of the postcard — Where clinicians live is honest about it.
Hiring is local, not state-wide
Registration is national, but jobs live in individual health districts. Where you apply is where you’ll live — decide the place first.
Regional means incentives
The further from Sydney, the harder the recruiting — and the more employers offer. The Hunter, Illawarra and regional cities are where value and lifestyle meet.
Tolls are a line in the budget
Sydney’s motorways are tolled, and a western-suburbs commute can cost serious money each week. Locals plan around it; so should your suburb choice.
Summer is humid, houses aren’t built for winter
Air conditioning is universal; insulation and heating often aren’t. July indoors in Sydney surprises people from colder countries.
The state is enormous
Broken Hill is closer to Adelaide than to Sydney. "Moving to NSW" tells us almost nothing until you’ve chosen your hundred kilometres of it.
A note on Country
  • New South Wales sits on the lands of many Aboriginal nations — Gadigal, Dharug, Awabakal, Wiradjuri, Bundjalung and dozens more. Acknowledgement of Country opens meetings, and health services work in partnership with Aboriginal community-controlled organisations across the state.
  • You are not expected to arrive across all of it. You are expected to listen and learn — and the cultural induction your service provides is worth taking seriously.
