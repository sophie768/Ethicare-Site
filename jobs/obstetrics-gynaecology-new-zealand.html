What the work is actually like

You are the specialist a midwife refers to.

The obstetrics and the gynaecology will feel familiar. The system around them will not, and it is worth understanding properly before you judge a job description.

The maternity model

Continuity sits with a midwife, not with you

Most pregnancies are carried by a Lead Maternity Carer — usually a midwife — who holds the care from booking to six weeks after birth. Consultants see referred and complex cases, run clinics and theatre, and provide the specialist advice the model depends on. It is a genuine change from a consultant-led service.

Mixed lists

Both halves of the specialty, in the same week

Outside the tertiary centres a post is genuinely obstetric and gynaecological: labour ward, clinics, elective and acute operating. A generalist appetite matters more here than in most specialties, because that is what the job actually is.

The team around you

Small departments, flat relationships

You would know the midwives, the anaesthetists and the theatre teams by name, and consultant presence on the labour ward is expected rather than negotiated. Most consultants describe the working relationship as noticeably flatter than the one they left.

The Lead Maternity Carer model is the single biggest adjustment. Referral pathways are defined and the working relationship with midwives is a professional partnership rather than a hierarchy. Your clinical authority is unchanged; what changes is how a woman reaches you, and who holds her care between the times she does. Consultants who expect to run every pregnancy find this harder than those who came looking for a specialist role with a clear edge to it.

Sub-specialty work is concentrated. Maternal-fetal medicine, gynaecological oncology, urogynaecology and reproductive medicine sit in a handful of tertiary centres. Elsewhere you would carry a generalist list with an interest attached — which suits some people exactly and frustrates others, so it is worth being honest with yourself early.

Abortion care sits in health law here. Since the law changed in 2020 it is regulated as health care rather than under the criminal law, and provision is part of ordinary service planning. Conscientious objection provisions exist and services differ, so this is a question worth asking of a specific post rather than of the country.

Private gynaecology operates in the larger centres and sits comfortably alongside a public post. Private obstetrics is a smaller market than in many systems, largely because the public model already delivers the continuity that private care is often bought for.

Getting registered

Your specialty is one of the quicker ones

Reviewed August 2026 · checked against the Medical Council of New Zealand’s current published guidance

You register with the Medical Council of New Zealand in the vocational scope of obstetrics and gynaecology. The route is the same one every specialist follows; three details are specific to you.

Obstetrics and gynaecology is on the fast-track list. A UK Fellowship or Membership of the relevant Royal College, held together with a CCT issued by the GMC or PMETB or a CCST issued by the STA, sits on the Council’s approved list — as does an Irish Membership or Fellowship held with a CSCST. The Council publishes twenty working days for a complete application on that pathway.

A FRANZCOG needs no assessment. If you trained through RANZCOG, your fellowship is an approved Australasian qualification. No comparability exercise, and no college advice to wait on.

Trained elsewhere? RANZCOG advises the Council. Qualifications outside those lists are assessed against the FRANZCOG standard, on the college’s own process. Allow considerably longer, and start the college paperwork early rather than after you apply.

What the Council will want to see, whichever route you are on:

  • A consultant-level job offer — a listed prerequisite on the fast-track pathway
  • Primary-source verification of your medical degree and specialist qualification
  • Evidence of at least 24 months’ recent practice in the specialty
  • A certificate of good standing from every authority you have registered with
  • Identity, criminal-history and English-language evidence where required

The order matters more than the speed. Primary-source verification through EPIC or MyIntealth is almost always the slowest step, so it starts first and runs while everything else happens. The job offer comes next, because on the fast-track pathway it is a prerequisite rather than a formality. The Council application is built around both, and the visa runs alongside it rather than after it.

Which vocational pathway fits you

Almost every consultant obstetrician and gynaecologist we move arrives by one of four MCNZ vocational pathways. They differ in what you already hold, not in how good a doctor you are — and the one that applies to you decides your timeline more than anything else in the move.

VOC4 · fast track

A UK or Irish award, with a CCT, CCST or CSCST

Obstetrics and gynaecology is one of the nine areas of medicine on the Council’s fast-track list. A UK Fellowship or Membership with a CCT or CCST, or an Irish award with a CSCST, sits on the approved list. A FRANZCOG doesn’t need this route — it is already an approved Australasian qualification, so it goes to VOC1 or VOC2.

What the fast track actually asks of you

VOC4 has prerequisites the other routes don’t, and one of them surprises people: a job offer in Aotearoa New Zealand at consultant or specialist level is a listed prerequisite, so the role comes first and the application is built around it. You’ll also need 24 months’ practice in obstetrics and gynaecology at 0.5 FTE or more within the past five years — 12 of them in the 18 months before you apply — in a health system the Council treats as comparable, three verified references, and your qualifications already moving through EPIC / MyIntealth. Verification is usually the slowest single step, which is why we start it before anything else.

And what follows approval

VOC3 and VOC4 both lead to provisional vocational registration rather than the full scope. You practise under a named supervisor in an approved position — supervision is a condition of the provisional scope, not a judgement on you. If your job, location or supervisor changes during it, you apply to vary your scope at least 20 days ahead.

Two things worth diarising. Your registration meeting happens in New Zealand, within two weeks of your intended start date, and you cannot practise until it is done and your practising certificate issued — allow about three days after the meeting for that. And moving to the full vocational scope is an application you make once the Council’s requirements are met, not something that arrives on a date.

Where this comes from. The pathway definitions, prerequisites, processing times and approved areas of medicine above are the Medical Council of New Zealand’s own published guidance, read in August 2026 against its approved-qualifications list dated 13 August 2026. Times are for complete applications; an incomplete one restarts the clock, and the list is added to periodically. Check your own case against the Council before you make a decision on it.

Life in New Zealand

Long nights on the labour ward, and a short drive home.

A twenty-minute commute, and the water or the hills at the end of it.

Obstetrics is intense wherever you do it, and nobody here will pretend otherwise. What differs is what happens either side of the shift: the commute is short, the department is small enough that cover is a conversation, and the rota is built around a whole career rather than a decade of it.

The other half is what the time off is worth. Most hospitals sit within half an hour of open water, a river or a mountain, so a weekend does not need planning weeks ahead. For families that is usually the argument that settles it.

The nights are the same everywhere. The drive home is not.

What we can’t promise

A move this size deserves the limits as well as the pitch. Three things you will never hear us guarantee — and what we do instead.

Timelines that aren’t ours to give

Registration sits with the Medical Council and visas with Immigration New Zealand. We give you the realistic range, keep your file complete and moving, and chase what can be chased — but we will not quote a faster number to win you over.

A specific job in a specific city

Obstetrics and gynaecology recruits widely, but the exact combination of sub-specialty interest, unit size and town you have in mind may not be open this year. If your heart is set on one place, we will tell you how long that wait could be — before you resign anything, not after.

That the move is right for you

Some moves shouldn’t happen. If the scope, the pay maths or the family logistics don’t stack up, we will say so plainly. We would rather lose a placement than see you land in the wrong life.

Common questions

Frequently asked

How does the midwife-led model change my job?

Most pregnancies here are carried by a Lead Maternity Carer, usually a midwife, who holds the care from booking through to six weeks after birth. You see referred and complex cases, run clinics and theatre, and provide the specialist advice the model depends on.

Your clinical authority is unchanged. What changes is how a woman reaches you, and who holds her care between the times she does. Consultants either take to that quickly or find it a real adjustment, and it is worth knowing which you are before you accept a post.

Will I keep my sub-specialty work?

Maternal-fetal medicine, gynaecological oncology, urogynaecology and reproductive medicine concentrate in the tertiary centres. Elsewhere you would carry a generalist obstetric and gynaecological list with a special interest attached.

Tell us which matters more — it decides which hospitals we put in front of you.

What is on-call like in a regional unit?

Thinner than you may be used to. Fewer consultants share the rota, you are called more often, and you are the one who drives in.

Against that, birth numbers are lower and the theatre team knows you. Paid at penalty rates plus an availability allowance — and we give you the actual rota and the annual birth numbers before you decide.

Is there private practice?

Private gynaecology is available in the larger centres and sits comfortably alongside a public post, provided it does not cut across your contracted commitments.

Private obstetrics is a smaller market here than in many systems, largely because the public model already delivers continuity of care. Worth calibrating your expectations on that before you plan around it.
