The professions

One board, several scopes

“Nurse” covers several register entries here, and midwifery is assessed in its own right — the same board, but not the same application.

Registered nurses

The largest workforce in the system, with two assessment routes and a published self-check for overseas applicants.

Enrolled nurses

A second-level scope with its own register entry — not a junior RN.

Nurse practitioners

Advanced scope with prescribing — endorsed on top of RN registration.

Specialty nursing

ICU, theatre, emergency, mental health — the specialty shapes the week more than the title.

Midwives

Registered in their own right — assessed separately from nursing even where you hold both.

Worth knowing before you search

The self-check is worth doing before anything else. The Nursing and Midwifery Board publishes an assessment route for internationally qualified nurses and midwives with a self-check at the front of it — which stream you land in decides your documents, your costs and your timeline.

Do it before you spend money on anything. The pathway checker walks you into the right version of it.

The structural fact

National registration, state employment

Your registration crosses every border. Your employment does not: each state and territory hires on its own enterprise agreements, with its own pay scales, ratios and progression.

What travels

Your registration

One national register through Ahpra. Move from Queensland to Tasmania and your registration comes with you — no re-application, no new board.

What doesn’t

Your terms

Pay, ratios, rostering and progression are set state by state. Two offers with the same title are two different agreements — compare the agreements, not the titles.

The register crosses borders. The payslip does not.

What it is like to practise here more broadly — the funders, the states, cultural safety and what feels different — is explained once, properly, in practising in Australia.

Where the work happens

Where might that work take you?

Hospital wards are the visible part. A large share of Australian nursing happens in primary care, aged care and the community — and midwifery models vary by state and service.

The settings

The map, honestly

Where the roles actually are, and what each setting changes.

Public hospitals

Acute, specialty and critical care on state agreements — ratios and rostering vary by state.

Private hospitals & clinics

A large sector in its own right, on employer agreements rather than state ones.

Aged care

Residential and community aged care — where demand is growing fastest, with its own funding and standards.

Primary, community & remote

Practice nursing, community health and remote-area nursing — the broadest scopes and the strongest need.

Across the family

What applies across nursing & midwifery

The things that are the same whichever of these professions you hold.

Who regulates this work?+

The Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia, through Ahpra — one board and one national registration that travels between states and territories. Nursing scopes and midwifery are separate register entries with separate applications.

Registration is the first gate, and it runs on its own timeline. How registration works in Australia · Check your pathway

What does the pay look like?+

Registration is national; pay is not. Public terms are set state by state through enterprise agreements, so the same job pays differently in Perth and in Hobart — and comparing two offers means comparing two agreements. Private and scheme-funded work varies again.

For any live vacancy the package is confirmed with you directly — we don’t quote figures against named roles.

Which visa would I need?+

Most clinicians move on an employer-sponsored work visa, and several professions in this family sit on the skilled occupation lists, which shapes the route to permanent residence.

The visa can usually run in parallel with registration rather than after it. Visas and residence

What about the rest of the move?+

Schools, housing, healthcare, banking, shipping and the first few weeks are the same for every profession, so we explain them once rather than on each page.
