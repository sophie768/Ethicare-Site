Chapter 04

Creating an international recruitment programme

Sophie CareemFounder, Ethicare Resourcing · former NHS change leader
Toolkit · 04 of 08
OverviewThe workforce challengeWhy it matters01 · Building a sustainable workforce02 · Ethical & responsible sourcing03 · Planning & preparation04 · Creating a recruitment programme05 · Relocation & family integration06 · Induction & belonging07 · Retention & belonging08 · Measuring success

The strongest programmes don’t start with a vacancy. They start with a pipeline.

Most international recruitment runs like a relay race: advertise, interview, registration, visa, arrival, each leg handed off and forgotten. The best candidates rarely survive it. Somewhere around month four of silence, another country calls them back. The strongest programmes treat the whole journey as one relationship, started early and never dropped.

The alternative is a pipeline that never switches off: you know who might be ready in a year, and they hear from you before there’s a vacancy to fill. International recruitment is a programme, not a project.

The most successful programmes don’t fill vacancies faster. They continuously build future workforce capacity.
Part 01

Building a talent pipeline

Move away from reactive recruitment. The best candidates are often six to twelve months from being available, so the relationship should start now.

Where a pipeline comes from

Map

  • Talent mapping
  • Workforce forecasting
  • Talent communities

Network

  • Professional associations
  • Universities
  • Conferences

Nurture

  • Referrals
  • Previous applicants
  • International networks
Part 02

Identifying potential early

One of the biggest differentiators: don’t wait for registration, and don’t only look at who’s available today.

Look for the things that predict whether someone will thrive and stay: how they work, why they’re moving, and whether the whole family is behind it. Registration status tells you none of that.

Questions worth asking early

  • Who has genuine potential?
  • Who could be ready in twelve months?
  • Who would become exceptional with the right support?
Part 03

Conditional offers

The practical answer is the conditional offer. After interview and assessment, a candidate with the right qualifications, experience and values receives a genuine offer of employment, conditional on completing professional registration within an agreed timeframe. The candidate gains the confidence to invest; the employer secures talent early, rather than competing for the few already fully registered.

Crucially, professional standards never change. Employment only proceeds once every requirement set by the relevant regulatory authority has been met.

Conditional offers don’t lower standards. They give talented professionals the confidence to begin the journey.

“Slower, more complex and expensive” than international counterparts: that is how Australia’s National Cabinet–commissioned review described registration for overseas practitioners, before recommending 28 reforms to make it simpler, quicker and cheaper.

The Kruk ReviewRobyn Kruk AO, Independent review of overseas health practitioner regulatory settings: final report August 2023, endorsed by National Cabinet December 2023

Reform is already visible. Fast-track pathways opened for GPs from New Zealand, the UK and Ireland in 2024, comparable-country nurses followed in 2025, and a record 16,622 internationally qualified nurses registered in Australia in a single year, close to three times pre-pandemic numbers. The pipeline you build today moves faster than the one you built two years ago.

Part 04

Supporting the journey

Between offer and arrival is where candidates are won or lost. Support the whole path, and stay in touch.

What the journey needs

The process

  • Registration guidance
  • English-language testing
  • Immigration & documentation

The preparation

  • Interview preparation
  • Workplace readiness
  • Relocation planning

The person

  • Family & wellbeing
  • Regular communication
  • Progress reviews
Every month without communication increases the risk of withdrawal.
Best practice · Keeping a pipeline warm

The months between offer and arrival are where candidates are won or lost:

  • Make conditional offers, so registration becomes a shared project rather than a lonely one.
  • Check in monthly. Silence reads as indifference.
  • Celebrate the milestones: registration granted, visa approved, flights booked.
  • One named contact per candidate. Never a shared inbox.
Part 05

Candidate experience

Candidate experience is easy to overlook, and it’s directly tied to retention. Reply quickly. Tell the truth about delays. Notice when a candidate goes quiet, because that’s usually when a competing offer has landed. The best pipelines are built on relationships, not spreadsheets: how someone is treated before they start shapes how long they stay after.

Part 07

Common challenges

Named honestly, most are manageable. You will not avoid every risk. The job is to see each one coming and shrink it.

Challenges, and how to reduce them

Process delays

  • Registration & English tests
  • Immigration delays
  • Plan realistic timelines & buffers

Losing momentum

  • Competing offers
  • Candidate withdrawals
  • Keep communicating, stay engaged

Life happens

  • Changing family circumstances
  • Financial pressures
  • Supervision availability

Building your recruitment programme: a checklist

Before you launch a campaign
  • Have we identified priority professions?
  • Do we have a talent pipeline, not just a vacancy list?
  • Are we engaging candidates before registration is complete?
  • Can we make conditional offers?
  • Is structured registration support available?
  • Are we communicating regularly throughout the journey?
  • Is relocation and family support in place?
  • Are candidates prepared for their first day before they arrive?
