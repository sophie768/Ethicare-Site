Question one

Can we hire internationally at all?

Almost always yes — if it’s a role a board will register and the timeline is realistic. Four things decide it, and it’s worth knowing them before you advertise.

01 · The gate

Registration decides everything after it

Whether the clinician can register with the relevant board sets the visa, the start date and the offer. It’s the first thing we check — before anyone’s time is spent.

See the registration checker →
02 · The route

The visa follows the pathway

Once the board route is clear, the work visa follows a known process. We sequence it and keep the file moving; the decisions stay with the immigration authority. This is coordination, not immigration advice.

03 · The clock

A realistic timeline

For the professions we cover, offer to arrival is typically five to nine months. We’ll tell you where your role is likely to sit before you commit to the route, rather than after.

04 · The fine print

Supervision and accredited posts

Some boards expect a supervised period, or a post that meets an accreditation standard, before a clinician can work unsupervised. Better known before you advertise than after you appoint.

Question two

Who’s actually out there — and are they real?

The honest answer today: ask us. We can tell you who we’re already talking to in your profession and region, and exactly where each person stands on registration — the one fact a CV database can’t give you.

What we’re building

  • Registration-verified availability — profession, board and stage, so “available” means a real date, not optimism.
  • When someone could realistically start, read from where they are in the process.
  • The regions they’d actually move to, so a shortlist fits the posting.
  • Kept current by the clinician, because it’s the relocation plan they already use — not a profile to maintain.

What it isn’t

  • Not a CV dump. Those exist, they’re commoditised, and they earn nothing on trust.
  • Not a job board with profiles. The value is the pre-qualification, not the matching.
  • Invisible by default. Opt-in, one named introduction at a time, contact details released only on acceptance.
  • Never gated for candidates. Every tool stays free to the clinician, however they got their job.

It opens to employers when there’s demand to justify it — five services asking “who have you got in WA?” is a service worth running; a list with no one asking is just a list. So for now it works the reliable way: tell us the role and the region, and we’ll answer personally.

Question three

Will they stay?

The question that decides whether you hire this way again. Retention isn’t an HR problem — it’s a relocation one, and it’s the work we’re built around.

“Sophie always puts candidate care first, and has invested heavily in preparing candidates for a successful move — ensuring they have as much support as possible when relocating to a new country.”

Maree LoynesFormer Allied Health Recruitment Specialist, Health New Zealand MidCentral

A regional allied health role that had stayed open

The challenge

A regional allied health service, one role open long enough that the gap had started to feel normal. Local advertising hadn’t moved it, and the location was never going to sell itself from a job description alone.

The search

A small, targeted search rather than a volume campaign. The decisive conversations weren’t about the role: where the candidate stood with the board, what their partner would do, and whether the timeline was realistic for a family.

The outcome

Registered, relocated and still in post — so the service filled a role it had stopped expecting to fill, and didn’t have to run the whole process again a year later.

Why it holds: we don’t stop at arrival

The first year is where a placement is won or lost, so it’s where we stay — personal coordination of registration, visa and the move itself, then contact through settling in: the bank account, the GP, the school place, the fortnight when a family wonders whether they got it right. For clinicians we place it’s included as standard; for your direct hires it’s available by arrangement.
