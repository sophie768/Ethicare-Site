The practical side

Registration, pay and your move

Registration

The Medical Council of New Zealand registers all doctors. For specialist recognition, your College assesses whether your training is comparable, and that finding usually decides your pathway. Registration runs on its own timeline and is nearly always the longest part of the move, so it is the thing to settle before you give notice or enrol a child in a school.

Check your pathway →

Pay

Public-system pay is set by a national collective agreement, so your base salary comes from a published scale. Your step on it, your hours and your on-call commitment move the total. Private and ACC-funded work sits outside the agreement.

What you could earn →

Immigration

Most doctors move on a work visa tied to an accredited employer. General practice, anaesthesia, psychiatry, emergency medicine, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, radiology, ophthalmology and the physician and surgical specialties sit in Tier 1 of the Green List (Straight to Residence), checked against Immigration New Zealand’s Appendix 13 in force from 9 March 2026. The list changes, so confirm your own occupation before planning around it. The visa can usually run alongside registration.

The shape of the system

Fewer layers between you and the patient

New Zealand is small enough that the same consultant may see a patient in clinic, operate on them, take the call about them at two in the morning and write to their GP afterwards. Sub-specialty depth is thinner than in a large system; generalist breadth is expected, and valued.

Referrals

The GP is the front door, and the loop closes with them

Referrals arrive from someone you may know by name, and continuity is a working structure: general practice holds the long-term relationship, and patients return there after discharge.

General practice · outpatient clinics · shared care
Acute care

Built around distance

Smaller departments, consultants on the floor, and air and road retrieval as a routine part of acute care.

Emergency medicine · anaesthesia · retrieval · nursing
On the ward

Decisions reach you sooner

Fewer registrars and thinner sub-specialty cover, and a great deal of care after discharge carried by community teams, district nursing, aged residential care and accident-funded rehabilitation.

Senior medical officers · registrars and RMOs · allied health · community nursing
What surprises most consultants

You will do more of your own work. Fewer registrars, thinner sub-specialty cover, and a service that expects you to be a generalist inside your specialty. For many international consultants that broader responsibility is both the attraction of working here and the biggest adjustment of the first year.

The second surprise is the size of the professional community. In most specialties you will know your counterparts around the country by name within a year or two, which makes referral relationships personal and the multidisciplinary meeting less formal and considerably more useful.

Settings and regions

Where the work happens

A tertiary post, a regional hospital and a rural generalist role are further apart here than the job titles suggest. Location sets your case mix, your on-call, your sub-specialty support and how far away the nearest scanner is.

Hospitals

Hospital medicine

Most of it happens outside the main centres.

Regional & secondary hospitals

Broader case mix, less sub-specialty support, a team small enough that everyone knows each other, and clinical decisions that are yours to make. A good number of consultants arrive intending to take a tertiary post and stay in one of these instead.

Often around you: SMO colleagues across specialties · registrars and RMOs · nursing · allied health · imaging · the local GPs

Tertiary centres

Sub-specialty work, complex referrals, teaching and research, concentrated in a handful of cities, with the national service responsibility that goes with it.

Public and private

Public hospital services mean unselected acute work, the full multidisciplinary system, collective-agreement pay that is the same wherever you do the job, and the on-call that comes with it. Private practice is elective lists, more control over your week and income outside the agreement, often alongside a public appointment. The two differ in case mix, autonomy and what you are responsible for, and many consultants hold both.

Community

Outside the hospital

Careers where the working week looks nothing like a ward round.

General practice & community

Longer consultations and continuity of care, with a business model attached to the clinical one.

Rural generalist

Broad scope, autonomy, and distance as a clinical factor in every decision. Deeply rewarding, and best entered with a plan for the professional isolation that comes with it.

Retrieval, transport & telehealth

Geography makes this core work: air and road retrieval, and telehealth that stands in for a clinic.

Aged residential care

An increasing share of medicine, mostly outside hospital walls, and one of the quieter routes into a portfolio career.

Population and portfolio

Beyond direct care

Clinical careers where the patient is a population, a cohort or a student.

Māori & Pacific health providers

Kaupapa Māori and Pacific organisations delivering care on their own terms, with whānau at the centre of the model.

Public health & population health

Prevention, screening, outbreak and policy work — a clinical career with the patient defined as a population.

Academic, teaching & research

Joint appointments with the medical schools, trials work and supervision, usually alongside clinical sessions.

Locum & fractional work

A good way to try the country, and a specialty, before committing your family to either. Sometimes it is the better first step, and we will say so.

Location is a clinical decision

Two hospitals four hours apart can offer the same job title and a very different week: the on-call, the case mix, and how far the nearest tertiary centre is. The destination guides cover each region.

How we work

You do not have to work this out on your own

Two doctors with almost identical CVs can face different routes into New Zealand. Which College assesses you, where you trained, how recently you have practised in the specialty and what your logbook shows all move the answer.

We look at what you hold, where you trained and the kind of practice you want, then set out the route, the steps and the likely timeline. If there is an obstacle, we say so before you pay a College assessment fee.
