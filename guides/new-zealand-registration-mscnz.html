Stage 04 · Can I work there? · Medical Sciences Council of New Zealand

Registering as an anaesthetic technician

For internationally qualified anaesthetic technicians and UK operating department practitioners. Two routes in, one benchmark of two years’ experience, and a scope that widened in April 2025 — which route you are on decides almost everything you submit. The checklist is built into the page, so nothing you tick goes out of date.

Section 01

Understanding the Council

The Medical Sciences Council of New Zealand — Te Kaunihera Pūtaiao Hauora o Aotearoa — registers and oversees anaesthetic technicians, and medical laboratory science practitioners, under the Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act 2003. Its purpose is to protect the public by making sure everyone practising is qualified, competent and fit to do so.

A protected scope of practice

You cannot lawfully work as an anaesthetic technician in New Zealand without registration with the Council and a current annual practising certificate. Registration confirms you have met the standard in the scope; the APC is what permits you to work, renewed every year.

Case-by-case assessment outside the UK route

Overseas qualifications other than recognised UK ones are assessed individually against a standard set of criteria approved by the Council. The quality and completeness of the evidence you provide directly shapes the outcome.

Confirm your pathway→Evidence, application and fee→Council assessment→Registration in the AT scope→APC once resident, then practise
The APC cannot be issued in advance.

You can only apply for your annual practising certificate once you are resident in New Zealand or can evidence your move. Registration can be in hand before you travel; the certificate that lets you work cannot. Plan the start date around that.

Section 02

Which pathway applies to you

Two broad routes, and placing yourself correctly early saves real time. The first is streamlined; the second weighs your qualification, registration and experience individually against the Council’s criteria.

Route 1 — streamlined

UK-trained, HCPC-registered

You hold a United Kingdom qualification in anaesthetic technology and are registered and in good standing with the Health and Care Professions Council.

  • With around two years’ full-time-equivalent post-qualification specialised experience — roughly 3,680 hours — you will generally be eligible, though the Council may require a period of supervised practice once you start.
  • Course information is waived: no academic transcript, syllabus or clinical logbook.
  • With less than two years’ experience you can still apply and be assessed, which may include a Work-Based Assessment in practice.
Route 2 — individual assessment

All other overseas qualifications

You qualified outside the UK. Your qualification, registration and experience are weighed individually against the Council’s standard criteria.

  • Training closer in scope to New Zealand’s — Australia and Ireland, for example — tends to map more readily, but every application is considered on its own merits.
  • Course information is required: transcript, syllabus and clinical logbook where applicable (Section 04).
  • The completeness and clarity of that evidence is what decides the outcome and the timeline.
Check eligibility first.

Before assembling documents, complete the Council’s Eligible check form. It gives you an early read on whether you can apply, so you know where you stand before investing time and money.

Section 03

The expanded scope, and what it means for ODPs

From 1 April 2025 a new Gazette notice broadened the activities and responsibilities anaesthetic technicians in New Zealand can undertake, aligning the role more closely with international roles such as the UK’s Operating Department Practitioner.

The Council has said this reflects the increasing complexity of anaesthetic care and the need for a flexible, highly skilled workforce. Anaesthetic technicians may practise across operating departments, radiology, intensive care, maternity and emergency departments — anywhere anaesthesia is administered or airway emergencies are managed. Expanded scopes, for example post-anaesthetic care and peripherally inserted central catheters, are available on completion of an accredited training pathway and Council approval.

For ODPs

Clearer pathways into the New Zealand system

The expanded scope gives clearer registration pathways for Operating Department Practitioners who meet the relevant competencies. If you hold a recognised UK qualification and HCPC registration in good standing, you apply via the UK route in Section 02.

A profession in demand

On the skill-shortage list

Registered anaesthetic technicians sit on Immigration New Zealand’s skill-shortage list, so the role is viewed favourably for visa purposes. Lists change — check the current one before you rely on it.

Section 04

Qualifications and experience

What the Council needs to see depends on your pathway. The qualification and the experience evidence are required on both; the course information is required only if you trained outside the UK.

Your qualification

Route
Both routes
Provide
A certified copy of your qualification — certified as a true copy by a solicitor, Notary Public, Justice of the Peace or an Officer of the Court.
Tip
Confirm who may certify and the wording before you pay; getting it wrong means doing it twice.

Course information

Route
Non-UK route only
Provide
Academic transcript in English — subjects, grades and the grading system used. Academic syllabus or curriculum — the contents of each subject and the hours for each year of study. Clinical logbook, if applicable — the procedures completed during the clinical component.
Mistake
Missing any of the three where the route requires them.

Employment and experience

Route
Both routes
Provide
A statement from your most recent or current employer with start and end dates, normal contracted hours per week, and job title. An up-to-date CV detailing your relevant work experience and the countries in which you worked.
Mistake
An employer statement that leaves the full-time-equivalent hours ambiguous.
Evidence the hours.

The two-years, roughly 3,680-hours benchmark is central to the UK route. Make sure the employer statement puts the full-time-equivalent hours beyond doubt — a vague letter is the most common cause of a follow-up query.

Section 05

Eligibility and documents

Alongside your qualification and experience, the Council needs a set of supporting documents. Each exists for a reason, and understanding why makes it easier to satisfy.

Present it like a professional portfolio: clean, high-resolution colour scans; pages straight and complete; consistent naming; certification done correctly the first time. A well-organised application is read more favourably, and assessed faster, than a disorganised one containing exactly the same facts.

Identity

Why
To confirm who you are and link your records.
Provide
A certified copy of your passport or another government-issued photo ID, and one passport-style photograph certified on the reverse as a true likeness by a Justice of the Peace or referee. Certified evidence of any name change — marriage, birth or deed-poll certificate.

Current registration

Why
To confirm you are registered and in good standing where you practise.
Provide
Evidence of your current registration status and a Certificate of Good Standing — from the HCPC for UK applicants.
Timing
No more than six months old at submission. Requesting too early is a frequent, avoidable reason to start again.

Translations

Why
The Council assesses in English.
Provide
A certified translation to accompany any document not in English, supplied with the certified original.

Section 06

References and criminal history

Two requirements with precise rules that are easy to get slightly wrong — and both arrive at the Council directly from source, never via you, which makes them the slowest part of the file.

References

Two certificates of character

  • One Certificate of Standing and Experience — personal — completed by someone unrelated to you who has known you for a year or more.
  • One Certificate of Competence and Experience — professional — ideally from a registered anaesthetic technician. If you have not practised as an AT before, a recent lecturer or employer may be used.
  • Referees complete the Council’s own template and return it directly to the Council. Tell them to expect an email.
Criminal history

Four rules

  • From each country you have lived or worked in for more than twelve months, from the age of sixteen.
  • Produced by the National Police Authority for that country — not a local or state office.
  • Sent directly to the Council from the issuing authority. Third-party pre-employment checks are not accepted.
  • No more than six months old on the day you submit.
A conviction is not an automatic bar.

The right approach is full, honest disclosure. Non-disclosure is treated far more seriously than most underlying matters.

Section 07

English language requirements

Safe, precise communication with patients and colleagues in a high-stakes clinical environment.

Every applicant must meet the Council’s English Language Policy, which sets out several routes — for example evidence that English was the sole language of instruction and assessment throughout your qualification, or a recognised test result. Where a test is required the Council accepts IELTS; you provide a copy of your results. Confirm the current accepted tests and minimum scores against the Council’s live policy before booking, because the policy is periodically updated.

Sitting the test too early.

Applications take time to assemble, so an early test risks expiring before you submit. Time it so the result remains valid when your application reaches the Council.

Section 08

Cultural competence: the step you can start today

You must complete the Foundation Course in Healthcare and Te Tiriti o Waitangi, delivered by Mauri Ora Associates, and provide the certificate of completion with your application.

It builds your understanding of cultural competence and the obligations that flow from te Tiriti. Practice in New Zealand reflects a system-wide commitment to equitable health outcomes, and particularly to safeguarding the wellbeing of Māori. The Council’s Cultural Competence Standards are part of the competence framework every registrant is expected to meet.

Required with the application

Foundation Course in Healthcare and Te Tiriti o Waitangi

Online, delivered by Mauri Ora Associates. Of everything in this guide it is the step you can most easily begin today, before gathering a single other document — and a genuine introduction to the healthcare culture you would be joining.

Section 09

Applying, and what happens next

Once your evidence is assembled, the application itself is methodical.

  1. 1

    Complete the Eligible check

    Confirm you are eligible to apply before you commit time and money.

  2. 2

    Confirm your pathway

    UK and HCPC streamlined route, or individual assessment for other overseas qualifications.

  3. 3

    Assemble your evidence

    Qualification, experience, identity, registration, references, police checks, English, and the Mauri Ora certificate.

  4. 4

    Submit your application and fee

    Complete the application in the portal. The fee is paid on submission and is non-refundable.

  5. 5

    Council assessment

    Your qualification, experience and fitness are assessed. Supervised practice or a Work-Based Assessment may apply.

  6. 6

    Registration, then your APC

    Once registered in the AT scope, apply for your APC — required before you can practise, and only once you are resident or can evidence your move.

How long it takes — the Council’s stated aim

Around six weeks from a complete application

Once the Council has a complete application and the full fee, it aims to process it within roughly six weeks. Referee certificates and police checks must arrive directly from source, which is often the slowest-moving part — set those in motion early.

Fees

Always check the current figures

No amounts are printed here deliberately. Fees are non-refundable and the Council reviews them periodically. Confirm the current figures on the Council’s official fees schedule before you budget or pay.

Where people get stuck

Almost every avoidable delay traces back to one of these

Wrong pathway assumption

Assuming a non-UK qualification follows the streamlined UK route. Confirm your pathway first.

Vague experience evidence

An employer statement that does not make the full-time-equivalent hours — the roughly 3,680-hour benchmark — clear.

Third-party police check

A pre-employment background check instead of a national-authority check sent directly to the Council.

References sent via you

Referee certificates must reach the Council directly, on the Council’s template.

Stale Certificate of Good Standing

Older than six months at submission. Time it carefully.

Course information gaps

Missing transcript, syllabus or logbook where the non-UK route requires them.

The fix for all of these

Do the Eligible check, confirm your pathway, set referees and police checks moving early, and have everything reviewed before you submit. Most of these are invisible to the person making them.

Section 11

The questions we are asked most

Should I do anything before applying?

Yes. Complete the Council’s Eligible check form first. It gives you an early read on whether you can apply before you invest time and money.

I am a UK-trained ODP. Can I register?

The 1 April 2025 scope change opened clearer pathways for Operating Department Practitioners who meet the relevant competencies. If you hold a recognised UK qualification and are HCPC-registered in good standing, you apply via the UK route.

Do I need to provide my course syllabus?

Not if you are UK-trained — course information is waived for that route. Applicants from elsewhere provide the transcript, syllabus and, if applicable, the clinical logbook.

Will I need supervised practice?

Possibly. UK-trained applicants may be required to complete a period of supervised practice, and those with less than two years’ experience may undergo a Work-Based Assessment. It is designed to support safe practice in a new system, not to penalise you.

How long does registration take?

The Council aims for around six weeks once it has a complete application and the fee. Referee certificates and police checks arriving directly from source are often the slowest step — start them early.

When can I apply for my APC?

Once you are registered and either resident in New Zealand or able to evidence your move. You must hold a current APC before you can work in the scope.

Can Ethicare review my application?

Yes. We review your evidence — particularly your experience documentation and pathway choice — against what the Council looks for, and flag likely issues while they are still easy to fix. The registration decision always rests with the Council.
