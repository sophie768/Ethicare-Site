Why Australia

Modern oncology, sustained demand, and a country built for the outdoors

Radiation therapists are in strong, sustained demand across Australia's cancer services — public and private, metro and regional. Departments run modern linear-accelerator fleets and contemporary planning, the work is genuinely rewarding, and one national registration lets you move between states without starting over.

  • Strong demand across oncology services nationwide
  • A clear comparable-regulator fast lane for UK and Irish training
  • National registration recognised in every state and territory
"Modern planning, real patient continuity — and the coast on your days off."

Clinicians move here from across the world — the UK, South Africa, Ireland, the UAE and beyond — and many tell us the move gives back real time without compromising the work.

Getting registered

Registration overview

Reviewed July 2026 · checked against the Medical Radiation Practice Board of Australia’s current published guidance

Radiation therapist is a protected title, so you register in that division with the MRPBA through Ahpra. Keep one distinction clear: registration lets you practise, while a migration skills assessment (via ASMIRT) is a separate process for certain visas.

Whether your route is quick or involves extra steps depends on how the Board recognises your qualification. You'll typically be assembling:

  • Your radiation therapy qualification and full academic transcript
  • Clinical-placement evidence, where required
  • Registration history and a current Certificate of Good Standing
  • English-language evidence, where the standard requires it

Pathways into registration

Your route depends first on whether the Board already recognises your qualification.

Comparable-regulator fast lane

Programs approved by the UK's HCPC (therapeutic radiographers) and Ireland's CORU (radiation therapists) from 2010, and NZMRTB-accredited programs, within the Board's date ranges, are treated as substantially equivalent.

Qualification assessment

Otherwise the Board assesses your qualification against the Australian radiation-therapy standard. Where gaps remain, the outcome may direct you to the National MRP examination or a period of supervised practice.

Scope of practice

Across the cancer-care pathway

Radiation therapists work across planning and delivery, with real scope to specialise as you go.

The areas you can work in

The core of the job

Simulation and pre-treatment imaging, treatment planning, and image-guided delivery on linear accelerators — with patient set-up and reproducibility at its heart.

Where you can specialise

Dosimetry and advanced planning, brachytherapy, SABR and SRS, and specialist tumour sites — or education and research.

Registration and licence are separate

Registration is national, but using radiation is licensed by each state or territory, so you will usually also hold a state radiation-use licence. Your employer helps arrange it.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Do I need an ASMIRT assessment to register?

Not generally. The MRPBA assesses your qualification for registration; ASMIRT carries out migration skills assessments tied to certain visas (most often the points-tested 189, 190 and 491). On an employer-sponsored 482, an ASMIRT assessment generally isn't required just to register — but it may be needed for other migration routes. We'll confirm what applies to you.

Will my UK or Irish qualification be recognised?

Often, yes — UK therapeutic-radiographer programs approved by the HCPC and Irish radiation-therapist programs approved by CORU, from 2010 and within the Board's date ranges, are treated as substantially equivalent. NZMRTB-accredited programs are recognised too. Your qualification must match the Board's listed division, institution, title and award year.

Will I have to sit the National exam?

It depends on your assessment. Applicants recognised via a comparable regulator are generally exempt; otherwise the exam has a common Part A plus a division-specific Part B, set in the Australian context. Don't assume either way — your assessment confirms it.

Is registration the same as a radiation licence?

No. MRPBA registration is national and lets you practise; using radiation is licensed separately by each state or territory. You may need both — your employer usually helps with the local licence.
