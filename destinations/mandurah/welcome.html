Mandurah is a city built around water — an estuary twice the size of Sydney Harbour, a network of canals, and a long ocean coast — sitting under an hour south of Perth. For years it was where Perth went to holiday or retire. That is changing fast, and it is worth understanding why before you decide.

Six things nobody tells you

Before you decide
  • The “retirement town” label is out of date — the growth now is young families and commuters.
  • Summer is genuinely hot and dry; the estuary and the sea breeze are what make it comfortable.
  • Complex and subspecialty care is in Perth. Day-to-day work is here; the tertiary centre is an hour north.
  • The train changed the city. A Perth job while living on the coast is a real option, not a fantasy.
  • Crabbing is a genuine institution here, not a tourist gimmick — the estuary is full of blue swimmers.
  • Which water you live near — canal, estuary or ocean — shapes your budget and commute more than the suburb name.
In one line

A fast-growing waterside city under an hour from Perth, cheaper than the capital and built for an outdoor life — as long as you are clear-eyed that the big-hospital work is up the road, not on the doorstep.
