What the work is actually likeTime to get to know each patient.

Psychiatry here is community-first and consultant-light, which changes both the pace of a consultation and how much of the service you end up shaping.

The pace

A caseload you can hold in your head

What psychiatrists notice first is the length of a consultation and the size of a caseload. Neither is generous by any absolute measure — the system is stretched — but both are a different order from the ones most consultants arrive from.

The service

Being rebuilt, and you get a say

Mental health is where Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora is investing most visibly. There is real appetite for new models of care, and services are small enough that a consultant with a view can change how one works within a year.

Te ao Māori

A model of wellbeing that shapes the work

Frameworks such as te whare tapa whā are built into how services are designed here rather than offered as an aside. Nobody expects you to arrive fluent. Most consultants call it the most interesting shift of the move.

Most consultant posts are in community mental health, with acute inpatient, crisis and consult-liaison work alongside. Subspecialty posts — child and adolescent, older persons, addiction, forensic — all exist, but outside the main centres you would likely carry general adult work with them.

Compulsory treatment work is part of the role. Assessments, second opinions and tribunal work sit inside the job. If you have worked under comparable legislation the framework will feel familiar, but the terminology and the thresholds differ, and it takes a few months to feel fluent. Your supervision period exists partly for this.

Private outpatient practice is reasonably common outside contracted hours, and demand is strong in the larger centres. As with any senior medical role, it is fine provided it does not cut across what you have committed to publicly.

Getting registeredTwo things are specific to your specialty

Reviewed August 2026 · checked against the Medical Council of New Zealand’s current published guidance

You register with the Medical Council of New Zealand in the vocational scope of psychiatry. The route is the same one every specialist follows; two details catch psychiatrists out in particular.

There is an extra form, unique to your specialty. Psychiatry is one of only three specialties that require a supplementary information form on the fast-track pathway. It is easy to miss, and without it your application is not complete — which is a delay that reads like a decision.

Allow longer than the headline figure. The fast track is published at twenty working days for a complete application, but the Council states plainly that psychiatry applications may take longer. Better you hear that now than in month three.

RANZCP advises, the Council decides. Where your qualification is not on the approved list, the college assesses your training against the FRANZCP standard and gives advice. The decision itself remains the Council’s.

What the Council will want to see, whichever route you are on:

  • A consultant-level job offer — a listed prerequisite on the fast-track pathway
  • Primary-source verification of your medical degree and specialist qualification
  • Evidence of at least 24 months’ recent practice in the specialty
  • A certificate of good standing from every authority you have registered with
  • Identity, criminal-history and English-language evidence where required

The order you do things in matters more than the speed. Certificates of good standing and certified copies have short validity windows, and the Council’s window and Immigration New Zealand’s are not the same. We map that sequence with you and review your documents before anything is lodged.

Which vocational pathway fits you

Almost every consultant psychiatrist we move arrives by one of four MCNZ vocational pathways. They differ in what you already hold, not in how good a doctor you are. The one that applies to you decides your timeline more than anything else in the move.

VOC4 · fast track

A UK or Irish membership or fellowship, with a CCT, CCST or CSCST

Psychiatry is one of the areas of medicine on the Council’s fast-track list. Fellowship or Membership of the Royal College of Psychiatrists with a CCT or CCST, or Irish MRCPsych with a CSCST, sits on the approved list. An FRANZCP goes to VOC1 or VOC2 instead.

20 working daysPsychiatry may take longer, the Council notes
VOC1

FRANZCP, and already registered here

An approved Australasian postgraduate qualification plus general registration in New Zealand. Nothing is assessed for comparability, because your fellowship already is the standard.

10 working daysThe quickest route the Council publishes
VOC2

FRANZCP, but no NZ general registration

The same approved Australasian qualification, without general registration here yet. Most RANZCP fellows moving from Australia for the first time come this way.

20 working daysFrom a complete application
VOC3

Trained outside New Zealand and Australia

Your training and experience are assessed on their merits, with RANZCP advising the Council against the FRANZCP standard. This is the route where early college paperwork earns you the most time.

Around three monthsIn most cases, once the application is complete

What the fast track actually asks of you

VOC4 has prerequisites the other routes don’t, and one of them surprises people: a job offer in Aotearoa New Zealand at consultant or specialist level is a listed prerequisite, so the role comes first and the application is built around it. You’ll also need 24 months’ practice in psychiatry at 0.5 FTE or more within the past five years — 12 of them in the 18 months before you apply — in a health system the Council treats as comparable, three verified references, and your qualifications already moving through EPIC / MyIntealth. Verification is usually the slowest single step, which is why we start it before anything else. Psychiatry also has its own additional-information form, and the Council notes that psychiatry applications may take longer to assess than the published time.

And what follows approval

VOC3 and VOC4 both lead to provisional vocational registration rather than the full scope. You practise in psychiatry under a named supervisor in an approved position — supervision is a condition of the provisional scope, not a judgement on you. If your job, location or supervisor changes during it, you apply to vary your scope at least 20 days ahead.

Two things worth diarising. Your registration meeting happens in New Zealand, within two weeks of your intended start date, and you cannot practise until it is done and your practising certificate issued — allow about three days after the meeting for that. And moving to the full vocational scope is an application you make once the Council’s requirements are met, not something that arrives on a date.

Where this comes from. The pathway definitions, prerequisites, processing times and approved areas of medicine above are the Medical Council of New Zealand’s own published guidance, read in August 2026 against its approved-qualifications list dated 13 August 2026. Times are for complete applications; an incomplete one restarts the clock, and the list is added to periodically. Check your own case against the Council before you make a decision on it.

Life in New ZealandHeavy work, and somewhere to put it down at the end of the day.

A short commute, and the coast or the hills at the end of it.

Psychiatry is emotionally weighty work in any system, and the thing that makes it sustainable is what happens after the shift. Most services here sit in cities small enough that the commute is minutes and the coast or the hills are close enough for a Saturday.

That matters more in this specialty than in most. Consultants who move here talk less about the scenery than about having the capacity, at seven in the evening, to be present for their own family. It is not a lifestyle bonus. It is part of doing this job for a long time.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Will my subspecialty transfer?

Usually — child and adolescent, older persons, addiction and forensic posts all exist. But services are smaller than the tertiary centres most consultants come from, so outside the main centres you would likely carry general adult work alongside.

If a narrow focus matters, say so early: it changes which services make sense.

How much of the job is compulsory treatment work?

Assessments, second opinions and tribunal work are part of the role. If you have worked under comparable legislation the framework will feel familiar, but the terminology and thresholds differ — expect a few months to feel fluent. Your supervision period exists partly for this.

Is there private practice in psychiatry here?

Yes — outpatient work outside your contracted hours, and reasonably common. Demand is strong in the larger centres. It sits fine alongside a public role provided it does not cut across your contracted commitments.

What is on-call actually like?

It depends on the size of the service. Main centre: a larger roster with registrar cover in front of you. Regional: a thinner rota, called more often, lower volume.

Paid at penalty rates plus an availability allowance — and we give you the actual rota before you decide.
