Stage 06 · Find the right role

The short answer

Registration gets you eligible. It doesn’t get you hired.

Most clinicians who come to us are employable in Australia. The question is rarely whether you will find work, but where, how quickly, and whether the first role is the right one.

Hiring here is not one system. There is a public side with its own rhythm and rules, a private side with different ones, and a set of habits about CVs, interviews and timing that are easy to get wrong from the other side of the world. This page is the map. The CV & interview guide owns the detail of presenting yourself; this one is about the market you are presenting to.

“We don’t currently have a suitable role for you” never means “we can’t help you.” Reading the market honestly, saying where else to look and why an application is not landing is most of what recruitment expertise is. It is free, and it is below.

Section 01 · The market

How hiring works in Australia

Three things to understand before you send anything: who employs clinicians, how each of them decides, and where the roles actually appear.

The public system

Who hires, and how

Eight state and territory health services, each hiring on its own terms and under its own enterprise agreements. A role in one state tells you little about the next, and the same title can sit on a different grade and a different pay scale a border away.

Private and community

The other half of the market

A much larger private sector than New Zealand’s: private imaging, private hospitals, allied health practices, and general practice as private business. Faster decisions and more variation in what is offered, including whether an employer will sponsor a visa at all.

Where roles appear

Advertised, and not

The state health careers portals, the main job boards, your college or association’s listings, and agencies like us. Sponsorship is often stated in the advert; where it is not, ask before you invest a week in the application.

Section 02 · Demand

Where the demand is, honestly

Demand is real and uneven. Regional and outer-metropolitan services are usually easier to land than the capital-city departments, and some professions have a genuine shortage while others have locally trained graduates waiting. Some roles also carry location conditions tied to funding — general practice most of all — which the visa and registration guides explain. None of that is about you, and all of it changes what a sensible search looks like.

Our profession pages carry what we know about demand for each role, and the live list shows what is open today. Read an advert for what it tells you about the employer: whether it mentions overseas applicants, sponsorship or supervision, and how long it has been open.

We describe demand as we see it from placements and employer conversations. It moves; treat it as a reading, not a forecast.

Section 03 · The stuck case

Registered, applying, and hearing nothing back

This is the conversation we have most often with people who came to us late. It is almost never one thing, and it is almost always one of these seven.

  1. 1

    The CV is shaped for the country you left

    Grades, bandings and acronyms that mean nothing here; the things a local reader looks for buried on page three.

    Rewrite it for the reader, not the writer. The CV & interview guide and the CV checker exist for exactly this.

  2. 2

    The search is one city wide

    The departments everyone applies to are the ones with a queue. The same role a few hours away may have been open for months.

    Widen the radius before you lower the grade. The destination guides show what living there actually looks like.

  3. 3

    The role needs supervision the employer cannot give

    Some pathways arrive with a supervised or restricted period attached. A small team may want you and still be unable to staff the supervision.

    Ask about it before applying, and look for services large enough to carry it. The registration guide explains what your pathway attaches.

  4. 4

    It is the budget, not you

    Public recruitment moves with money. A vacancy can exist, be advertised, and then sit unfilled because the service cannot hire this quarter.

    Nothing to fix. Keep the application warm, ask when it is likely to move, and look sideways in the meantime.

  5. 5

    The interview went differently from how you thought

    Panels here weigh how you work with people at least as much as what you know, and a confident answer in one culture reads as a warning sign in another.

    Am I ready to apply? lets you practise the questions; the interview guide explains what the panel is listening for.

  6. 6

    The visa timing is the unspoken question

    Most of the clinicians we place arrive on an employer-sponsored visa, so the employer is weighing whether to sponsor and how long you will take to arrive. Say plainly where you are with registration and any skills assessment, and what your realistic start date is.

    The visa guide sets out the sequence so you can describe yours in one sentence.

  7. 7

    The experience mix is not what the advert wants

    Deep in one subspecialty when the department wants breadth, or the reverse. Common, and rarely said out loud in a rejection.

    Ask for the reason; most employers will give one if asked plainly. Then decide whether to broaden or to hold out for the right shape of role.

Section 04 · What we do

When we have no role for you

A recruitment agency with nothing to place you into usually goes quiet. We would rather tell you what we can see.

If you send us your CV and where you are with registration, we will tell you honestly how it reads against this market: what is likely to land, what is not, and why. We will say which parts of the country and which kinds of employer are worth your time this year, and which are not. Where we can introduce you, we will; where we cannot, we will say where else to look. And we keep you on file, so the day a role appears that fits, you hear first.

What we will not do is promise you a role, dress up a bad fit as a good one, or tell you a market is warmer than it is. Long-term careers are the whole point; a placement that does not last helps nobody, least of all you.

Two ways in: register your interest with your CV and registration status, or ask us a question first. Either reaches a person.
