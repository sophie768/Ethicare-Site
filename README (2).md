# Brisbane photography — audit

## Added 23 Aug 2026

- `brisbane-cbd-street-buses-dusk.jpg` — two Brisbane City Council buses (routes 381 and P142) in
  the CBD bus lane at dusk, towers either side, commuters on the footpath. Brisbane, confirmed by
  the liveried buses and route numbers. Portrait crop.
  The library's other Brisbane files are all river-and-bridge views; this is the first street-level,
  everyday, getting-to-work shot — which is what the transport chapter actually needs.

- `brisbane-st-stephens-cathedral.jpg` — St Stephen's Cathedral, Gothic Revival sandstone with
  twin spires, hemmed in by CBD towers on an overcast day. Brisbane, confirmed twice over: the
  building itself, and a pink "BRISBANE" banner on a pole at frame left. Portrait crop.

- `brisbane-plaza-bronze-figure.jpg` — a bronze of a seated man on a public bench in a paved
  plaza, figs overhead, playground equipment behind. Filed on Sophie's attribution. **No
  independent marker in frame** — no signage, no identifiable building — so the attribution rests on
  her word rather than on the photograph, which is fine, but it means the caption should not name
  the plaza or the artwork. Nothing in the frame contradicts Brisbane.

## Not taken in

**A carved sandstone tiki-style garden statue** (offered as Brisbane, 23 Aug 2026) was not added.
Two reasons, and neither is about the photograph's quality:

1. It carries no information about Brisbane. A garden ornament against tropical foliage could be a
   courtyard anywhere on earth; a reader learns nothing about the city from it.
2. It is a faux-Polynesian/Balinese carving, which sits awkwardly in an Australian city guide —
   decorative at best, and culturally muddled next to the genuine Aboriginal rock art we now use in
   the Darwin guide.

Say the word and it goes in, but on the "every element earns its place" test it does not earn one.
