Welcome to Nelson Tasman

Kia ora, and a warm welcome to Aotearoa

Moving to the other side of the world is a big step — and choosing Nelson Tasman means arriving in the sunny, easygoing corner at the very top of the South Island. It’s a place people describe with a sigh of relief: golden beaches, three national parks, and a creative, down-to-earth community wrapped around a working harbour city.

You won’t be doing this alone. New Zealanders are known for their warmth and manaakitanga — hospitality and care for others — and the top of the south is a genuinely welcoming place to land. Nelson city (Whakatū) is compact and walkable, with a base hospital right in town, while Richmond, Motueka and the bays give you room to breathe.

This guide is written to make your move smooth and unhurried — from the health system, IRD numbers and KiwiSaver to the suburbs, the school zones and where to spend your first free weekend. Think of it as the conversation we’d have over a flat white, in your pocket for whenever you need it.

Nau mai, haere mai ki Aotearoa — welcome to New Zealand. We can’t wait to see you settle in.

Section one · Overview

Sunshine, sea and three national parks

Whakatū — Nelson — sits at the head of Tasman Bay, sheltered by ranges and ringed by beaches, orchards and vineyards. With Nelson city, Richmond and a string of coastal towns, the region pairs an easy small-city life with some of the best outdoors in New Zealand.

Overview · Culture

A creative city with heart

Nelson is one of New Zealand’s oldest cities, and its compact centre still wears its history — timber villas, the landmark Christ Church Cathedral on its hill, and a walkable grid of galleries, cafés and craft studios. It’s long been an artists’ town, the birthplace of the World of WearableArt, and home to the Suter Art Gallery and a famous Saturday market.

Whakatū is the Māori name for Nelson, and the wider top of the south — Te Tauihu — is the rohe of several iwi, whose history runs through the bays and valleys. A strong maker culture keeps the food, art and music scene lively for a city its size, from weekend markets to a busy calendar of festivals.

It all adds up to an unpretentious, welcoming place — easy to find your feet in, and quick to feel like home.

Overview · Transport

Getting around

Nelson Tasman is compact and easy: most trips across the city take ten or fifteen minutes, and Nelson, Richmond and Motueka sit on one easy coastal route. A car makes the beaches and parks simple to reach, and the region’s airport keeps you well connected to the rest of the country.

Driving & cycling

Short, easy commutes and a flat, cycle-friendly city with a growing network of paths. New Zealanders drive on the left, and parking is straightforward.

Buses

The eBus network links Nelson, Stoke, Richmond and Motueka, with a tap-and-ride Bee Card.

Air

Nelson Airport is one of the country’s busiest regional airports, with frequent direct flights to Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch.

Ferry & road

Picton and the Cook Strait ferry to Wellington are about two hours east, with Marlborough and the West Coast within a scenic drive.

Overview · Climate

The country’s sunniest corner

Sheltered by the ranges, Nelson Tasman regularly records some of the highest sunshine hours in New Zealand. Summers are warm, settled and made for the beach; winters are mild by the coast, with crisp, clear days and frosts inland. It’s a four-season climate that leans firmly towards the sun.

Section two · Healthcare

A base hospital serving the top of the south

Nelson is the healthcare hub for the top of the South Island, with public hospital and specialist services delivered by Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora — a broad case-mix and varied clinical work, close to home and close to the beach.

Nelson Hospital

The region’s base hospital, providing acute, surgical, maternity and specialist services for Nelson, Tasman and the wider top of the south within Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora.

A regional referral hub

Serving a large, spread-out catchment, the hospital sees a wide range of presentations — good for building broad clinical experience.

Community & rural

Community, mental health and rural services reach across Tasman, Motueka and Golden Bay, including a hospital and community services in Golden Bay.

A private option

Private hospital and specialist services in the city offer elective procedures and shorter waits alongside the public system.

Healthcare · Everyday care

GPs, pharmacies & urgent care

Registering with a GP & dentist

Once you settle in, enrol with a local GP. Enrolment means subsidised visits — typically around NZ$50–$70, more as a casual patient. Many practices allow online enrolment once you have an address. Dental care is private (roughly NZ$90–$150 for a check-up).

Pharmacies

Pharmacies are found throughout Nelson, Richmond and the towns. Prescriptions are partly subsidised once you’re enrolled with a GP — commonly a small standard charge per item.

Emergency & after-hours
·Emergency Department — for urgent or critical care; in an emergency, dial 111.
·Urgent care clinics — accident & medical clinics offer extended-hours care.
·Healthline 0800 611 116 — free 24/7 advice from registered nurses.
Section three · Banking & Finance

Setting up your finances

Getting your finances in order early makes day-to-day life much easier. Most of this can be started before you arrive.

Go deeperSalary & pay in New Zealand healthcare→

Opening a bank account

The major banks are ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank and Westpac. You’ll usually need your passport, work or resident visa, and proof of a NZ address. Many banks let you start online before arrival, then activate in person — ask for online banking and a debit card at the same time.

Tax — your IRD number

Every worker needs an IRD number from Inland Revenue. Apply online once you have a NZ address and bank account; it usually takes around 7–10 working days. Give the number to payroll as soon as it arrives so the correct tax is applied. Wages are commonly paid fortnightly.

Sophie’s tip

Start your bank account application before you fly. Having an account number ready makes your IRD application and payroll setup far smoother in those hectic first weeks.

KiwiSaver & sending money home

KiwiSaver is a government-supported retirement savings scheme. On a work visa you can usually opt in or out. Contributions are a percentage of your pay (commonly 3%, 4%, 6%, 8% or 10%), with employers contributing at least 3%. You can withdraw your funds if you permanently leave New Zealand.

Sending money overseas: banks can transfer internationally, but specialist services such as Wise or OFX are often cheaper and faster. Always compare the exchange rate and the fees before moving larger amounts.

Section five · Housing & Areas

Finding a place to live

Nelson Tasman offers everything from character villas and hillside homes with bay views in the city to family homes in Richmond and Stoke, and orchard-and-beach living around Motueka. Commutes are short across the region, so wherever you land, work is rarely far away.

Most rentals are advertised online. The main sites are trademe.co.nz/property (NZ’s largest rental marketplace) and realestate.co.nz (nationwide rentals and homes for sale). Because good rentals move quickly here, many people rent for a few months first to get to know the area before committing.

A guide to the areas

Central Nelson & The Wood

Walkable to the hospital, cathedral and cafés, with character villas and a genuine city-centre feel.

The hills & Britannia Heights

Elevated suburbs with sun and views over Tasman Bay, a short drive or cycle from town.

Tāhunanui & Atawhai

Beachside and coastal suburbs with a relaxed, holiday feel and easy access to the city.

Stoke & Richmond

Good-value family suburbs between Nelson and Tasman, well served by schools, shops and parks.

Motueka & Māpua

Orchard towns and an estuary village near the Abel Tasman — relaxed, creative and by the water.

Wakefield & Brightwater

Friendly rural villages south of Richmond — space and community, with an easy commute.

A tip on choosing

Because commutes are short, choose on lifestyle: the city buzz of central Nelson, the sun and views of the hills, or the value and space of Richmond, Stoke or a nearby town. If you have children, check the local school and its zone before you sign.

Housing · Everyday services

Utilities, rates & rubbish

Power & gas

Choose from providers like Genesis, Mercury, Meridian and Contact. Compare rates on Powerswitch (powerswitch.org.nz).

Water & rates

Council rates fund local services; if you rent, rates are usually included in your rent.

Heating & internet

Heat pumps and log burners are common; fibre broadband is widespread. Spark, One NZ and 2degrees are the main providers.

Section six · Shopping & Food

Where to shop

Shopping centres on central Nelson and Richmond, with malls, big-box retail and a superb weekend market culture. A few of the main ones:

Trafalgar & Hardy Streets

The city heart, with shops, cafés and restaurants running up towards the cathedral steps.

Richmond Mall

The region’s main shopping mall, with major stores and food under cover.

Big-box retail

Homeware, hardware and supermarkets cluster around Stoke and Richmond, between the two centres.

Nelson Market

The famous Saturday market on Montgomery Square — produce, crafts and food — is a weekend institution.

Supermarkets & groceries

New Zealand’s supermarkets fall into a few main groups — knowing the difference helps you balance budget and convenience.

Pak’nSaveThe cheapest of the big chains — a no-frills, bulk-buy warehouse format. Best for budget shopping.
WoolworthsMid-range and widespread, with strong online shopping and delivery (formerly Countdown).
New WorldMore upmarket, with a wider fresh and specialty range.
Four Square & FreshChoiceSmaller neighbourhood stores for convenience and top-ups.

Pak’nSave, Woolworths and New World are all in Nelson and Richmond. The region is prime growing country — apples, hops, berries and seafood — so local produce is excellent and cheap at the markets and roadside stalls. Bring your own bags — single-use plastic bags are banned.

A taste of home

Halal & South Asian. Indian, Middle-Eastern and Asian grocers in Nelson and Richmond stock familiar staples, and many supermarket lines are halal-processed — check the label.

Asian. Chinese, Korean, Japanese and South-East Asian supermarkets and eateries serve a growing community.

British & European. The larger supermarkets and specialty importers stock familiar brands from home.

Faith & community · Places of worship

Nelson Tasman has welcoming communities across many faiths — Christian churches of many denominations (Nelson’s cathedral is a city landmark), a mosque and Islamic centre, and Buddhist and other groups. Most list service times online, and many are among the quickest ways to build a support network when you arrive.

Section seven · Recreation & Travel

Beaches, parks and trails

Life here revolves around the outdoors: swim and kayak the golden beaches of the Abel Tasman, tramp in Kahurangi and Nelson Lakes, cycle the Great Taste Trail between cafés and vineyards, and browse the arts, markets and gardens of the city — all within easy reach.

Section eight · Education & Family

Schooling & study

Nelson Tasman offers a full range of early childhood education, schools and tertiary study. The school year runs late January to mid-December, in four terms with two-week breaks in April, July and October.

Go deeperEducation & schools in New Zealand→

Early Childhood (ECE)

Kindergartens, daycare, Montessori, kōhanga reo and home-based care for ages 0–5. Government 20-hours funding applies for ages 3–5.

Primary & intermediate

State, state-integrated and private schools for Years 1–8 across Nelson, Richmond and the towns.

Secondary

Years 9–13, working towards NCEA, the national qualification, with several well-regarded colleges.

Tertiary

The Nelson Marlborough Institute of Technology (NMIT) offers vocational and degree study, with campuses in Nelson and Richmond.

School zones come first

In New Zealand, where you live can determine which state school your children attend. For many families, the school decision leads the choice of where to rent or buy.

In-zone = guaranteed

Children living inside a school’s enrolment zone are guaranteed a place.

Out-of-zone = ballot

You can still apply, but popular schools run a ballot for limited spots.

Check the address

Zones can be specific — confirm the exact address is in-zone.

The order that works best: shortlist schools you like → check each school’s zone map → search for rentals inside that zone → confirm the address is in-zone before you sign.

Nelson Tasman for families

Beyond school and work, Nelson Tasman is one of the easiest places in the country to raise a family — beaches and river swims, playgrounds and bush walks, and much of the best of it free. Short commutes and an outdoor life give families more time together, and a friendly, creative community makes settling in easy. Children often settle fastest through school, sport and local clubs.
