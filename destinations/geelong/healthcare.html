Healthcare in Geelong

Geelong Guide · Chapter 4 of 9

Geelong punches above its population clinically, for one structural reason: its hospital is the only tertiary centre in Victoria based outside Melbourne. Understanding that designation is the difference between reading this as a regional posting and reading it accurately.

The system

A genuine tertiary centre
The region’s principal referral hospital is the only tertiary hospital in Victoria based outside metropolitan Melbourne, and the largest hospital in regional Victoria.
A large catchment
Its primary catchment covers around 350,000 people, extending to roughly 500,000 for some specialist services drawn from further along the south-west coast.
District services behind it
Smaller hospitals and community health services across the Surf Coast, the Otways, Colac and the Bellarine refer inward for specialist and complex care.
A genuine private sector
Private hospitals and specialist imaging providers operate alongside the public system, giving real choice for clinicians and patients alike.
Melbourne is close, not essential
An hour up the freeway if a case needs a Melbourne subspecialty opinion, but the great majority of work is managed locally.
A note on names
  • We don’t name hospitals, trusts or private providers as places of work anywhere on this site — a named employer implies a relationship, and the post and the package get confirmed with you directly.
  • Everything above is described structurally for that reason. If you want to know exactly where a role sits, ask us and we will tell you plainly.

What the tertiary role does to your work

A tertiary centre with a large regional catchment behind it pulls in two directions at once, and both are true of the same job.

What the region gives you
A case mix closer to a metropolitan teaching hospital than a typical regional posting — genuine subspecialty exposure, teaching relationships with Deakin University’s medical and health-science programmes, and a role in the state’s health system that carries real weight.
What it asks of you
Comfort working with a smaller specialist bench than a Melbourne teaching hospital, even while the case complexity is comparable.

Imaging and radiation, specifically

This is where our Australian work is concentrated, so it is the part we can speak to in most detail. As the only tertiary imaging service in regional Victoria, Geelong’s capability sits closer to a metropolitan department than a typical regional posting — a full range of modalities, and a teaching relationship with Deakin University’s health science programmes. For a technologist or radiographer that has a specific consequence: genuine subspecialty exposure is available without a five-million-person city built up around it, and cross-training tends to be actively supported rather than merely permitted.

How pay works here

Registration is national but pay is not. In the public system, salaries are set by the relevant Victorian health system industrial agreement, negotiated between the health service and the union covering your profession — so the same role can sit on a different scale in another state. In the private sector, pay is negotiated individually and is often higher, particularly where a service is hard to staff.

We do not publish figures we have not verified against the agreement that actually governs the role. For the indicative bands and how the frameworks work, see Salary in Australia, explained honestly.

What to ask at interview

  • How does the tertiary designation affect my day-to-day caseload?
  • Which cases, if any, go to a Melbourne subspecialty service?
  • How many colleagues sit alongside me in my specialty specifically?
  • What is the on-call pattern, and how often is it genuinely called upon?
  • Which modalities or sub-specialties would I be expected to cover?
  • What CPD time and funding is provided, including travel to Melbourne?
  • Is there a teaching component through Deakin, and is it supported?
The short version

Geelong is Victoria’s only tertiary hospital outside Melbourne, which is why the clinical case mix is wider than the population suggests. The trade is a smaller specialist bench than a Melbourne teaching hospital — and for imaging specifically, a full-modality service most regional cities cannot offer.
