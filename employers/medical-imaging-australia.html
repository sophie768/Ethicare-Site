Your next radiographer might not live in Australia yet.

Radiographers, sonographers, radiation therapists and nuclear medicine technologists, found internationally, checked against their registration route before you meet them, and supported through the move so that they stay.

Two marketsAustralia and New Zealand, and nowhere else

Australia is the newer of our two markets, and medical imaging is where we work in it. That is deliberate: a small number of professions we understand properly, rather than national coverage of everything.

Why the role is still open

The hardest imaging roles to fill are hard for the same few reasons

Public services and private practices recruit from the same pool. The roles that stay open longest tend to be in the modalities that take longest to train for — MRI, sonography, mammography — and in places a long way from a capital city. Those are the roles where an internationally qualified clinician, properly prepared, makes the difference.

The candidates exist

The United Kingdom, Ireland and South Africa train imaging professionals whose qualifications have established routes through Ahpra and ASAR. What holds many of them back is not knowing whether their registration would work — the first thing we answer.

Registration is the real timeline

Not the notice period, not the visa. Three of the four professions go through the Medical Radiation Practice Board; sonographers go through ASAR. Knowing which stage a candidate is at is the difference between a start date and a hope.

Regional is a different sale, not a harder one

A role in Bunbury or Bundaberg does not sell itself from a job description. It sells when someone shows a household the actual place — the school, the commute, the partner’s work — and helps them decide it fits.

How it works

Three things you get with every introduction

They mirror the questions a clinician asks before moving, pointed the other way. They are also why the people we introduce arrive, and stay.

Registration status, stated plainly

  • Registered — holds current Ahpra registration or ASAR accreditation.
  • Application underway — lodged with the board, with the stage and the likely date.
  • Pathway assessed — we have mapped the route and what it will take; nothing lodged yet.
  • Not yet assessed — and we say so, rather than let optimism stand in for a date.

Ethicare does not grant registration and does not guarantee it. The regulator does that. What we do is know where each person stands, and tell you.

Relocation readiness, checked before you invest

  • Does the household support the move? A partner’s work, children’s schooling and the timeline, asked in the first conversation rather than after the offer.
  • Do they understand the location? Not “would you relocate regionally” but the actual town, shown to them.
  • Is the timing realistic? Registration, visa and notice, put in order, so a start date is one you can plan a roster around.

And we stay after arrival, because that is where placements are won or lost

Retention is not only an HR problem. It is very often a relocation problem — the fortnight when a family wonders whether they got it right, the school place that fell through, the partner who has not found work. So the first year is where we stay: registration and visa completion, the move itself, then contact through settling in. For clinicians we place it is included; for your direct international hires it is available separately.

Not one labour market

Imaging is recruited by modality, and the supply, the route in and the likely timeline differ for each. This is what we recruit into Australia, and the thing worth knowing about each.

Ahpra Medical Radiation Practice Board

  • General radiography The widest pool, and the quickest to place
  • CT & MRI Cross-sectional experience transfers well
  • Mammography & screening Screening accreditation is a further step on top of registration — plan for it
  • Interventional & theatre Smaller pool, usually with on-call attached
  • Radiation therapy Its own division; designated centres only
  • Nuclear medicine & PET Ahpra plus an ANZSNM skills assessment, plus state radiation licensing

ASAR Not a registered profession

  • Sonography General, obstetric, vascular and cardiac

Sonographers are accredited by ASAR after ASMIRT assesses the qualification, not registered by Ahpra. The evidence, the timeline and the failure points are all different, and it is the modality where an introduction made before the route is checked wastes the most time — yours and theirs.

Consultant radiologists are doctors with a different regulator again. We recruit them into Australia too; ask.

Who’s out there

Who we are talking to now

Ask us. We will tell you who we are already in conversation with in your modality and your state, and exactly where each person stands on registration — the one fact a CV database cannot give you.

We do not publish candidate profiles here yet, and when we do they will be anonymised, current and shared with the clinician’s consent — a mammographer in Sydney, Ahpra-registered, fourteen years’ experience, is useful to you only if she is real and still available. Until that is running, it works the reliable way: tell us the role and the region, and a person answers.

What happens next

From the brief to the first year

1 · The brief

The modality, the rooms, the case mix, the roster and the place. A person reads it and comes back within a working day with a clear read on whether we can help, and roughly how long it would take.

2 · The search

Targeted rather than volume. Registration route checked and household questions asked before a name reaches you.

3 · The introduction

Each candidate arrives with a registration status, a realistic start window and the regions they would move to. One named introduction at a time.

4 · The move, and after

Registration and visa completion, the relocation, and contact through the first year — so the role you filled stays filled.
