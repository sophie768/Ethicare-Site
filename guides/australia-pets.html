The pet relocation guide

Moving with pets

There’s one member of the family who isn’t mentioned in the employment contract. The dog. Or the cat. And for a great many families, leaving them behind isn’t an option.

The short version

Absolutely possible. Not remotely last-minute.

Moving pets to Australia works from many countries, and thousands of families do it every year. But it requires considerably more planning than booking an extra seat on the flight.

Australia operates strict biosecurity requirements designed to protect their unique environments and animal health. That means your pet’s move needs to be planned alongside your own, not a few weeks before departure.

For most owners, a pet isn’t cargo to be transported after the move. They’re part of who’s moving.

Section 01 · Timing

Start planning early

This is the single most important thing on the page. The process your pet follows depends on the country you are moving from, not the fact that you’re heading to Australia.

Requirements can include microchipping, vaccinations, rabies testing, parasite treatments, veterinary examinations, official documentation, import permits and quarantine. And the order matters: where a test or treatment has to happen within a specified window, getting the sequence wrong can delay the whole move.

When to start

As soon as Australia becomes a serious possibility, not when the removal boxes are packed. You don’t need a signed contract to start researching your pet’s requirements, and starting early is the cheapest thing you can do.

Section 02 · The rules

What Australia requires

Australia sets requirements according to the animal-health and biosecurity risk of your country of export, so two families moving the same breed from different countries can face different processes.

Australia

Among the strictest in the world

Requirements depend on the country your pet is travelling from and the type of animal. Dogs and cats from approved countries may need to complete a series of veterinary and biosecurity steps before departure.

  • Identification by microchip
  • Rabies vaccination and testing
  • Parasite treatments
  • Veterinary certification
  • An import permit

Some animals will also need to spend time in post-entry quarantine after arriving. There are additional restrictions affecting certain breeds and circumstances, so don’t assume that because somebody else’s dog moved successfully, yours follows the same route. Use the Australian Government’s current requirements for your country of export as your starting point.

One rule for both

Regulations change. A checklist somebody used a few years ago, or even relatively recently, should never replace current government guidance. Check the source, not the forum post.

Section 03 · Quarantine

Will my pet have to quarantine?

Possibly. It depends on the destination, the animal and the country of origin — Australia categorises countries by animal-health and biosecurity risk, and that determines the process.

If quarantine is required, availability becomes part of your relocation timeline. Facilities book up, and that’s one of the clearest reasons pet travel shouldn’t be left until the end of the process.

Three dates, not one

Your employment start date, your family’s flights and your pet’s travel date may not be the same. Plan them as three linked decisions rather than assuming everyone arrives together.

Section 04 · Sequencing

Your pet doesn’t necessarily have to travel with you

This can feel strange at first, particularly if your pet has always been part of family life. But it is often the most practical option.

A clinician might travel first, begin work, move out of temporary accommodation and secure a pet-friendly long-term rental before their partner, children and pets follow. Other families move together and arrange for the pet to arrive separately.

There isn’t one correct way. Think about what makes the transition easiest for both your family and your animal, and remember that arriving into a settled home is a much gentler landing for a dog than arriving into a serviced apartment and a house-hunt.

Section 05 · Somewhere to live

Finding a pet-friendly rental

Getting your pet into the country is only half the problem. You also need somewhere to live that will have them.

Check the rules around pets before assuming a property is suitable, and pay particular attention to temporary accommodation. A serviced apartment or short-term let that works perfectly for a family may not accept a dog or cat. If your pet is arriving after you, use that window to find suitable long-term accommodation.

Then think beyond whether the landlord permits pets:

  • Is there enough space?
  • Is there a secure garden, if you have a dog?
  • Are there good places nearby for walking?
  • How long will your pet be alone while you’re working?

The right home for your family needs to work for them too.

Section 06 · Cost

How much does it cost?

Pet relocation can be expensive. The total depends on where you’re travelling from, your destination, the size and type of animal, veterinary requirements, transport arrangements and whether quarantine is required.

Potential expenses include veterinary appointments, vaccinations, blood tests, official certification, permits, airline transport, travel crates, pet relocation companies and quarantine. For a large dog or multiple animals, the total can become a significant part of the family’s relocation budget.

Get quotes early

It is far easier to plan for this six months out than to discover it once you’ve committed most of your savings to flights, accommodation and setting up the new home. Our relocation cost guide shows where this sits against everything else you’ll be paying for.

Section 07 · The journey

What is the flight actually like for them?

Understandably one of the biggest worries for owners, and international pet travel is very different from taking an animal on a short domestic journey.

The journey

Ask for it stage by stage

Depending on the route and airline arrangements, your pet may travel in a specially designed crate in an appropriate part of the aircraft rather than alongside you in the cabin. There may also be stopovers or transfers.

A reputable pet transport specialist or airline should be able to explain the journey clearly, including where your animal will be during each stage. If your pet is elderly or has a medical condition, discuss the proposed route with your vet well in advance.

Medication & sedation

Not your decision to make alone

If you’re worried about how your pet will cope, speak to your vet and the company responsible for transporting them.

Sedation can create additional risks during air travel and may not be permitted or recommended in some circumstances. The right approach depends on the individual animal and the requirements of the carrier, so ask both, and follow the advice.

Should you use a pet relocation company?

You don’t have to manage all of this alone. Specialists can coordinate veterinary timelines, documentation, travel crates, flights and quarantine arrangements. At a cost. Some families value having someone run a complicated process while they’re simultaneously dealing with visas, registration, jobs, schools and their own move; others prefer to manage it themselves. If you do use one, establish exactly what the quote includes and what you’ll still need to arrange separately.

Section 08 · Check before you spend

Older pets, restricted breeds & other animals

Three situations where the answer needs checking before you spend any money on the process.

Older pets

A different conversation

Moving a healthy three-year-old dog and moving a fourteen-year-old dog are very different decisions. For an older animal, the length of the journey, underlying conditions and ability to cope with transport all need careful thought.

That doesn’t automatically mean an older pet can’t relocate. It means the decision belongs with your vet, rather than resting on whether the paperwork permits it.

Breeds & species

Some animals can’t come

Australia restricts the importation of certain dog breeds or types, and there can be restrictions affecting hybrid animals. If there’s any possibility your dog falls within a restricted category, check the official rules first, don’t rely on appearance, breeder descriptions or informal advice.

For pets other than dogs and cats, birds, rabbits, reptiles, don’t assume the dog-and-cat process applies. Check whether your animal can legally be imported before making any plans. For some species and countries of origin, it may not be possible at all.

Section 09 · Settling in

The first few weeks after arrival

The journey is only one part of your pet’s transition. Everything familiar has changed. Their home has gone, the smells are different, the routine has changed, and the family is stressed and surrounded by boxes.

Establish a predictable routine as quickly as you can. Find a local vet, update identification details where required, and learn the local rules for registration, microchipping and dog control.

Then do the enjoyable part. Find the walks. Find the parks. Find the beach.

For a lot of families, exploring the new neighbourhood with the dog is the first thing that makes the place feel like home.

Section 10 · The package

Could your employer help?

Pet relocation isn’t normally a standard employment benefit, but that isn’t quite the end of the question.

If your employer is providing a general relocation allowance rather than paying only specified expenses, you may have some flexibility over how that money is used. Never assume pet transport is covered. Ask what the relocation contribution can and cannot be used for before making any bookings.

Our offer guide covers how to raise this well, and relocation support is often more negotiable than the salary itself.

Useful contacts

Specialists & quarantine facilities

You don’t have to organise an international pet move alone. Specialist relocation companies can coordinate veterinary requirements, documentation, flights, travel crates and quarantine arrangements. For candidates relocating from the UK, established providers include:

PetAir UK

A vet-led international pet relocation company providing services to Australia and New Zealand.

Airpets International

Heathrow-based international pet transport specialist with veterinary and animal-handling facilities.

PBS Pet Travel

UK pet relocation company arranging international transport for dogs, cats and other animals.

Quarantine in Australia

One government facility

Cats and dogs requiring post-entry quarantine in Australia are accommodated at the Australian Government’s Post Entry Quarantine Facility at Mickleham, near Melbourne.

Requirements for importing animals depend on your country of origin and can change, always check the latest Australian Government or New Zealand Ministry for Primary Industries guidance before making arrangements. Ethicare does not receive commission from any of the providers listed here. We’d recommend obtaining several quotes and confirming current government requirements directly before you book anything.

Common questions

The questions we’re asked about moving with pets

How far in advance should I start?

As soon as the move becomes a serious possibility. Several of the veterinary steps have to happen in a particular order and within specified windows, and quarantine facilities have finite availability, so the timeline is set by regulation rather than by how organised you are. Starting early costs nothing and prevents the most common delays.

Will my dog definitely have to go into quarantine?

Not necessarily, it depends on the destination, the animal and your country of origin, since Australia categorises countries by biosecurity risk. Where quarantine does apply, treat facility availability as part of your relocation timeline rather than an afterthought.

Can my pet travel on the same flight as me?

Sometimes, but don’t assume it. Depending on the route and airline, your pet may travel in a specially designed crate in an appropriate part of the aircraft rather than in the cabin, and there may be stopovers or transfers. A reputable specialist or the airline should walk you through where your animal will be at each stage.

Should my pet be sedated for the journey?

That’s a decision for your vet and the transport company, not something to arrange yourself. Sedation can carry additional risks in air travel and may not be permitted or recommended depending on the animal and the carrier’s requirements.

My dog is a restricted breed, is that the end of it?

Check the official rules before assuming either way, and before spending anything. Australia restricts certain breeds or types, and there can be restrictions on hybrid animals. Don’t rely on appearance, breeder descriptions or informal advice. This is one to confirm at the source.

What about a rabbit, bird or reptile?

Considerably more complicated, and for some species and countries of origin importing isn’t possible. The dog-and-cat process doesn’t transfer. Confirm whether your animal can legally be imported before making any relocation plans around it.

Will an employer pay for pet relocation?

It isn’t a standard benefit, so never assume it. But if the employer offers a general relocation allowance rather than reimbursing only specified expenses, you may have flexibility over how it’s used. Ask what the contribution can and can’t cover before you book.

No pressure, just a conversation

Tell us about them in the first conversation

Where you’ll live, where the children will go to school, when the family should travel, and how and when your pets make the journey. These aren’t small details to sort out once the job is secured. They’re part of whether the relocation works for everybody.
