Food, Pako & the everyday

Geelong Guide · Chapter 8 of 9

Geelong chapters

The test of a place is not its best restaurant. It is what an ordinary Wednesday feels like, and whether Saturday morning has a shape. Geelong does well on both, and one street does much of the work.

Pako is the centre of gravity

Pakington Street in Newtown — “Pako” to everyone who lives here — is the café and shopping strip that gives the inner suburbs their social life. It is not a tourist strip; it is where people actually spend a Saturday morning, and the standard of coffee and casual dining is genuinely high for a city this size.

Down at the water, the waterfront and Little Malop Street precincts carry most of the dinner-out trade, with views across Corio Bay thrown in. Between the two, a Saturday farmers’ market rotates through the region and is worth building a routine around.

Eating out, honestly assessed

What is genuinely good
Café culture, casual dining on Pako and the waterfront, and a growing wine-region influence on the local food scene. For a city of 270,000 the standard is well above what the population would suggest.
What is thinner
The sheer range Melbourne carries. A specific niche cuisine may still mean an hour’s drive, though the gap has narrowed noticeably in the last decade.
Where the wine comes from
The Bellarine Peninsula and the Moorabool Valley, both twenty to thirty minutes out — cool-climate pinot noir and chardonnay country, local by any reasonable definition.
Where to find groceries from home
The main supermarkets carry more than they used to, and Geelong’s size supports a genuine range of specialist grocers. For anything unusual, Melbourne is an hour away.

A week, once the novelty has worn off

Weekdays
A short commute and light left in the evening
The thing people mention most is arriving home early enough for a walk along the waterfront before dinner.
Saturday morning
Pako, or the market
Coffee and errands on Pakington Street, or the rotating farmers’ market, depending on the week.
Saturday afternoon
The water, or the wine
Eastern Beach or the Bellarine coast if the wind allows it, a Moorabool Valley cellar door if it does not.
Sunday
The Great Ocean Road, or nothing at all
Torquay and beyond for a proper day out, or the Barwon River trails if you would rather stay close to home.

Finding your people

Geelong is large enough to have genuine variety and small enough that joining something — a football club, a surf lifesaving club, a Deakin-adjacent community group — is a fast route in. Clinicians consistently tell us the professional community here feels close for a tertiary centre, partly because the specialist bench is smaller than Melbourne’s.

On the wider question of community, faith and belonging — and being honest that a regional city carries smaller communities than a capital does — see Community & belonging in Australia.

The short version

A genuinely good café strip, a wine region on the doorstep, and eating out that punches above the population. The real change is the shape of the week — a short commute buys back the evening, and Pako gives Saturday morning somewhere to be.
