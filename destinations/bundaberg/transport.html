The honest summary: you will need a car, you will use it constantly rather than occasionally, and Brisbane is a real trip rather than a quick hop, by road or by air.

Inside the city

Most trips across Bundaberg take ten to fifteen minutes. A local bus network exists but is limited, and it is not built for shift work — most clinicians drive. Parking is generally easy and often free, a real change if you are arriving from a capital city.

Getting to Brisbane

By road
About four hours down the Bruce Highway. Straightforward, and a normal weekend trip if you plan around it.
By air
The regional airport runs direct flights to Brisbane in around seventy minutes — the quickest option if time matters more than cost.
By rail
Bundaberg sits on the Brisbane–Cairns line, with regional and tilt-train services calling. Slower than flying, and a genuine option if you would rather not drive.
By coach
Regional coach services run the highway corridor and are the fallback if you are without a car.

The car question, answered plainly

Yes, essential. Bargara, Elliott Heads, Mon Repos and the cane-country day trips are not on a transit route, and shift work does not fit a limited bus timetable. If you are a two-adult household, plan for two cars; some manage on one for the first year if one partner is not commuting.

Licence conversion, importing a vehicle, insurance and registration all work the same way across Australia and are covered once in Living in Australia. Nothing about it is specific to Bundaberg.

First-month practicalities
  • Sort a car early — the regional used market is thinner than a capital’s, so give yourself options.
  • Check whether your accommodation has off-street parking. Most does; it is worth confirming.
  • If you are commuting from the coast, drive the route at your actual start time once before you commit.
  • Book flights or the train early around school holidays, when Brisbane demand from the region rises.
The short version

A car is essential inside the city and outside it. Fifteen minutes to anything local, four hours or a short flight to Brisbane — and the car earns its keep on the coast and the cane-country day trips, not the commute.
