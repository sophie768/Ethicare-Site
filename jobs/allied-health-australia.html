The professions

Registered and self-regulated, side by side

Physiotherapy, occupational therapy and podiatry register through national boards under Ahpra. Speech pathology, dietetics and exercise physiology are self-regulated — professional associations assess qualifications, and employers rely on that.

Worth knowing before you search

The NDIS is why this family’s job market looks different here. The national disability scheme funds allied health directly and at scale — it built a large community and private sector almost overnight, and it is where much of the demand for OTs, speech pathologists and physiotherapists now sits.

Scheme-funded work is a genuinely different week from hospital work: mobile, goal-based, and shaped by plans and funding rules. Ask which funder sits behind any role — it changes the job more than the title does.

The structural fact

Registered or self-regulated — and what that changes for you

It does not change whether you can work here. It changes who decides you are eligible, what you can rely on before you arrive, and what employers will ask to see.

Ahpra professions

A board decides

Physiotherapy, OT and podiatry have national boards: published criteria, a formal assessment, and a registration that is yours before any employer enters the picture.

Self-regulated professions

The profession decides

Speech pathology and dietetics have no register — the professional association assesses your qualification, and employers set their own requirements on top. Unregistered does not mean unrecruited; it means the gate is in a different place.

No register does not mean no standards — it means a different gatekeeper.

What it is like to practise here more broadly — the funders, the states, cultural safety and what feels different — is explained once, properly, in practising in Australia.

Where the work happens

Where might that work take you?

Hospitals are one employer among several. Australian allied health runs across state health services, a very large private sector, the NDIS, aged care and schools.

Across the family

What applies across allied health

The things that are the same whichever of these professions you hold.

Who regulates this work?+

It depends on the profession — and that is the point of this page. Physiotherapy, occupational therapy and podiatry register through national boards under Ahpra. Speech pathology and dietetics are self-regulated: Speech Pathology Australia and Dietitians Australia assess qualifications, including for migration, and employers rely on that assessment.

Registration is the first gate, and it runs on its own timeline. How registration works in Australia · Check your pathway

What does the pay look like?+

Registration is national; pay is not. Public terms are set state by state through enterprise agreements, so the same job pays differently in Perth and in Hobart — and comparing two offers means comparing two agreements. Private and scheme-funded work varies again.

For any live vacancy the package is confirmed with you directly — we don’t quote figures against named roles.

Which visa would I need?+

Most clinicians move on an employer-sponsored work visa, and several professions in this family sit on the skilled occupation lists, which shapes the route to permanent residence.

The visa can usually run in parallel with registration rather than after it. Visas and residence

What about the rest of the move?+

Schools, housing, healthcare, banking, shipping and the first few weeks are the same for every profession, so we explain them once rather than on each page.
