Stage 04 · Can I work there? · Medical Radiation Practice Board of Australia

Registering as a medical radiation practitioner

For internationally qualified diagnostic radiographers, radiation therapists and nuclear medicine technologists. Two processes run here that sound alike and are not — professional registration, and a migration skills assessment. You may need one, the other, or both, and getting them in the right order saves months. The checklist is built into the page, so nothing you tick goes out of date.

At a glance

Registration and migration are separate

The Board registers you to practise. ASMIRT assesses skills for certain visas. A positive migration assessment does not grant registration, and registration does not by itself satisfy migration.

Three divisions, one Board

Diagnostic radiographer, radiation therapist, nuclear medicine technologist — distinct registrations. You register in the division your qualification supports.

Check the fast lane first

Qualifications accredited by the UK’s HCPC or Ireland’s CORU within the Board’s date ranges are treated as substantially equivalent — generally no portfolio assessment and no exam.

Registration is national; radiation licensing is not

Each state or territory licenses the use of radiation separately. You will usually need both, and employers normally guide you through the local one.

Section 01 · The one that matters most

Two separate processes

International candidates routinely conflate two completely separate assessments. They are run by different bodies, for different reasons, and the outcomes can differ.

1 · Lets you practise

Professional registration — Ahpra and the MRPBA

The Board assesses whether you meet the registration standards required to practise. Without MRPBA registration you cannot legally work in Australia as a medical radiation practitioner — whatever your visa says. It considers your formal qualifications only.

2 · For certain visas

Migration skills assessment — ASMIRT

Required for some skilled-migration visa pathways and carried out by an approved assessing authority. Its purpose is immigration, not registration. It can weigh your work experience as well as your qualifications — which is why the two outcomes can differ.

The key point.

A positive migration skills assessment does not grant registration, and registration does not by itself satisfy migration requirements. Some people are approved for skilled migration and still have to meet the Board’s registration requirements.

Section 02

The Board, and what it does

A protected profession

The MRPBA sits within the National Registration and Accreditation Scheme, administered by Ahpra. You must be registered to use the title and to work in the role.

Three divisions, registered separately

Diagnostic radiographer, radiation therapist and nuclear medicine technologist — distinct registrations with distinct education pathways. You register in the division your qualification supports.

Registration is national. Radiation licensing is not.

MRPBA registration applies Australia-wide. Using radiation is licensed separately by each state or territory, so you will usually also need a radiation-use licence where you work. Employers normally guide you through the local one.

Is my qualification recognised?→Submit to Ahpra / MRPBA→Assessment — exam if required→Registration granted→State radiation licence→Practise

Section 03

Is my qualification recognised?

Your pathway depends first on whether the Board already recognises your qualification. For some applicants it does, and that is the quickest route of all. For everyone else, the Board assesses your qualification portfolio and reaches one of three defined outcomes.

The fast lane — comparable regulators.

If your qualification was accredited by a regulator the Board recognises as comparable for your division, within its published date ranges, it is treated as substantially equivalent and you are qualified for general registration — generally without a portfolio assessment or the National exam. This currently includes qualifications approved by the UK’s HCPC and Ireland’s CORU within the Board’s date ranges, plus a list of specific recognised qualifications by institution and award year. The match has to be exact — division, institution, award title and year. Check your own position on the Board’s “Is my qualification recognised?” page before assuming either way.

If it isn’t already recognised — the three outcomes

s.53(b) National Law

Substantially equivalent, or based on similar competencies

Meets the full set of criteria. The most direct route toward general registration.

s.53(c) National Law

Relevant to the profession

Meets the threshold criteria but not full equivalence. Registration is still possible, typically with an additional step such as the National examination.

Threshold not met

Neither equivalent nor relevant

The minimum threshold criteria are not met, so the qualification does not currently support registration in the division.

Recognition is always subject to the Board’s assessment of your individual evidence, and the recognised lists and date ranges are reviewed periodically — confirm your position against the Board’s current page.

Section 04

How the Board assesses a qualification

Three published criteria. Knowing them lets you assemble evidence that answers them directly rather than hoping the transcript speaks for itself.

  1. 1

    Professional registration

    Your qualification was recognised by a registration or licensing body for the profession where you trained, and you met any pre-registration assessment there — or, where there is no such body, it enabled you to practise.

  2. 2

    Quality assurance and accreditation

    Your programme was externally accredited against published education standards, including contemporary evidence-based practice and work-integrated clinical learning.

  3. 3

    Educational level

    The academic level is comparable, at minimum, to an Australian bachelor’s degree — AQF level 7.

The core topics the Board looks for

Across all divisions: anatomy, physiology and pathology; principles of patient care; clear communication; medical radiation physics; safe radiation practice; CT imaging; and the safe use of medicines including contrast media. Then the division’s own.

Diagnostic radiographer

Projection radiography and fluoroscopy

The examinations the Board expects a diagnostic radiography or medical imaging degree to have covered.

Radiation therapist

Planning, delivery and verification

Radiation therapy planning, treatment delivery and treatment verification.

Nuclear medicine technologist

Radiopharmaceuticals, SPECT and PET

Radiopharmaceuticals and SPECT and PET imaging.

In our experience.

It is easy to assume registration is just paperwork. It isn’t — it is a real assessment of whether your training meets the Australian standard. The part you control most is the part that matters most: complete, well-organised documents, sent early.

Section 05

The National MRP examination

It exists to confirm that practitioners whose qualifications are not assessed as substantially equivalent still meet the Australian standard before registration. Not everyone sits it.

Applicants already recognised as substantially equivalent — including those accredited by a comparable regulator within the date ranges — generally are not required to. Where a qualification is assessed as “relevant to the profession” rather than fully equivalent, the exam is commonly required. Treat it as a possible step confirmed by your assessment, not an automatic one.

Where required it is a written, knowledge-based assessment: a component common across the divisions, a component specific to yours, and Australian-context material — radiation safety legislation, the professional capabilities, culturally safe practice — alongside clinical knowledge you will already recognise.

Confirm the current format.

Structure, components and pass requirements are set by the Board and reviewed periodically. Check its National exam pages before you prepare — no format is reproduced here for exactly that reason.

Section 06

English language

All applicants must satisfy the MRPBA’s English Language Skills Registration Standard.

It sets out the recognised ways to demonstrate English — an approved test result, or one of several exemption pathways, for example where your education was conducted and assessed in English in a recognised country.

No scores or exemption criteria are reproduced here, deliberately.

The Board reviews the standard periodically and the accepted tests and pathways change — the minimum scores did in April 2026. Read the current standard before you book any test, and if you need one, time it so the result is still valid when your application reaches the Board.

Section 08

The employer-sponsored (482) pathway

Most people we support relocate under an employer-sponsored visa. On that pathway MRPBA registration is the priority — it is what allows you to practise once you arrive.

Your employer sponsors the role; the Board registers the practitioner. Both have to happen, and they are separate workstreams. An ASMIRT migration skills assessment is not generally required simply to obtain registration on a 482. ASMIRT’s assessment is a migration step tied to particular visa pathways — most often the points-tested skilled-migration visas, 189, 190 and 491.

That is not a blanket “ASMIRT is never required.” Additional migration assessments may be needed if you later pursue permanent skilled migration or a different visa. Confirm what applies to you with your employer or a registered migration adviser — that is their call, not ours, and not the Board’s.

Before you apply

Five things, in this order

Check recognition — is your qualification already on the Board’s lists? Understand your pathway — recognised, portfolio assessment, and whether the exam is likely. Gather your documents. Decide your visa route — employer-sponsored 482 or skilled migration, which decides whether ASMIRT applies. Then talk to us: we confirm the pathway, help secure the role and sponsorship, and review your evidence before you submit.

Section 09

Applying, and what happens next

  1. 1

    Determine your eligibility

    Use the Board’s “Is my qualification recognised?” pathway to understand your likely route.

  2. 2

    Prepare your documents

    Identity, qualification, transcript, clinical evidence, registration history, good standing, CV, English.

  3. 3

    Submit to Ahpra and the MRPBA

    Apply for registration in your division, with the non-refundable fee.

  4. 4

    Qualification and English assessment

    The Board assesses your qualification; you satisfy the English standard if it applies to you.

  5. 5

    National examination, if required

    Where your assessment outcome requires it, you sit and pass the National exam.

  6. 6

    Registration granted

    Once registered, and any radiation-use licence is in place, you are cleared to practise.

  7. 7

    Secure your role and start

    Many Australian employers expect registration to be in place first. With it confirmed, you are ready.

How long it takes

It varies

With the completeness of your application, document verification and your circumstances. Start the slowest items — Certificates of Good Standing, verification — early.

Fees

Confirm before you budget

Ahpra and the Board set registration fees; separate fees apply to the National exam and to state radiation licences. All are reviewed periodically, so none are printed here.

Timing

Don’t make irreversible moves too early

Don’t resign or relocate until your registration eligibility has been assessed and confirmed. Sponsored candidates should coordinate registration and visa timelines with their employer.

Where people get stuck

Almost every avoidable delay traces back to one of these

Confusing registration with migration

Assuming ASMIRT is the first step. For practice, MRPBA registration is the one you need.

Assuming ASMIRT is always required

On a 482 it generally is not, for registration — but it can be for other visa pathways. Confirm yours.

Thin qualification evidence

A missing transcript or clinical-training detail, so the Board cannot judge equivalence.

Assuming you must sit the exam

Whether the National exam applies depends on your assessment outcome. Do not assume either way.

English evidence too early, or the wrong test

Check the current registration standard before booking, and time it so it stays valid.

Irreversible decisions too soon

Resigning or relocating before your registration eligibility is confirmed.

The fix for all of these

Understand the two processes, get your qualification assessed early, prepare complete documents, and confirm your visa route with your employer.

Section 11

The questions we are asked most

Do I need an ASMIRT assessment to register?

Not generally. The MRPBA assesses your qualification for registration; ASMIRT conducts skills assessments for migration purposes tied to certain visa pathways. On an employer-sponsored 482 an ASMIRT assessment is not generally required just to register, but it may be needed for other migration routes.

So who actually assesses my qualification?

For registration, the MRPBA does, through Ahpra. That assessment determines your pathway and whether you need to sit the National exam.

Will I have to sit the National examination?

It depends on your qualification and the outcome of the Board’s assessment. Some applicants are required to; others are not. Your assessment confirms it — do not assume either way.

What English evidence do I need?

You must satisfy the Board’s English Language Skills Registration Standard. The accepted tests and exemption pathways are set in that standard and reviewed periodically, so confirm the current requirements before booking anything.

How long does registration take?

It varies with the completeness of your application, document verification and your circumstances. A complete, well-evidenced submission is the best way to keep things moving.

Is registration the same as a radiation licence?

No. MRPBA registration is national and lets you practise; using radiation is licensed separately by each state or territory. You may need both, and your employer will usually help with the local licence.

Can I start applying for jobs before I am registered?

You can start the conversation early, and many employers support you through the process. But a lot of Australian employers expect registration to be in place, or well under way, before they finalise a role — so get the MRPBA application moving first. We line the two up so they do not hold each other up.

Can Ethicare help with all of this?

Yes. We help secure the role and sponsorship, and guide the preparation and timing of your registration, flagging issues while they are still easy to fix. The registration decision always rests with the Board.
