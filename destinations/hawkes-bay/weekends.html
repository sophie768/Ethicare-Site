Moving to New Zealand

A flat plain with two hundred kilometres of cycle trail on it, a peak with the region’s best view above it, surf beaches half an hour east, and hill country and ranges behind. Most of it is free and almost all of it is close.

Te Mata Peak

The landmark, and the region’s daily ritual. A steep ridge rising straight off the plain behind Havelock North, with a road to the top and a network of tracks up it. From the summit you get the whole bay, the plain, the ranges and, on a clear day, Ruapehu.

It takes about forty minutes to walk up and it is busy at dawn and dusk with people doing exactly that before or after work. Te Mata is also a place of significance in local tradition, and the story of the sleeping giant is worth knowing before you climb it.

The coast

Waimarama & Ocean Beach
Thirty to forty minutes south-east over the hills. Proper surf beaches, patrolled in summer, and the region’s weekend default.
Te Awanga & Haumoana
Twenty-five minutes from Napier, where the vineyards meet the shingle beach. Cellar doors and sea in the same afternoon.
Cape Kidnappers
One of the world’s most accessible mainland gannet colonies, on the cliffs at the end of the bay. Reachable on foot at low tide or by tour.
Waipatiki & the northern beaches
North of Napier — small, bush-backed bays that stay quiet even in January.
Marine Parade, Napier
The Norfolk pines, the sunken gardens and the aquarium. A shingle beach rather than a swimming one, and a lovely evening walk.
Fishing and diving
Good off this coast, with crayfish and pāua on the rocky sections. Rules on size and quantity are real — check before you gather.

On two wheels

The Hawke’s Bay Trails deserve their own paragraph. Around two hundred kilometres of linked, sealed, flat path across the plain, along the Tukituki and Ngāruroro rivers and around the coast — one of New Zealand’s Great Rides, and unusually useful because it connects real destinations.

The Wineries Ride is the one people do first: a flat loop between cellar doors with a designated driver or a shuttle at the end. The Landscapes and Water Rides cover the coast and the rivers. Bike hire is easy and the whole network is genuinely family-friendly.

Inland and upward

The Tukituki valley
Between Havelock North and the ranges — river swimming holes, vineyards and the prettiest driving in the region.
Kaweka & Ruahine Forest Parks
Serious tramping country an hour or so inland, with huts, tops and real remoteness.
Boundary Stream & Maungaharuru
A mainland island sanctuary north-west of Napier, with some of the best native birdsong in the North Island.
Central Hawke’s Bay
South through Waipawa and Waipukurau to the Tukituki’s upper reaches and the quiet coast at Porangahau.
Taupō & the volcanoes — 2 hr 15
Over the ranges to the lake, the Tongariro crossing and the ski fields.
Gisborne & the East Cape — 3 hr
North through Wairoa to the first place in the world to see the sun.
