What the work is actually like

You are in theatre, not supervising from the corridor.

The anaesthesia itself will feel familiar within a fortnight. What changes the job is how departments are staffed and what sits inside your scope.

Consultant-delivered

You do the list, not just the handover

Consultant presence in theatre is the norm rather than the aspiration, and departments are small enough that you know the surgeons, the technicians and the recovery staff by name. Most consultants describe the working relationship as noticeably flatter than the one they left.

Generalist breadth

Outside the tertiary centres, generalism is the job

Cardiac, neuro, complex paediatric and high-risk obstetric work concentrates in a handful of hospitals. Elsewhere you would carry a broad list with a subspecialty interest attached — which suits some people exactly and frustrates others, so it is worth being honest with yourself early.

The team around you

Anaesthetic technicians, not ODPs

A distinct regulated profession with its own board and its own training. The working relationship is similar, the scope and the terminology are not. Worth knowing before your first list rather than during it.

The scope is wider than most consultants expect. The Council defines anaesthesia to include peri-operative care, intensive care and pain management. In smaller hospitals that is not theoretical: anaesthesia and intensive care often share a roster, and you would be expected to cover both.

One thing to flag before you sign anything. If you intend to work predominantly in intensive care or in pain medicine, those are separate vocational scopes with their own requirements. Cover inside an anaesthesia post is one thing; a job that is really an ICU job with an anaesthesia label is another. We get the job description in writing and read it properly.

Private surgical hospitals operate in the larger centres and anaesthetic sessions are readily available — more so than in most specialties. It sits comfortably alongside a public post provided it does not cut across your contracted commitments.

Getting registered

Your specialty is one of the quicker ones

Reviewed August 2026 · checked against the Medical Council of New Zealand’s current published guidance

You register with the Medical Council of New Zealand in the vocational scope of anaesthesia. The route is the same one every specialist follows; three details are specific to you.

Anaesthesia is on the fast-track list. UK fellowship with a CCT or CCST, or an Irish fellowship with a CSCST, and a complete application is processed quickly — the Council publishes twenty working days for a complete application on that pathway.

A FANZCA needs no assessment. If you trained through ANZCA in Australia, your fellowship is an approved Australasian qualification. No comparability exercise, and no college advice to wait on.

Trained elsewhere? ANZCA advises the Council. Qualifications outside those lists are assessed against the FANZCA standard, on the college’s own additional-information form. Allow considerably longer, and start the college paperwork early rather than after you apply.

What the Council will want to see, whichever route you are on:

  • A consultant-level job offer — a listed prerequisite on the fast-track pathway
  • Primary-source verification of your medical degree and specialist qualification
  • Evidence of at least 24 months’ recent practice in the specialty
  • A certificate of good standing from every authority you have registered with
  • Identity, criminal-history and English-language evidence where required

The order you do things in matters more than the speed. Certificates of good standing and certified copies have short validity windows, and the Council’s window and Immigration New Zealand’s are not the same. We map that sequence with you and review your documents before anything is lodged.

Which vocational pathway fits you

Almost every consultant anaesthetist we move arrives by one of four MCNZ vocational pathways. They differ in what you already hold, not in how good a doctor you are — and the one that applies to you decides your timeline more than anything else in the move.

VOC4 · fast track

A UK or Irish fellowship, with a CCT, CCST or CSCST

Anaesthesia is one of the areas of medicine on the Council’s fast-track list. Fellowship of the Royal College of Anaesthetists with a CCT or CCST, or an Irish fellowship with a CSCST, sits on the approved list. A FANZCA doesn’t need this route — it is already an approved Australasian qualification, so it goes to VOC1 or VOC2.

20 working daysCouncil’s published time for a complete application
VOC1

FANZCA, and already registered here

An approved Australasian postgraduate qualification plus general registration in New Zealand. Nothing is assessed for comparability, because your fellowship already is the standard.

10 working daysThe quickest route the Council publishes
VOC2

FANZCA, but no NZ general registration

The same approved Australasian qualification, without general registration here yet. Most ANZCA fellows moving from Australia for the first time come this way.

20 working daysFrom a complete application
VOC3

Trained outside New Zealand and Australia

Your training and experience are assessed on their merits, with ANZCA advising the Council against the FANZCA standard. This is the route where early college paperwork earns you the most time.

Around three monthsIn most cases, once the application is complete

What the fast track actually asks of you

VOC4 has prerequisites the other routes don’t, and one of them surprises people: a job offer in Aotearoa New Zealand at consultant or specialist level is a listed prerequisite, so the role comes first and the application is built around it. You’ll also need 24 months’ practice in anaesthesia at 0.5 FTE or more within the past five years — 12 of them in the 18 months before you apply — in a health system the Council treats as comparable, three verified references, and your qualifications already moving through EPIC / MyIntealth. Verification is usually the slowest single step, which is why we start it before anything else.

And what follows approval

VOC3 and VOC4 both lead to provisional vocational registration rather than the full scope. You practise in anaesthesia under a named supervisor in an approved position — supervision is a condition of the provisional scope, not a judgement on you. If your job, location or supervisor changes during it, you apply to vary your scope at least 20 days ahead.

Two things worth diarising. Your registration meeting happens in New Zealand, within two weeks of your intended start date, and you cannot practise until it is done and your practising certificate issued — allow about three days after the meeting for that. And moving to the full vocational scope is an application you make once the Council’s requirements are met, not something that arrives on a date.

Where this comes from. The pathway definitions, prerequisites, processing times and approved areas of medicine above are the Medical Council of New Zealand’s own published guidance, read in August 2026 against its approved-qualifications list dated 13 August 2026. Times are for complete applications; an incomplete one restarts the clock, and the list is added to periodically. Check your own case against the Council before you make a decision on it.

Life in New Zealand

The list finishes, and the day is still yours.

A short commute, and the coast or the hills at the end of it.

Anaesthesia is one of the few consultant specialties where the working day genuinely ends. Lists are planned to finish, and the rota is built around a whole career rather than a decade of it. That is the thing consultants mention first, ahead of the scenery.

What you do with the time is the other half. Most hospitals sit within half an hour of open water, a mountain or a river, so the weekend does not need planning weeks in advance. For families that is usually the argument that settles it.

The rota is built for a career, not for a decade.

Where you could work

Where the consultant posts actually are

Anaesthesia is one of the few specialties recruiting in effectively every hospital, which means the choice is genuinely yours rather than dictated by where the vacancy happens to be.

Tertiary centres

Subspecialty lists, ANZCA trainees to teach, and a larger roster to share the out-of-hours between.

Regional hospitals

A broad generalist list, a shorter distance to the people making decisions, and more of the department known to you by name.

Cross-cover

In smaller hospitals anaesthesia and intensive care often share a roster. Good variety, but get it in writing first.

Private sessions

Private surgical hospitals in the larger centres, with sessions readily available alongside a public post.

The honest bit

What we can’t promise

A move this size deserves the limits as well as the pitch. Three things you will never hear us guarantee — and what we do instead.

Timelines that aren’t ours to give

Registration sits with the Medical Council and visas with Immigration New Zealand. We give you the realistic range, keep your file complete and moving, and chase what can be chased — but we will not quote a faster number to win you over.

A specific job in a specific city

Anaesthesia recruits widely, but the exact combination of subspecialty list, hospital and town you have in mind may not be open this year. If your heart is set on one place, we will tell you how long that wait could be — before you resign anything, not after.

That the move is right for you

Some moves shouldn’t happen. If the scope, the pay maths or the family logistics don’t stack up, we will say so plainly. We would rather lose a placement than see you land in the wrong life.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Will I keep my subspecialty lists?

Cardiac, neuro, complex paediatric and high-risk obstetric work concentrates in the tertiary centres. Elsewhere you would carry a generalist list with a subspecialty interest attached. Tell us which matters more — it decides which hospitals we put in front of you.

How much ICU cross-cover comes with the job?

In smaller hospitals, anaesthesia and intensive care often share a roster. In the main centres they are separate departments.

Your scope permits the cover, but predominant intensive care practice is its own vocational registration — so we get the job description in writing before you commit to anything.

Is there private work in anaesthesia?

More than in most specialties. Private surgical hospitals operate in the larger centres and anaesthetic sessions are readily available. It sits fine alongside a public role provided it does not cut across your contracted commitments.

What is on-call actually like?

Main centre: a larger roster with registrar cover in front of you. Regional: a thinner rota, called more often, and you are the one who drives in.

Paid at penalty rates plus an availability allowance — and we give you the actual rota before you decide.
