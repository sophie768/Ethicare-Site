Career guide · Speech & language therapy · New Zealand

Help people find their voice. Find more space for yours.

Speech and language therapy in New Zealand reaches from hospital wards to classrooms and kitchen tables. The route in works differently from most health professions, and that is the part worth understanding first.

At a glance

The pathway works differently here.

Speech-language therapy is self-regulated in New Zealand. There is no statutory registration board of the kind physiotherapy and occupational therapy have. What most employers expect instead is Registered Membership of the New Zealand Speech-language Therapists’ Association, which people shorten to NZSTA.

Professional body

NZSTA holds the professional framework. Membership, not statutory registration, is what employers ask to see.

Two overseas routes

Mutual Recognition, if your association and your training are both in a signatory country. Otherwise Qualifications Approval.

Trained in the UK?

The RCSLT is a signatory to the Mutual Recognition Agreement, so certified members who trained in the UK have the shorter route.

Immigration

Speech Language Therapist sits on the Green List under Straight to Residence, with NZSTA membership named in the requirements. What that means

One profession, two spellings. In the UK and Ireland you are a speech and language therapist. In New Zealand the profession writes itself as speech-language therapist, and the official occupation title is Speech Language Therapist. Same job, same training, different house style. We use the New Zealand form when we are describing New Zealand.

The work

Your career can look different here.

Speech-language therapists work across New Zealand’s health, education and community systems, supporting children and adults with communication and swallowing needs.

Breadth

More settings than most systems offer

Depending on your experience, the work can run into paediatrics, schools and learning support, adult rehabilitation, neurological services, disability, dysphagia, community practice or private services.

Scale

A small country, spread a long way

New Zealand’s population is small and its communities are dispersed. That often means working across settings rather than in one, and alongside families, teachers and multidisciplinary teams rather than only clinicians.

The catch

The professional route is the unusual part

For an internationally trained therapist, the clinical work travels well. What does not travel automatically is the credential, and the route in is not the one most health professions follow.

The important difference

You do not register through a statutory health board.

Unlike physiotherapy or occupational therapy, speech-language therapy does not currently have a statutory registration board in New Zealand. NZSTA provides the professional framework for the profession instead.

In practice this matters less than it sounds and more than it sounds, at the same time. Less, because there is no board application to sit alongside your visa. More, because most New Zealand employers require prospective speech-language therapists to hold Registered Membership of NZSTA, so it functions as the gate whether or not the law calls it one.

For an internationally trained therapist there are two routes to that membership, and which one applies to you is decided by where your association is and where you trained.

Trained in the UK?

If you completed an accredited UK speech and language therapy programme and hold Certified Membership of the RCSLT, the Mutual Recognition Agreement is likely to be your route to NZSTA membership. It is the shorter and cheaper of the two, and it is worth confirming your eligibility before you plan anything else around it.

Check my eligibility
Read this before you rely on it

Mutual recognition still has requirements.

Holding membership or certification with an overseas partner association does not mean NZSTA membership is granted automatically. There are two conditions that catch people out, and both are worth checking today rather than after you have handed in notice.

You must have trained in your association’s own country. The agreement rests on the equivalence of training programmes, not on the membership card. NZSTA says directly that you cannot enter through the Mutual Recognition Agreement if you are a member of a signatory association but trained somewhere else. So a therapist who trained outside the six countries and later gained RCSLT certified membership through the RCSLT’s own overseas process does not qualify on this route.

Dysphagia competency is not optional here. You cannot apply under the Mutual Recognition Agreement if you do not meet the dysphagia competency requirements. If that is you, the Qualifications Approval Process is the route instead, and since 12 June 2023 NZSTA has said so explicitly.

Do not assume your UK registration simply transfers

The route is shorter for eligible applicants from a mutual-recognition country. It is still an application, with its own evidence and its own fee, and NZSTA asks applicants to check its current requirements before submitting anything.

Route two

Not eligible for mutual recognition? There is another way in.

Internationally qualified speech-language therapists who do not meet the Mutual Recognition criteria apply through NZSTA’s Qualifications Approval Process. NZSTA assesses whether your qualification, clinical experience and professional competence meet its requirements for Registered Membership.

The current criteria are specific, and most of them are about evidence rather than judgement. Meeting them is what makes an application complete; it is not a guarantee of approval.

  • An acceptable speech-language therapy or speech pathology qualification
  • At least one year of supervised clinical practice, defined as no less than 36 weeks of full-time work at a minimum of 30 hours a week
  • Certified evidence of 1,000 hours of practice within the past five years
  • Demonstrated competency in communication disorders and in dysphagia
  • A record of continuing professional development
  • A criminal conviction record, or the equivalent, for every country you have lived in for 12 months or more in the last ten years since turning 18
  • English-language evidence, where it is required of you

The decision is final, and the fee is not refundable

NZSTA’s qualifications approval committee makes the decision and there is no appeal against it. That is the reason to establish which route applies to you before you pay, rather than after. It is also the reason we will read your evidence with you first if you ask us to, whether or not you ever work with us.

English language

Will I need IELTS?

Not everyone will.

For the Qualifications Approval Process, NZSTA requires IELTS Academic where your speech-language therapy qualification was completed in a language other than English, or where English is your second language. The current standard is a score of at least 7 in each of the four modules, with nothing below 7.

Test requirements move, and they move without much notice. Confirm the current standard with NZSTA before you book and pay for a test.

The detail that decides the most

Dysphagia experience matters.

If you are planning to work in New Zealand, do not leave your swallowing evidence to the end. NZSTA assesses dysphagia competence as part of both overseas processes, and it carries more consequence than any other single criterion.

On the Mutual Recognition route it is a bar: without it you cannot apply. On the Qualifications Approval route it is a condition: applicants who do not meet the dysphagia competencies may still be granted Registered Membership, but with a scope of practice carrying a condition that they are not dysphagia trained. NZSTA is candid that this restricts the roles available in New Zealand, so we are candid about it too.

If you work with adults, acute services or rehabilitation, check your dysphagia evidence early.

Where you could work

One profession. Very different settings.

Health services

Hospital and community services for adults and children with communication and swallowing disorders, within Health New Zealand · Te Whatu Ora. Work can include stroke, neurological rehabilitation, acute care, developmental services and multidisciplinary rehabilitation.

Education

Speech-language therapists work with children and young people inside New Zealand’s education system, alongside families, teachers, caregivers and other specialists.

Community

Closer to people’s homes, whānau and everyday environments, often with wider health, disability and social-care teams around you.

Private practice

Opportunities exist outside the public system too, including specialist and community-based services.

Working with children

Your workplace might be a classroom, not a clinic.

For paediatric therapists, New Zealand offers work inside education as well as health.

Therapists working in learning support may work across schools and communities rather than from one clinical base. The work means understanding a child’s communication needs in the context of their home, their education setting and their community, and working closely with parents, caregivers, teachers and other specialists.

It is a different shape of week from a hospital caseload. For some therapists it is the reason to come.

Pay

What could I earn?

There is no single speech-language therapy salary in New Zealand, and anyone who quotes you one is guessing. Pay depends on the employer, the sector, your experience, your qualifications and which employment agreement covers the role.

NZ$78,725 – NZ$116,481Education sector · qualified speech language therapists · nine steps, effective 2 April 2026

That is the published base salary scale for qualified speech language therapists working in education, set under the Primary Teachers’ Collective Agreement with NZEI Te Riu Roa. Where you sit on it depends on your qualification group and your assessed step.

What this figure is

A published scale for one employment agreement, not the salary for every speech-language therapist in the country.

What decides your step

Your qualification group and how much of your previous service is recognised. Recognition of overseas service is worth asking about early.

Immigration

And then there is the visa.

Your professional eligibility and your immigration eligibility are two separate processes, decided by two different organisations, on two different timetables.

Speech Language Therapist is on New Zealand’s Green List, under the Straight to Residence tier, as ANZSCO 252712. The occupational requirements name membership of the New Zealand Speech-language Therapists’ Association, which is the neat part: the professional route on this page is not a separate errand from the immigration one, it sits inside it.

What being on a list does not do is decide your case. Immigration settings change, and individual eligibility turns on your circumstances, your role, your employer and the criteria in force on the day you apply. We are not immigration advisers and we will not tell you that you can go straight to residence. We will tell you where to read the current rules, and help you sequence the professional side so it is ready when the immigration side asks for it.

Your career does not have to start in Auckland.
Regional New Zealand

Speech-language therapy work exists across New Zealand, not only in Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch.

For some international therapists a regional role offers exactly what made them look at New Zealand in the first place: a smaller community, easier access to the outdoors, a shorter journey to work, and the chance to become part of a local service rather than one name in a large department.

Regional life does not suit everyone, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. A thinner professional network, longer drives to specialist services and fewer options if a job does not work out are all real. The point is to compare places honestly against your own life, rather than to be steered towards wherever a vacancy happens to be.

Sophie’s view

Speech and language therapy is the profession where people most often assume the hard part is the visa, and then discover it was the credential. The two routes are not interchangeable, and which one you are on is decided by where you trained, not by how good you are.

If you take one thing from this page, make it the dysphagia evidence. It is the difference between a full scope and a restricted one, and it is far easier to gather while you are still in post than after you have left.

SophieCEO & Founder, Ethicare Resourcing
