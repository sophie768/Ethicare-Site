In depth

The detail, when you want it

Registration, pay, the work itself and life outside it. Everything is here, one section at a time.

Why New ZealandTime to actually care, and a country that closes on time.

GPs are in acute, sustained demand across New Zealand, urban and rural alike, and general practice here is still built around continuity and genuine generalist breadth. The shortage is real enough that you get to choose where you live, which is not true in most systems.

What draws most GPs is not the pay. It is that the consultation has room in it, that you keep the same families for years, and that the generalism is real rather than a triage function for somebody else’s clinic.

The Green List matters more than people expect. General practice sits in Tier 1, which means you and your family arrive on a Straight to Residence pathway rather than a work visa you later convert. Partner work rights and schooling come with it.

The medicine is proper generalism, and the weekends are yours.

What the work is actually likeThe practice is a business, and you can own part of it.

The clinical work will feel familiar inside a month. What changes the job is that New Zealand general practice runs on independent practices with enrolled patient populations, and you sit inside one as an employee, a contractor or an owner.

Enrolment

Patients belong to a practice

People enrol with a practice rather than turning up at whichever is nearest, and funding follows that enrolled population. It is the mechanism behind the continuity everyone praises, and the reason your list is yours.

The three models

Salaried, contractor, owner

Salaried is the softest landing and the most predictable. Contractor arrangements pay on what you generate. Buying into a practice is a business decision as much as a clinical one, and it is open to you in a way it may not have been at home.

Your week

You agree your sessions

You agree the number of sessions and how they sit across the week, with after-hours arrangements defined separately. Most GPs tell us this is where the balance actually comes from, rather than from the hours themselves.

Getting registeredRegistration overview

Reviewed July 2026 · checked against the Medical Council of New Zealand’s current published guidance

To practise as a GP in New Zealand you'll register with the Medical Council of New Zealand (MCNZ), working towards vocational registration in general practice. Most internationally trained GPs from comparable health systems register without re-sitting exams.

The Council assesses your qualifications and experience against New Zealand standards, often alongside the RNZCGP for the general-practice scope. You'll typically need:

  • Verification of your primary medical qualification
  • Evidence of recent general-practice experience
  • Certificates of good standing & a police clearance
  • Confirmation of English-language proficiency, if required

Which vocational pathway fits you

Almost every GP we move arrives by one of four MCNZ vocational pathways — and we'll confirm yours from the first conversation. Whichever applies, your qualifications are verified at source through EPIC / MyIntealth; it's often the slowest single step, so we start it early.

VOC4 · fast track

FRACGP, or a UK or Irish general-practice qualification

General practice is one of the areas of medicine on the Council’s fast-track list. FRACGP from Australia; FRCGP or MRCGP with a CCT from the UK; MICGP with a CSCST from Ireland. A job offer at specialist level is a listed prerequisite on this route, alongside 24 months’ recent general practice.

The RNZCGP Fellowship plus general registration in New Zealand — the direct route to the vocational scope, with nothing assessed for comparability.

FRNZCGP, but no NZ general registration

VOC3

A qualification that isn’t on the approved list

RNZCGP assesses your training against the FRNZCGP standard. “Equivalent to” leads to around 12 months’ supervision; “as satisfactory as” to an assessment pathway, around 18 months.

Around three monthsIn most cases, once the application is complete

What follows a VOC3 or VOC4 approval

Both lead to provisional vocational registration rather than the full scope. You practise under a named supervisor in an approved position — a condition of the provisional scope, not a judgement on you — and if your practice, location or supervisor changes during it, you apply to vary your scope at least 20 days ahead.

Two things worth diarising. Your registration meeting happens in New Zealand, within two weeks of your intended start date, and you cannot see patients until it is done and your practising certificate issued — allow about three days after the meeting. And moving to the full vocational scope is an application you make once the Council’s requirements are met, not something that arrives on a date.

Where this comes from. Pathway definitions, prerequisites and processing times above are the Medical Council of New Zealand’s own published guidance, read in August 2026 against its approved-qualifications list dated 13 August 2026. Times are for complete applications; an incomplete one restarts the clock. Check your own case against the Council before you make a decision on it.

Registration checklist

What to gather before you apply to the MCNZ:

  • Certified copies of your qualifications
  • Evidence of recent, relevant clinical practice
  • A certificate of good standing from every authority you’ve registered with
  • A police / criminal-history check
  • Proof of identity, plus any name-change documents
  • English-language evidence (IELTS or OET), if required
  • One personal and one professional referee
  • Your application fee, ready at submission

Advice & notes

Start early

Gathering documents before you apply is the single best way to avoid delays. Missing paperwork is the most common hold-up.

Mind the dates

Certificates of good standing and certified copies have a short validity window, so time them so they’re still current when you submit.

Where the job offer fits

On the fast-track pathway a specialist-level job offer is one of the Council’s prerequisites, so the role does come first. The first step is a conversation, not paperwork. We’ll sequence it with you.

Life in New ZealandSurgery finishes, and the country is already there.

A short commute, and the coast or the hills at the end of it.

General practice is one of the few medical careers where you largely set your own week, and in a country this size that compounds. A four-day pattern is common and unremarkable, and the fifth day is not swallowed by a commute.

Most practices sit within half an hour of open water, a mountain or a river. For families that tends to be the argument that settles it, well before anyone works out the tax.

You set the sessions, so the week is yours to shape.

Where you could workThe shortage is national, so the choice is yours

Very few professions let you pick the place first and the job second. General practice is one of them, in a country where the difference between a city and a small town is twenty minutes rather than an afternoon.

Main centres

Larger practices, teaching and registrar supervision, and the widest choice of employment model. Housing costs the most here.

Provincial towns

A broader everyday scope, a shorter distance to the people making decisions, and housing that changes the maths entirely.

Rural and remote

The widest scope in the country, often with additional incentives, and the practice is frequently the health service for the district.

Urgent care

Clinic-based acute work as a whole role or alongside a practice list. A good fit if you want the variety without the continuity.

Compared · Australia and New ZealandIf you’re weighing both countries, general practice itself is the difference

On paper the two look remarkably similar: English-speaking systems and strong demand for experienced GPs. In practice general practice works quite differently in each, and that is where the decision actually lives.

Australia generally offers greater earning potential, a far wider choice of locations and a more commercially driven model of general practice. New Zealand offers a smaller, more connected system where continuity of care and community medicine still carry real weight. Neither is universally the better choice; it depends on what you want from the work, and from life outside it.

Registration is a separate process in each country — never assume one follows from the other. The full comparison sits in Australia or New Zealand?, and the money side in Where will your salary go further?

Common questions

Frequently asked

Will I need to re-sit exams?

Almost never. GPs from comparable health systems register towards vocational registration without sitting New Zealand exams — what varies is the supervision that comes first. If your qualification is on the Medical Council's approved list you take the fastest route with no separate college equivalence assessment. If it isn't, RNZCGP assesses your training and the outcome sets the length: broadly around a year of supervision, or longer where an assessment pathway applies.

Supervision here is not someone checking your work. It is a named colleague, regular scheduled conversations, and a structured way of showing you have settled into New Zealand general practice. Most GPs describe it as useful rather than onerous — and it ends with permanent vocational registration.

How different is the funding and consultation model?

This is the biggest practical adjustment, and it is worth understanding before you arrive. Practices enrol patients and receive capitation funding for that enrolled population, topped up by a patient co-payment at the time of the appointment. Most people pay something to see their GP; children and Community Services Card holders pay less or nothing, and practices in high-need areas are funded differently again.

What it means day to day: your list is your list, continuity is real, and the funding follows the relationship rather than the activity. There is no QOF-style points system driving your week. Standard appointments are short by international standards, though longer consultations are widely available and how you structure your day is largely yours to negotiate.

Salaried, contractor or practice ownership — which should I choose?

They are different jobs, not just different pay structures. Salaried is the simplest landing: a known income, employment protections, someone else carrying the business. Contractor arrangements give you more control over sessions and a higher ceiling, with the trade-off that you carry more of your own risk, tax and indemnity admin. Ownership is a business as well as a clinical role, and rewards people who want a say in how the practice is run.

Our honest advice is to arrive salaried or on a simple contract, learn the system for a year, then decide. Almost nobody regrets that order. We'll show you the numbers for a specific role, alongside what living in that town actually costs, rather than a national average.

What is rural general practice actually like here?

Broader than you are probably imagining, and that is the appeal. Rural GPs manage presentations that would be referred on in a city — minor trauma, acute medicine, obstetric and emergency care, sometimes inpatient work — with real distance between you and the nearest hospital. Demand is highest here, incentives are often better, and the clinical breadth is why many people never go back to metropolitan practice.

It asks something of you in return: fewer colleagues on site, on-call that really is on-call, and a small community where you are the doctor at the supermarket too. Some people find that the best part of the job. We will tell you plainly which category we think a role falls into.

Do I have to become an RNZCGP Fellow?

Not to work. You practise under vocational registration in general practice; Fellowship of the Royal New Zealand College of General Practitioners is the college qualification alongside it. If you already hold FRNZCGP, that is usually the most direct route to permanent vocational registration.

Many internationally trained GPs go on to Fellowship because it is the standard marker of a New Zealand generalist and it opens teaching, supervision and leadership roles. It is a choice about the shape of your career rather than a condition of holding a job, and it is not something you need resolved before you move.

Will my family be able to come, and can my partner work?

Yes, and this is one of the strongest parts of the New Zealand offer for GPs. General practice sits at the top tier of the Green List, which means a residence application from the outset rather than years on a work visa first. Partners can generally work, and children attend state school on the same basis as New Zealand children.

Immigration settings do change, so we confirm the current position for your family and your nationality before anything is lodged, and we work alongside a licensed immigration adviser where the situation needs one. We would rather give you the accurate answer than the encouraging one.
