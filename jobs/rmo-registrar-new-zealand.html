Being straight with you

We don’t run a vacancy list at this level.

Every other profession page on this site has a roles section. This one does not, and it would be easy to fake. Here is what actually happens instead.

Services outside the main centres come to us when they need a senior registrar, a registrar or a house officer — often at short notice, and usually for posts that were never advertised nationally. There is no board to watch and no listing to refresh. There is a list of doctors who told us where they are and what they are after, and we work from it when something lands.

So the useful thing you can do is register your interest. It takes a few minutes, it costs nothing, and it is genuinely how these posts get filled. If nothing suitable comes up we will say so rather than keep you warm.

The levels

Where you would fit

Titles differ between systems, so it is worth being explicit about what these words mean here rather than assuming they map onto the ones you use now.

Senior registrar

Post-fellowship and final-year posts

Most often in regional services, and frequently a step towards a consultant appointment in the same place. If that is what you are after, say so early — it changes which posts we put in front of you.

Registrar

Service registrar roles

In regional and provincial hospitals. Rarely advertised nationally, and more often filled through a conversation than an application form. Broad exposure, and a shorter distance to the consultants than you may be used to.

House officer & SHO

Fewer of these come our way

And almost all of them sit outside the metro centres. Worth registering your interest rather than waiting for a listing, because a listing is unlikely to appear.

Regional posts here are good jobs, not the consolation prize they can be elsewhere.

Getting registered

General registration, not a vocational scope

This is the main thing that differs from the consultant pages on this site. A consultant applies for a vocational scope in a named specialty. At this level you are applying for general registration with the Medical Council of New Zealand, and which route applies depends on where you qualified and where you have been practising since.

We deliberately do not restate the routes here. Registration is a national topic, it changes, and it is explained once in the guide that owns it rather than paraphrased on every page that touches it — which is how sites end up with two answers to one question.

What is worth knowing before you read it: the route is decided by your qualification and your recent practice, not by the level of the job you are applying for; the paperwork discipline is the same as at consultant level, and primary-source verification is usually the slowest single step; and the visa can generally run alongside registration rather than after it.

Life in New Zealand

A regional hospital, and a country you can reach on a day off.

A short commute, and the coast or the hills at the end of it.

Doctors at this level usually arrive for the training and the breadth, and stay for the second half of the sentence. Regional services give you more responsibility earlier and a much shorter distance to the people making decisions.

Outside work, most hospitals sit within half an hour of open water, a river or a mountain, so a weekend does not need planning weeks ahead. It is a genuinely different rhythm from a metro training post, and for a lot of people that is the point.

More responsibility, sooner, and a shorter walk to the consultant.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Do you have a list of registrar jobs?

No, and we would rather say so than pretend otherwise. We do not run a vacancy list at this level.

What happens instead is quieter: services outside the main centres come to us when they need a senior registrar, a registrar or a house officer, often at short notice and for posts that were never advertised widely. Register your interest and you are on the list we actually work from.

Is registration different from the consultant route?

Yes, and it is the main thing to understand. A consultant applies for a vocational scope in a named specialty. At this level you are applying for general registration, and which route applies depends on where you trained and where you have been practising.

It is the same regulator and the same paperwork discipline, but a different question is being asked of you. The pathway checker will tell you which route is likely to apply before you spend anything.

Will a registrar post lead to a consultant job?

Sometimes, and more often in regional services than in the main centres — a senior registrar post in a smaller hospital is frequently a step towards a consultant appointment in the same place.

We will be straight with you about whether a specific post is genuinely that, or simply service cover. The two look identical in an advert and are very different careers.

Why don’t you publish pay for these roles?

Because the package depends on the service, the roster and the agreement that covers it, and we would rather confirm the real number with you than print a range that turns out not to apply.

Ask us for the actual figure on an actual post and you will get it.
