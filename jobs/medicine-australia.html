Career guide · Medicine · Australia

One registration.
Eight health systems.

Your registration travels the whole country. Your pay, your rostering and your hospital do not — and understanding that split is most of understanding a medical career here.

The careers

Several medicines, one register

Consultant, GP, rural generalist or doctor in training — the register is the same, but the route in, the employer and the working week are not.

Consultants

Specialist recognition through your college, then state-employed or private practice.

General practice

Medicare-funded, largely private businesses — a different economy from hospital medicine.

Rural generalists

Broad scope, procedural work and autonomy — a recognised career path here, not a compromise.

Doctors in training

State-employed rotations, with terms set by each state’s agreement.

Non-clinical medicine

Research, public health, medical administration — the routes beyond the ward.

Worth knowing before you search

Ask which state before you ask which hospital. Public hospitals are run by eight separate state and territory systems on their own enterprise agreements. Two consultant posts with the same title in different states are different jobs — pay scale, rostering, leave and progression all move when you cross a border.

Nothing in a job advert makes that obvious. Ask us and we will set two offers side by side properly.

The route in

The college decides the specialty; the Board registers the doctor

For specialists, recognition runs through your specialist college — comparability is assessed against the Australian trained standard, and the outcome shapes everything that follows. The Medical Board, through Ahpra, then registers you to practise.

The assessment

Your college, first

Specialist recognition is assessed by the relevant college against Australian-trained standards — substantially, partially or not comparable. The outcome decides supervision, any further requirements and how quickly you stand fully recognised.

The registration

Then the Board

With the college outcome in hand, the Medical Board grants the registration class that matches it. Provisional or limited first is normal, not a demotion — and the pathway checker maps which class likely applies to you.

“Comparable” is a finding, not a feeling — get it assessed before you resign anything.

What it is like to practise here more broadly — the funders, the states, cultural safety and what feels different — is explained once, properly, in practising in Australia.

Where the work happens

Where might that work take you?

Public hospitals are only part of the map. Australian medicine runs across a very large private sector, Medicare-funded general practice, and distances that make rural medicine its own discipline.

The map

Where the roles are, and what each setting changes.

State public hospitals

Acute, teaching and tertiary medicine on state agreements — the training ground and the safety net.

Private hospitals & practice

A larger share of elective and specialist work than most systems — different economics, different week.

General practice

Medicare-funded and mostly privately owned, with bulk-billing and billing models shaping the day.

Rural & remote medicine

Outreach, retrieval and rural generalism — the most autonomy, often the strongest packages, and real isolation to plan for.

Across the family

What applies across medicine

The things that are the same whichever of these professions you hold.

Who regulates this work?+

The Medical Board of Australia, through Ahpra — one national registration covering every state and territory. Specialist recognition is assessed by the relevant specialist college before the Board grants the matching registration class.

Registration is the first gate, and it runs on its own timeline. How registration works in Australia · Check your pathway

What does the pay look like?+

Registration is national; pay is not. Public terms are set state by state through enterprise agreements, so the same job pays differently in Perth and in Hobart — and comparing two offers means comparing two agreements. Private and scheme-funded work varies again.

For any live vacancy the package is confirmed with you directly — we don’t quote figures against named roles.

Which visa would I need?+

Most clinicians move on an employer-sponsored work visa, and several professions in this family sit on the skilled occupation lists, which shapes the route to permanent residence.

The visa can usually run alongside registration. Visas and residence
