# Melbourne photography — audit

## Added 23 Aug 2026

- `melbourne-cbd-street-towers-overcast.jpg` — looking down a CBD street into the Southbank tower
  cluster on a grey day, tram wires overhead, cars parked both kerbs. Jasper Hotel visible.
  Melbourne, confirmed. Portrait crop. Useful as the honest weather shot: most Melbourne imagery is
  sunny, and the climate is a real consideration for anyone moving from a warm country.
- `melbourne-yarra-southbank-crown.jpg` — the Yarra at Southbank: Crown Towers, the Exhibition
  Centre's gold scalloped roof, the Southbank promenade, parkland on the north bank.

- `melbourne-holi-festival.jpg` — Holi in a Melbourne park: two men mid-throw, colour powder in
  the air, dry summer grass, marquees behind, a sari at frame right. Melbourne, on Sophie's
  attribution — nothing in frame names the city, so **caption it as Holi in Australia rather than
  naming Melbourne** unless the source confirms it. Tall portrait crop (1500×2000).

  Pairs directly with `qld/qld-mareeba-mosque.jpg` as belonging evidence, and covers the other half
  of the argument: the mosque shows the quiet, permanent, built kind of community; this shows the
  public, celebratory kind, in a park, with white Australians joining in. For a clinician from South
  Asia weighing whether their culture travels, that second point is the one that lands.

  Two cautions. The powder makes it the most saturated image in the whole library, which sits
  awkwardly beside a palette built on teal, cream and apricot — it needs to be a single deliberate
  accent, never one tile in a grid of calmer photographs. And it is a festival, not everyday life;
  used alone it would overstate. Best used next to the mosque, not instead of it.

- `melbourne-gurdwara-kirtan.jpg` — three Sikh musicians performing kirtan: saffron turbans, grey
  waistcoats, two harmoniums and a pair of tabla on a crimson-draped platform, microphones, an
  electronic tanpura. **Melbourne, confirmed** — a banner at frame right reads MELBOURNE, so unlike
  the Holi photograph this one carries its own evidence.

  The strongest of the three belonging images, and the one to lead with. The mosque is a building
  and the Holi shot is a crowd; this is **people doing something skilled, together, indoors, on an
  ordinary day** — a community with musicians, instruments, a sound system and a routine, which is
  what "established" actually looks like. It is also calm: greys, crimson and saffron sit far more
  comfortably beside teal and cream than the Holi powder does.

  Use it as the primary belonging image and keep Holi as the occasional accent. Caption it for what
  it is — kirtan at a Melbourne gurdwara — and do not name the gurdwara, since the banner names only
  the city.

- `melbourne-chinatown-gate.jpg` — the Little Bourke Street paifang: glazed guardian lions, orange
  tiled roof, Chinese lettering, red lanterns strung down the street beyond, a second gate visible
  further along, traffic and pedestrians under it. **Melbourne, confirmed twice over** — the Chinese
  inscription on the gate names Melbourne, and the car below carries a Victorian plate.

  This is the Chinatown shot the belonging set was missing, and it completes the group: the gurdwara
  gives community as skilled practice, the mosque gives it as a permanent building in a regional
  town, and this gives it as a **whole quarter of a capital city** — settled long enough to have
  built formal gateways to itself. Melbourne's is the oldest continuously inhabited Chinatown in the
  western world, which is a fact worth having a photograph for.

  A note on legibility: an ANZ branch and a Target sign are both in frame. That is not a flaw here —
  it is the argument. The everyday Australian city and the Chinese quarter are the same street.
  Portrait crop (1500×2250).

### Near-miss worth recording

`melbourne-yarra-southbank-crown.jpg` **was sent to us as Brisbane.** It is unambiguously
Melbourne: the Crown crest sits on the tower, the Melbourne Exhibition Centre's gold scalloped roof
("Jeff's Shed") is in frame on the right, and the river is the Yarra with the Southbank promenade
along it — not the Brisbane River. Had it gone into the Brisbane guide it would have been the Uluru
error again, three weeks later: a real photograph of a real city, captioned as a different one
1,700 km away.

Two river-city skylines look alike from a bridge. The tell is always a named building, never the
water. Open the file, find the building, then caption it.
