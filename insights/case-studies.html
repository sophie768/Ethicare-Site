Not marketing stories: real workforce examples, and what they taught us.

The whole toolkit argues one thing: that international recruitment succeeds or fails in the year either side of the appointment, not on the day someone signs. These examples show what that looks like in practice: a team rebuilt, and lives that took root. Details are shared with permission and anonymised to protect the people involved.

A sustainable AT team in Palmerston North

Like many services, a perioperative team at a hospital in Palmerston North faced ongoing Anaesthetic Technician vacancies. They were hard to fill locally, which piled pressure on existing staff and meant relying on locum cover to keep the service running. Rather than reach for a short-term fix, the service set out to recruit people with the skills, values and motivation to build long-term careers in New Zealand.

Case study · perioperative services

Three Anaesthetic Technicians, one lasting team

The challenge

Persistent AT vacancies, heavy reliance on locum cover, and mounting pressure on the existing team: a gap that local recruitment alone couldn’t close.

The approach

Three Anaesthetic Technicians were recruited, each having originally trained in India before building extensive experience at a leading JCI-accredited hospital in the Middle East. Selection focused on clinical capability, shared values and long-term commitment, and the focus stayed on preparation, support and integration across the whole journey: registration and relocation support, the Kiwi Workplace Readiness Programme before departure, regular two-way communication, and settlement planned before arrival.

What made it work

The department itself. The service manager personally met each new recruit and their family at the airport; accommodation was arranged, practical settlement advice was given, and the wider team put on a picnic so the new arrivals and their families could start building relationships. Simple gestures, but they said clearly: you are welcome here.

The honest part

It wasn’t without challenges. Registration and immigration needed careful coordination, and relocating meant leaving family, friends and familiar support behind. Because those challenges were recognised early and communication stayed regular, issues were resolved before they became barriers.

The outcome

All three arrived, registered and settled: into the roster, and into the town. The service moved from rolling locum cover to a stable permanent team, and the families who were met at the airport are still building their lives there.

The lesson: success lay not in the appointment itself, but in the planning, partnership and support that surrounded it.

International recruitment is most successful when organisations recruit people (not just professions) and support them long after they arrive.

Stories from those who have made the move

Ask the people we’ve supported what made the move worth it, and they rarely start with the role. They talk about the life that grew around it. Four of those stories, in their own words, shared with permission:

Radiographer · South Africa → Palmerston North

My children finally had the freedom to just be children

I remember one evening making dinner and looking out of the kitchen window. The children were outside on their bikes with the neighbour’s children. They’d been out there for ages, and I hadn’t once gone to check where they were. It suddenly hit me that I’d stopped worrying.

Here they have much more freedom to play, explore and simply enjoy being children. They come home covered in mud, usually with a new story to tell. We miss our family back home, and we always will, but we’ve never regretted giving our children this opportunity.

Radiographer · South Africa → Whakatāne

“Mum… when are we going to see snow?”

While I was worrying about registration, visas and everything that comes with moving to another country, my daughter only wanted to talk about one thing: snow. A few months after we arrived, we finally made a trip to the South Island. She picked up a handful, laughed for about five minutes, then complained that her hands were freezing. We still laugh about it now.

Children have a funny way of keeping things simple. For her, the move was never about paperwork or a new job. Now we’re always planning our next family adventure.

Anaesthetic Technician · Iran → Whangārei

Weekends look very different now

I moved for the chance to work as an Anaesthetic Technician. What I didn’t expect was how much I’d enjoy life outside the hospital. I’ve completed some of New Zealand’s Great Walks, including the Milford Track, and there are still plenty more on my list. I can finish work on a Friday and be walking through native forest, beside waterfalls or along the coast a few hours later.

Back home, life always felt busy. Here, it feels like there’s more time to enjoy it.

Sonographer · United Kingdom → Auckland

I never thought I’d live by the sea

I worked in one of London’s busiest hospitals, so I expected New Zealand’s biggest city to feel much the same. It really doesn’t. I’d always dreamed of living by the sea; now it’s completely normal to walk along the beach after work. I still catch myself looking up at the blue sky some mornings — after so many grey days in London, you really notice it.

It’s not that Auckland is quiet. It’s that life just feels a little less rushed.

These aren’t testimonials. They’re the real measure of whether international recruitment worked, and every one of them rests on support that continued long after arrival.
