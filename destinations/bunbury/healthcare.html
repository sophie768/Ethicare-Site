Healthcare in Bunbury

Bunbury punches above its population clinically, for one structural reason: it is where the South West sends its patients. Understanding that referral role is the difference between reading this as a small-city job and reading it accurately.

The system

A regional referral centre
The region’s main public hospital takes referrals from district hospitals across the South West, and provides the specialist services those towns do not carry.
District hospitals behind it
Smaller hospitals in the surrounding towns handle local and lower-acuity work, and refer inward when a case needs the specialist bench.
A genuine private sector
Private hospital and imaging services operate alongside the public system, including the only PET service anywhere in the South West.
Perth is the tertiary backstop
The most complex work goes north to the capital — two hours by road, and a normal part of how the region operates.
A note on names
  • We don’t name hospitals, trusts or private providers as places of work anywhere on this site — a named employer implies a relationship, and the post and the package get confirmed with you directly.
  • Everything above is described structurally for that reason. If you want to know exactly where a role sits, ask us and we will tell you plainly.

What the referral role does to your work

A regional referral centre with a large, thinly populated catchment behind it pulls in two directions at once, and both are true of the same job.

What the region gives you
A case mix wider than the city’s own population generates, because presentations arrive from across the South West rather than from Bunbury alone. Generalist breadth stays alive here in a way it often does not in a metropolitan subspecialty post.
What it asks of you
Comfort making decisions with a smaller bench beside you, and with distance in the system — some patients travel two hours to reach you, and some of your discussions with a tertiary colleague happen by phone.

Imaging and radiation, specifically

This is where our Australian work is concentrated, so it is the part we can speak to in most detail. The South West’s imaging capability sits across both sectors, and the private side is more substantial than people expect for a city of this size — general nuclear medicine, PET–CT, and the modern gamma-camera and CT capacity that goes with it. For a technologist or radiographer that has a specific consequence: you are more likely to be operating across a wider range of modalities than you would in a large metropolitan department where the work is subdivided.

It also means training and cross-skilling tend to be available rather than theoretical, because a smaller service needs people who can cover more than one room.

How pay works here

Registration is national but pay is not. In the public system, salaries are set by the relevant Western Australian health system industrial agreement, negotiated between the health service and the union covering your profession — so the same role can sit on a different scale in another state. In the private sector, pay is negotiated individually and is often higher, particularly where a service is hard to staff.

We do not publish figures we have not verified against the agreement that actually governs the role. For the indicative bands and how the frameworks work, see Salary in Australia, explained honestly.

What to ask at interview

  • How far does this department’s referral catchment actually extend?
  • Which cases go to Perth, and how is that handled day to day?
  • How many colleagues sit alongside me in my specialty specifically?
  • What is the on-call pattern, and how often is it genuinely called upon?
  • Which modalities or sub-specialties would I be expected to cover?
  • What CPD time and funding is provided, and does it cover travel to Perth?
  • Is there scope to cross-train, and is that supported or just permitted?
The short version

Bunbury is a referral centre, not a district hospital, which is why the clinical breadth is wider than the population suggests. The trade is a smaller bench and a two-hour road link to tertiary care — and for imaging specifically, a private sector deeper than you would expect at this scale.
