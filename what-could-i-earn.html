The money question

What could I earn?

A new role. A different lifestyle. What could it mean for you financially? Explore salaries in New Zealand and Australia, work out what would reach your account, and see how the numbers fit the life you want.

For you

Where are you considering?

Pay you can check

Here is something unusual about both countries: in the public systems, pay is set by collective agreement and published. That means we can show you indicative bands and name the agreement they come from, and it means you can verify us. We keep figures rough on purpose, rounded so neither end overstates, because a precise number against a role we have not seen would be a promise we are not in a position to make.

What might not suit you

The things people wish they had known

We would rather you heard these from us now than discovered them in month three. None of them is a reason not to come. All of them are reasons some people decide not to, and that is a perfectly good outcome.

A pay rise is not always a pay rise

If you are coming from a no-income-tax country, the gross figure can look excellent and the net one ordinary. Housing may cost more than you are used to as well. Run both against the cost of living before you get attached to the headline.

Progression is slower and flatter

Public-system scales are transparent, which is the good news. It also means less room to negotiate, and seniority often counts for more than performance. Some people find that fairer; others find it frustrating.

You may be further from family than any map suggests

Flights are long and expensive, and a two-week visit is not a weekend trip. This is the single most common thing people underestimate, and it is worth a conversation at home early on.

The first year costs more than the calculator says

Setting up a household from nothing, a car, furniture, a deposit, is real money that no relocation package covers in full. Build a buffer, not just a budget.

If any of this gives you pause, that is the page doing its job. Talk it through with us — we would rather have a clear conversation than a placement that unravels.

The other half of the answer

Is it worth it for you?

The numbers matter. So does the life around them. A move might bring more time outdoors, a new direction at work, or the chance to build a different family life. It can also mean starting again, being further from people you love, and finding your feet somewhere unfamiliar.

  • Will I enjoy the actual role and working pattern?
  • Does the budget work if we start on one income?
  • What opportunities will my partner and children have?
  • Can we afford visits home, and the unexpected?
  • Does this move bring us closer to the life we want?

You do not need every answer before you start exploring. You do deserve a clear picture before you commit to anything.
