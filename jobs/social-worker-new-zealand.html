How is this different from Australia?Registered by law, not by association

It is the difference that matters most if you are weighing both countries. Australia has no national register for social work: the Australian Association of Social Workers sets the standard, and eligibility for membership is what employers and funding bodies use in its place. New Zealand has statutory registration, a scope of practice and an annual practising certificate. Neither is simply easier. They are different shapes.

In depth

The detail, when you want it

Registration, what it gates, and how the move sequences around it.

Getting registeredMandatory, and it gates the job

Mandatory registration confirmed against swrb.govt.nz, August 2026. The Board’s detailed criteria for overseas-qualified applicants change; read them on its own site before you act, and we will go through them with you.

New Zealand is stricter than Australia here, and it is the difference that reorders everything else: social work is a protected profession, and registration with the Social Workers Registration Board is mandatory for anyone practising as a social worker.

Overseas qualifications are assessed individually against the New Zealand standard, and registration carries an annual practising certificate alongside it. Because registration gates the job rather than following it, this is the first step in the sequence and not the last.

“You cannot start a role and complete registration afterwards.”

That single sentence is why social work moves need planning further ahead than most. An employer cannot hold a post open indefinitely, and you cannot begin on a promise. Tell us early and we will sequence the registration, the offer and the visa together rather than one behind the other.

What to gather now

  • Certified copies of your social work qualification and transcripts
  • A certificate of good standing from every authority you have registered with
  • Evidence of your practice history, by date and setting
  • A police / criminal-history check
  • Proof of identity, plus any name-change documents
  • English-language evidence, if required

Why the order matters

In most professions on this site you can hold an offer while registration finishes. Not here. Start the Board’s process before you start applying, and treat any role you are offered as contingent on it — because it is.

What you would be paid

One scale, six professions

Rates payable from the 2026 anniversary in the APEX and Te Whatu Ora social workers collective agreement. Published by APEX and by Health New Zealand · Te Whatu Ora.

Open the social work, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, radiation therapy and anaesthetic technology agreements side by side and you find the same ten steps, to the dollar. That is the allied pay equity settlement: your step sets your salary, not your profession.

Step 1NZ$79,014Entry, on registration
Step 7NZ$110,290Top automatic step — one a year on your anniversary
Step 10NZ$119,300Recognition step, for experienced practitioners not in a designated role

Steps 1 to 7 are automatic, one a year on your anniversary date. Step 8 depends on objectives agreed with your manager when you reach step 7. Steps 9 and 10 are recognition steps. Designated roles — team leader, section head, professional advisor — sit above this on a separate six-grade scale.

Where you land on this ladder is the single biggest number in your offer, and it is set by how your overseas experience is counted. That is worth getting right rather than accepting.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Can I start work while my registration is being processed?

No. Social work is a protected profession in New Zealand: registration with the Social Workers Registration Board is required to practise, full stop. There is no provisional arrangement that lets you begin and finish the paperwork later.

It is the single most important thing to understand about moving here as a social worker, and it is why we would rather talk to you months ahead than weeks.

Will my overseas qualification be recognised?

It is assessed individually against the New Zealand standard rather than sorted by country, so there is no list to check yourself against. The Board publishes what it wants to see, and that is the place to start.

Send us your qualification and practice history and we will tell you honestly how it is likely to read before you pay anything.

Is it the same in Australia?

No, and the difference is structural. Australia has no national register for social work. The Australian Association of Social Workers sets the professional standard and eligibility for its membership is what employers and funding bodies use instead. That same assessment usually serves skilled migration, which is why it is worth starting first if Australia is your destination.

New Zealand registers you by law. If you are weighing both, that is the difference to weigh.

You have no social work vacancy listed. Does that mean you don’t place social workers?

No. A vacancy board shows what is advertised this week, not what we recruit. Send us your CV and tell us what you are looking for, and we will come back to you when something fits — and in the meantime you can use the guides and the plan to keep the move moving.

Will my pay be different from an occupational therapist’s?

On the Health New Zealand scale, no. Social work, dietetics, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, radiation therapy and anaesthetic technology share the same ten steps to the dollar under the allied pay equity settlement. Your step sets your salary, not your profession.

Which step you start on is negotiable and depends on how your overseas experience is counted. That is the number worth arguing about.
