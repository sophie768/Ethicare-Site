Why this page starts here

Regulated here, unregulated where many people trained

Anaesthetic technology is a registered profession in New Zealand, with its own council and its own scope. In several countries clinicians doing the same work are unregulated, which changes both how you apply and how the role is regarded once you arrive.

It also means your experience is recognised formally rather than informally.

In New Zealand

A registered profession

Anaesthetic technicians register with the Medical Sciences Council of New Zealand, against a defined scope of practice. Registration is what allows you to work, and it runs on its own timeline.

The practical upside: the role carries formal standing, and your experience is assessed and recognised rather than taken on trust.

Where you may have trained

Often no register at all

If you have practised in a country where the role is unregulated, expect the evidence requirements to focus on demonstrating scope rather than matching a qualification.

In practice that means gathering the detail of what you actually do — the lists, the techniques, the equipment, the supervision arrangements — and it is worth starting that early.

The shape of the day

One patient, one list, five handovers

Perioperative work is defined by handover — the same patient passing through five settings in a few hours, with a different combination of the team around them at each. Whichever of these professions you hold, this is the sequence your day runs on.

Stage 1

Pre-assessment

The patient is worked up before the day — history, medications, airway, comorbidities — so that surprises happen here rather than in the anaesthetic room.

Anaesthetists · pre-assessment nursing · the referring team
Stage 2

The anaesthetic room

Where anaesthetic technicians and anaesthetists work most closely: equipment checked, drugs drawn, monitoring on, airway secured.

Anaesthetists · anaesthetic technicians
Stage 3

Theatre

The list itself. Position, monitoring, drugs and equipment managed continuously while the surgical team works.

Anaesthetists · anaesthetic technicians · surgical and nursing teams
Stage 4

Recovery

Emergence, airway, pain and observation until the patient is safe to move — a handover that has to be complete rather than quick.

Recovery nursing · anaesthetists
Stage 5

Ward or discharge

Back to the ward, or home the same day for day-surgery lists, with the plan handed on to whoever picks up the care.

Ward and day-surgery teams · the patient and their whānau

The list only runs if the whole team is there. That is why theatre recruitment is a team problem, not a vacancy.

Across the family

What applies across theatre & perioperative

The things that are the same whichever of these professions you hold.

Who regulates this work?+

The Medical Sciences Council of New Zealand registers anaesthetic technicians, and anaesthetists register with the Medical Council of New Zealand. If you have practised in a country where the technician role is unregulated, expect the evidence requirements to focus on demonstrating scope rather than matching a qualification.

Registration is the first gate, and it runs on its own timeline.

What does the pay look like?+

Public-system pay is set by collective agreement rather than negotiated role by role, so the same job pays the same wherever in the country you do it. Private and ACC-funded work sits outside that and varies.

Indicative bands sit on each profession’s own guide above.

Which visa would I need?+

Most clinicians move on a work visa tied to an accredited employer, and some roles in this family sit on the Green List, which changes the route to residence considerably.

The visa can usually run in parallel with registration rather than after it.

What about the rest of the move?+

Schools, housing, healthcare, banking, shipping and the first few weeks are the same for every profession, so we explain them once rather than on each page.
