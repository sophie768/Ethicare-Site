Before you search

Sometimes the role matters more than the profession. A New Zealand employer may advertise for a “mental health professional” and consider applicants from several registered professions — looking for the right combination of mental-health experience, clinical skills, cultural capability and registration rather than one specific title.

If you are an occupational therapist or social worker who searched your own job title and found little, this is often why. The role you are right for may not be advertised under your profession’s name. Search by the service, not just the title — and ask us, because we read the adverts every day.

Where the work happens

Fourteen kinds of service, three kinds of employer

Mental health work here is not confined to a mental health department. A psychologist might be in rehabilitation, persistent pain, dementia assessment or workplace health; a psychiatrist in old age, addiction, forensic, liaison, child and adolescent or general adult psychiatry. The mix of professions changes with the setting.

Private, NGO & independent

Smaller teams, and more say over your caseload

Different referral routes and funding, and often a caseload you shape yourself. Public or private is not the same job with a different employer — the setting changes your caseload, your team, your appointment structure, your autonomy and what a working week looks like.

Registration, pay and the visa

Who regulates this work?+

Each profession has its own regulator — named on its card above. Registration is the first gate and runs on its own timeline; the pathway checker gives you your route in about ten minutes.

What does the pay look like?+

Public-system pay is set by collective agreement rather than negotiated role by role, so on the same step the same job pays the same wherever in the country you do it. Private and ACC-funded work sits outside that and varies.

Occupational therapy has an indicative band on its guide. For psychiatry, psychology and nursing the agreements are mid-bargaining, so we confirm the current rate for the role rather than print a number that may be out of date. The bands we do publish are on What could I earn?

Which visa would I need?+

Most clinicians move on a work visa tied to an accredited employer, and some roles in this family sit on the Green List, which changes the route to residence considerably.

On the accredited employer route the visa can run in parallel with registration rather than after it; other routes differ.

What about the rest of the move?+

Schools, housing, healthcare, banking, shipping and the first few weeks are the same for every profession, so we explain them once rather than on each page.
