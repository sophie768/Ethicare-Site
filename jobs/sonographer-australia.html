What actually matters, in five minutes

Seven questions that decide whether this move is worth exploring. Open the ones that matter to you.

Can I work there?Yes, through accreditation

Sonographers don’t follow the Ahpra route. Two steps, in order: ASMIRT assesses your overseas qualification and experience and issues your Certificate of Recognition in Ultrasound, then the ASAR accredits you to practise and to scan under Medicare. ASAR also needs you to be a citizen, a permanent resident, or to hold a valid Australian working visa.

What about Ahpra?

Sonographers don't follow the Ahpra registration pathway — the route is ASMIRT → ASAR. Ahpra (through the MRPBA) registers professions like diagnostic radiography and radiation therapy.

What you'll need to apply

ASMIRT — the Australian Society of Medical Imaging and Radiation Therapy — is the gateway; get that assessment moving first. You'll need:

  • Your ultrasound qualification and full academic transcript
  • Evidence of recent clinical scanning practice (recency matters)
  • Professional references confirming roles, dates and modalities
  • English-language evidence, where your visa or employer requires it
What ASAR expects of you

Alongside your ASMIRT certificate, you'll need to be an Australian or New Zealand citizen, a permanent resident, or hold a valid Australian working visa. If you're accredited on a working visa, each year's renewal depends on showing ASAR a valid visa for that year.

There's also a recency rule: if your ASMIRT certificate is more than three years old, ASAR generally expects evidence of about a year part-time or six months full-time in clinical ultrasound within the last three years. If you can't meet that, its return-to-practice policy applies instead.

Pay & benefitsHow pay is set

Sonographers are among the best-paid imaging professionals here. Public pay is set state by state; the private market is where it climbs fastest.

Public sector

State enterprise agreements

Each state's public-health agreement sets base pay and progression. We confirm the current band, verified against the agreement, for every role.

Private market

Large & competitive

A very large private imaging sector with high scan volumes — often the strongest earning potential for experienced sonographers.

On top of base

Loadings, leave & super

Overtime, on-call and after-hours loadings, four weeks' annual leave as standard, and compulsory superannuation on top of pay.

We don't print a figure against a named role until it's confirmed with the employer — and whatever applies to relocation, we get in writing before you decide. Ask us for realistic, current pay.

Scope of practice, and the limits we're upfront about.

Scope of practice: general, cardiac and beyond

Most sonographers in Australia work autonomously across a broad case mix; many focus around one or more sub-specialities while keeping general scanning. General sonography covers abdominal, obstetric, gynaecological, musculoskeletal, vascular and small-parts scanning — the backbone of private imaging. Cardiac sonography (echocardiography) is a distinct area — transthoracic, transoesophageal and stress echo — typically needing a recognised echo qualification. Senior, lead and education roles offer a genuine career ladder beyond the scanning list.

Compared · Australia and New ZealandSame profession, different system

Sonography is where the two countries diverge most sharply, starting with the fact that only one of them regulates the title.

New Zealand registers sonographers as a health profession with a protected scope. Australia does not regulate the title; professional recognition and an accreditation register govern who can scan and whose scans attract funding. That one difference shapes the paperwork before you arrive, who can employ you, and how much say you have over your own practice once you are there.

Registration is a separate process in each country — never assume one follows from the other. The full comparison sits in Australia or New Zealand?, and the money side in Where will your salary go further?

Common questions

Frequently asked

Will my overseas qualification be recognised?

That depends entirely on where and what you studied. ASMIRT assesses your qualification against the Australian standard as it stood when you qualified, with three possible outcomes: unconditional recognition, conditional — further evidence or additional clinical experience first — or not recognised. Unlike radiography, there's no standard exam you can simply sit if it falls short; the ask may be further evidence, more clinical experience or further education. Send us your details before you commit to anything and we'll tell you plainly which is likely.

What about the visa and skills assessment?

Yes — ASAR won't accredit you without it, whichever visa you're on. It does double duty though: for a skilled-migration visa (189, 190 or 491) the same certificate supports the skills assessment Home Affairs requires. Most of the sonographers we place move on an employer-sponsored 482 instead, where the employer sponsors the role and you work toward accreditation alongside. We work with licensed migration advisers either way.

How does it compare to New Zealand?

Australia's market is much larger; New Zealand has an acute shortage and unusually strong public pay. We set the two side by side above, and the full country comparison is in Australia or New Zealand?.
