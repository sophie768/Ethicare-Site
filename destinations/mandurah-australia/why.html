Why Mandurah

Mandurah Guide · Chapter 2 of 9

Mandurah earns a look for a specific kind of move: coast and space at a lower cost than Perth, with the capital still close enough to use. It is not for everyone, and the honest trade-offs matter as much as the appeal.

The case for Mandurah

The draw is a genuine waterside life — estuary, canals and ocean — at a price Perth’s coastal suburbs no longer offer. The city is growing, so housing and services are being built rather than stretched, and the family estates north and south are among the more affordable new housing within reach of a capital.

For work, the Peel region carries a real community and outpatient caseload, and private imaging has a steady presence. And because the train reaches central Perth in under an hour, a role in the city while living on the coast is a legitimate plan rather than a stretch.

What you give up

The trade is clinical breadth. The complex, high-acuity and subspecialty work concentrates in Perth’s tertiary hospitals, so if that is the centre of your career, Mandurah is a base near it rather than a substitute for it. You also take on a hot, dry summer, a car-dependent everyday outside the rail corridor, and a city still growing into itself — some services and culture are thinner than a capital’s.

Mandurah suits you if…
  • You want the coast and the estuary without a Perth coastal-suburb price.
  • Your work is community, outpatient or imaging-based — or you are happy to commute to Perth for it.
  • You are moving with family and want space, newer housing and the outdoors close by.
  • You value a slower, water-centred life over the density and options of a capital.
The honest version

Mandurah trades clinical breadth for space, coast and cost. If Perth is close enough to use when you need it, that can be a very good trade.
