Welcome to the ACT

A warm welcome, you’re in good hands

Deciding to move your life and career to the other side of the world is a big step, and an exciting one. Whether Canberra is already home to people you know or a brand-new chapter, I want you to feel supported from the very first conversation.

The ACT is Canberra: Australia’s planned 'bush capital’, a green, spacious, easy-to-navigate city with the highest incomes and some of the best services in the country, ringed by hills and never far from the snow or the coast. It offers big-city careers with small-city calm.

This guide walks you through healthcare, visas and registration, finances, housing, schooling and cost of living across the state, then breaks down the main places you might live so you can picture daily life in each. Think of it as the conversation we’d have over coffee, in your pocket, whenever you need it.

Welcome to the Australian Capital Territory. We’re so glad you’re here, and we’ll be alongside you every step of the way.

The Australian Capital Territory is essentially one planned city, Canberra, home to around 470,000 people, purpose-built as the national capital amid hills, lakes and bushland, with the highest median income in the country.

Ethicare insight

Candidates often picture Australia as one job market. It isn’t. The ACT registers nationally through Ahpra but hires locally through Canberra Health Services, and while it’s a smaller system, it’s well-resourced and serves a wide surrounding region of NSW.

Overview · Culture

Ancient country, planned capital

The Ngunnawal people are the traditional custodians of the Canberra region, part of one of the oldest living cultures on earth, a heritage increasingly recognised in the life of the national capital.

Canberra is young, planned and green, designed around Lake Burley Griffin and the bush, and it’s home to the nation’s great cultural institutions, the National Gallery, the War Memorial, the museums and Parliament itself. Despite its 'public-service town’ reputation, it has a lively food, wine and festival scene (Floriade, Enlighten) and an outdoorsy, active culture.

Sophie’s tip

Take time to learn about Ngunnawal Country and the story of the planned capital. Canberrans are proud of both, and it helps you feel part of the city faster.

Overview · Transport

Getting around

Canberra is spacious and easy to get around, with quick commutes, a growing light-rail line and good cycling. A car is handy, but distances within the city are short.

MyWay & light rail

Buses and the light-rail line run on the tap-on MyWay card, with light rail linking the city to Gungahlin and extending south.

Driving

Australians drive on the left. Canberra is famously easy to drive and park, and its wide roads and roundabouts make for quick commutes.

Cycling

Canberra has one of the best cycle-path networks in the country, many people commute by bike.

Flying

Canberra Airport links to the major capitals; the NSW south coast and the Snowy Mountains are an easy drive.

Sophie’s tip

You can usually drive on a valid overseas licence for a period after arriving, but you’ll need to transfer to an ACT licence once you’re a resident. Grab a MyWay card in your first week, and consider a bike, Canberra is built for cycling.

Overview · Climate

A year in the Canberra climate

Canberra has a crisp, four-season continental climate, warm, dry summers and cold, frosty winters, more extreme than the coastal cities nearby. The seasons are flipped from the Northern Hemisphere.

Ethicare insight

Two things catch newcomers out. Canberra winters are genuinely cold, with hard frosts and fog, so plan for heating and warm clothing, a point worth making to family. And despite the cool, the summer UV is strong, along with a real bushfire-season awareness in the surrounding country, worth understanding early.

Sophie’s View

Why I like the ACT

One of the things I love most about Canberra is how much it quietly gets right. It pairs excellent hospitals and the highest incomes in the country with short commutes, four real seasons and bush, lake and gallery all on your doorstep, and the snow and the coast barely two hours away. For healthcare professionals, it’s a rare combination of a serious career and a genuinely calm, balanced life.

Having spent many years in the NHS, I know how easily work can take over. What stands out to me about Canberra is how little time you lose to a commute, and how easy it is to build a life around the seasons, autumn colour, winter frosts and the snow, spring at Floriade, summer by the lake. If I could give one piece of advice, it would be to pick the district that puts your hospital campus and your family’s life within an easy reach. Here, that’s very achievable.

Section two · Healthcare

Healthcare in the ACT

The ACT runs a compact, well-resourced public health system, universal Medicare cover, a tertiary hospital and supporting hospitals, and a private sector, serving Canberra and a large surrounding region of southern NSW.

Go deeperUnderstanding healthcare in Australia→

Canberra Hospital

The territory’s flagship tertiary hospital, in Garran, with major emergency, trauma, intensive-care and specialist services, and a new critical-services building.

Centenary Hospital

Women and children’s services on the Canberra Hospital campus: maternity, neonatal intensive care and paediatrics.

North Canberra Hospital

In Bruce (Belconnen): emergency, acute and sub-acute care for the city’s north.

University of Canberra Hospital

A dedicated rehabilitation and sub-acute hospital, also in Bruce.

Good to know

Eligibility for Medicare usually begins once you hold an appropriate visa. Reciprocal Health Care Agreements give some countries limited Medicare access. The ACT system is compact but well-resourced, and it serves not just Canberra but a wide catchment across surrounding southern NSW.

Registration · Ahpra

Professional registration

Most healthcare professions are regulated nationally through Ahpra, the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency, and its profession-specific boards.

One national register

Once registered with Ahpra you can work anywhere in Australia, including all of the ACT, registration is national, not state-by-state.

Qualification assessment

Overseas qualifications are assessed against Australian standards; some roles require bridging programs or exams.

English language

You’ll usually need to evidence English proficiency through an approved test or a recognised pathway.

Start early

Registration takes time, begin gathering documents and references well before you plan to travel.

Ethicare insight

Even in compact Canberra, the main cause of a delayed start is documents gathered too late. Certified qualifications, references and ID take longer than expected, so begin the moment the ACT feels like a real option.

Section four · Banking & Finance

Setting up your finances

Getting your finances in order early makes day-to-day life much easier. Most of this can be started before you arrive.

Opening a bank account

The major banks are CommBank, Westpac, NAB and ANZ, and many offer accounts you can open online before you arrive, then verify ID in branch with your passport. ActewAGL and local credit unions also serve the region.

Tax File Number (TFN)

Every worker needs a TFN from the ATO. Apply online once you arrive and give the number to payroll so you’re taxed correctly. Without it, you’ll be taxed at the highest rate.

Sophie’s tip

Open your bank account before you fly where you can, having an account ready makes your payroll setup and first rent payment far smoother in those hectic first weeks.

The money set-up, in order

1Activate your bank account, verify ID in branch, order a debit card and online banking.
2Apply for your TFN, online via the ATO, then give it to payroll immediately.
3Choose your super fund, or accept the employer default, then review later.
4Set up salary packaging, ask payroll or your provider on day one if you’re in public health.
5Register for Medicare, if eligible under your visa or a reciprocal agreement.
6Get a local SIM & number, many banking and government steps need an Australian mobile.
Sophie’s tip

Salary is only half the picture, even on Canberra’s high incomes. The people who settle most smoothly budget for the bridge, the few weeks between arriving and your first full pay clearing. Think about temporary accommodation, a rental bond (typically four weeks’ rent), a car, and setting up a home. Canberra incomes are high, but so are some costs, so planning still pays off.

Sophie Careem · Founder & Director

Section seven · Housing & Areas

Finding a place to live

Canberra’s housing is spacious and well-planned but pricier than most of the country. Where you live is about which district suits you, and in a planned city, everything is close.

Canberra’s rental market is competitive and on the pricier side. You’ll usually apply with ID, references and proof of income, then pay a bond (typically four weeks’ rent) lodged with the ACT bond scheme. Many newcomers rent short-term first to learn the districts. The main sites are realestate.com.au (largest) and allhomes.com.au (a Canberra favourite).

Choosing your area, the order that saves the most stress

1Fix your work site first. Everything flows from your commute, decide it before you fall for a suburb.
2Check school catchments if you have children. Local intake is guaranteed; out-of-area isn’t.
3Shortlist two or three areas and rent short-term in one before committing.
4Weigh new vs established. The north is newer and better value; the inner suburbs are leafier and central.

Utilities & getting set up

Power & gas

Choose a retailer (ActewAGL is the main local provider, plus Origin, EnergyAustralia and others). Set up on move-in.

Water

Icon Water; if renting, most water charges are the landlord’s.

Internet

NBN via Telstra, Optus, TPG or Aussie Broadband.

Setting up home

Canberra has all the big retailers, IKEA, Bunnings, Kmart, plus Marketplace for second-hand.

Sophie’s tip

No Australian rental references? Bring landlord references from home, proof of income and savings, and your job offer as clean PDFs. In Canberra’s competitive market, a complete, instant application stands out, and locals swear by Allhomes.

Housing & family · Choosing where to live

Choosing where to live

For many healthcare professionals, the job is only half the decision. The other half is where your family will settle well. Schools, commute, housing cost and lifestyle are best weighed together. Here’s a practical way to think it through.

Schools often come first

Access to a government school is usually tied to where you live. Popular schools can be pinned to an exact address, catchments can change street by street, and out-of-area places aren’t guaranteed. If you have children, check the catchment before you sign a long-term lease. Independent, Catholic and faith schools offer more flexibility, but usually charge fees.

Your commute still matters

Canberra is compact and easy to move around, with quick commutes, a growing light-rail line and the country’s best cycle paths, so many people don’t rely on a car. Think about your shift times, which district puts you closest to your hospital campus (Woden for the main campus, Bruce for the north), and parking. Nowhere here is far.

The order that usually works best

A simple sequence that keeps the big decisions in the right order.

  1. 1Confirm your likely work site.
  2. 2Shortlist suitable school options if you have children.
  3. 3Check the catchment maps for those schools.
  4. 4Compare commute times to work at shift hours.
  5. 5Review rental availability and cost.
  6. 6Rent short-term first where you can.
  7. 7Choose a longer-term suburb once you know the area.

Section eight · Shopping & Food

Groceries, food & community

The essentials in one place, the big chains, the budget picks, and where Canberra’s international community finds the flavours of home, this is a surprisingly cosmopolitan city.

The big chains

Woolworths & Coles for full-range everywhere; ALDI for the cheapest weekly shop; IGA and independents for neighbourhood top-ups, vital in regional towns.

Flavours of home

The Asian food precincts of Dickson and Woden; specialty importers across the districts for South African, British, Indian and Middle-Eastern staples; and excellent fresh-produce and farmers’ markets around the city.

Sophie’s tip

Bring your own bags, single-use plastic bags are banned in the ACT, and most people keep a few reusable ones in the car for exactly this reason.

Good to know

For its size Canberra is cosmopolitan, thanks to its diplomatic and international community. Halal groceries and a wide range of Asian, Indian and Middle-Eastern ingredients are easy to find (Dickson, Woden, Belconnen); kosher supplies are more limited and sometimes brought in from Sydney. The farmers’ markets are excellent for local produce.

Faith & community · Places of worship

Religion & places of worship

Australia has no state religion and law protects freedom of belief. Canberra is diverse and international: at the last census around half of residents reported no religion, Christianity remained the largest faith, and Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism and Judaism are all established, supported by the city’s diplomatic and university communities.

Most communities list service and prayer times online and warmly welcome newcomers, often one of the quickest ways to build a support network after you arrive.

The main faiths & where to find them

Christianity

Catholic, Anglican, Uniting, Orthodox and many other churches across the districts, including the national churches near the parliamentary zone.

Islam

The Canberra Islamic Centre and the Canberra Mosque serve a well-established Muslim community across the city.

Hinduism

The Canberra Hindu Temple and cultural associations serve a growing community, with festivals through the year.

Buddhism

Several temples and meditation centres reflect Canberra’s diverse Asian communities.

Sikhism

Gurdwaras in and around Canberra welcome worshippers and visitors, with community kitchens (langar) open to all.

Judaism

The ACT Jewish Community centre serves the city’s Jewish community, with services and cultural activities.

Faith & community · Diversity & inclusion

Diversity, inclusion & belonging

The Australian Capital Territory is diverse, international and inclusive, and the law protects everyone’s right to be treated equally. Whoever you are, and whoever your family is, you’re welcome here.

Go deeperReligion, community & belonging in Australia→

A multicultural home

Canberra is highly educated and international, home to embassies, universities and communities from around the world, with roughly a third of residents born overseas. The city’s multicultural festivals and community organisations make it easy for newcomers to connect and belong.

Your rights in law

The ACT Discrimination Act 1991, the Human Rights Act 2004 (one of Australia’s first) and federal law protect people from discrimination on the basis of race, religion, age, sex, disability, sexuality and gender identity. The ACT Human Rights Commission handles complaints.

LGBTQ+ inclusion

Same-sex marriage has been legal Australia-wide since 2017, and Canberra has long been one of the country’s most inclusive cities, an early mover on equality and a ban on conversion practices. SpringOUT and Canberra’s Pride events feature, and QLife (1800 184 527) offers free peer support.

Inclusive workplaces & care

A growing number of health services hold Rainbow Tick accreditation or run active inclusion networks. Interpreters are free through TIS National (131 450), and the NDIS supports people with disability.

Ethicare insight

Canberra Health Services and most employers have active diversity and inclusion commitments, and many run staff networks for culturally diverse, First Nations and LGBTQ+ workers. If belonging at work matters to you, tell us, we can factor a team’s culture and inclusion record into where we suggest you land.

Section ten · Education & Family

Schooling & family life

The Australian Capital Territory offers a full range of early childhood education, schools and universities, run by the ACT Education Directorate. The school year runs late January to mid-December across four terms, a flip from the Northern Hemisphere calendar worth planning around.

Go deeperEducation & schools in Australia→

Early childhood

Long daycare, kindergarten and family day care for ages 0–5, with subsidies.

Primary

The first years of school through to the end of primary, across government, Catholic and independent schools.

Secondary

The senior years, ending with the ACT Senior Secondary Certificate, leading to university or training.

Tertiary

The Australian National University (ANU), the University of Canberra and others, plus the Canberra Institute of Technology (CIT).

Public school fees for visa-holder families

The ACT welcomes visa families into public schools; many 482 families are exempt from fees, though where they apply they can be higher than elsewhere.

Ethicare insight

The ACT is a mixed picture on school fees. The good news is that many clinicians are exempt: dependants of 482 visa holders in occupations on the Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List can qualify for a fee exemption by providing the employment contract that matches the approved occupation on the visa grant. Where a fee does apply, the ACT’s rates are among the highest in the country, so it’s worth confirming the current figures with the Education Directorate and checking your occupation carefully, we can help you work through it.

School catchments come first

For ACT government schools, your home address usually determines your local-intake (priority) school, children inside the zone are generally guaranteed a place, while out-of-area places depend on capacity, and popular schools can be oversubscribed. Boundaries can run street-by-street, so always check the exact address before you sign.

For families, the school decision usually leads the housing decision here, deciding it first prevents an expensive mid-year move.

Education & Family · Partners & work

Employment for your partner

A move only works if it works for the whole family. The good news: on most skilled and employer-sponsored visas your partner is included with full work rights, free to take any job, full or part-time, with any employer.

Work rights come as standard

Partners added to a 482, 186, or skilled 189/190/491 visa generally have unrestricted work rights, no separate employer sponsorship needed. Always confirm the exact conditions on your grant letter.

A broad job market

Canberra has one of the strongest, most stable job markets in the country, dominated by the public service but with real demand in healthcare, education, universities, technology, defence and professional services, and low unemployment.

Helping your partner land work

1Reformat the CV to Australian style, concise, achievement-led, no photo or date of birth. Set up a strong LinkedIn with an Australian location.
2Search the main boards, SEEK, Indeed and Jora, plus the ACT Government jobs site, APSJobs (federal public service) and health-service pages for public-sector roles.
3Check if the profession is regulated, teaching, nursing, trades and others need registration or a skills assessment. Start it before you arrive.
4Register with recruitment agencies, many Canberra roles are filled through agencies, who can also advise on local expectations and pay.
