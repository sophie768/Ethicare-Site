Whether you can

Can we hire internationally at all?

Almost always yes, if it is a role a board will register and the timeline is workable. Four things decide it.

01 · The gate

Registration decides everything after it

Whether the clinician can register sets the visa, the start date and the offer. We check it first.

See the registration checker →
02 · The route

The visa follows the pathway

Once the board route is clear, the visa follows a known process. We coordinate it; the decision stays with the immigration authority.

03 · The clock

A likely timeline

Offer to arrival is typically five to nine months for the professions we cover.

04 · The fine print

Supervision and accredited posts

Some boards expect supervised practice or an accredited post first. Knowing before you advertise avoids an appointment that cannot start.

Whether they stay

Will they stay?

Retention is a relocation question, and it is the work we are built around.

“Sophie always puts candidate care first, and has invested heavily in preparing candidates for a successful move — ensuring they have as much support as possible when relocating to a new country.”

Maree LoynesFormer Allied Health Recruitment Specialist, Health New Zealand MidCentral

A regional allied health role that had stayed open

Challenge, search and outcome

Local advertising hadn’t moved it. A small, targeted search turned on the board, the partner’s work and a realistic timeline for a family. The clinician registered, relocated and is still in post.

Why it holds: we don’t stop at arrival

The first year is where a placement is won or lost, so we stay in contact through settling in: the bank account, the GP, the school place. Included for clinicians we place; available by arrangement for your direct hires.
