Moving to New Zealand

Dunedin is the cheapest main centre in New Zealand to live in, and the gap is not marginal. Because Health New Zealand pays national agreement rates, the salary on your contract is the same one you would earn in Auckland — it simply buys considerably more here.

Why there are no prices on this page

We used to print a weekly rent and a monthly budget table here. We have taken them out. A figure that was true when a page was written is misleading a year later, and a candidate who plans a move around a stale number is worse off than one who plans around none.

What does not go stale is the shape of a month, and the judgement about which lines will surprise you — which is what the rest of this chapter is for. For today’s actual numbers use the calculators at the foot of the page, and use them to compare Dunedin against the city you are leaving. That is the only comparison that decides anything.

The three numbers that matter

Housing
The headline. Rents and house prices are well below Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch. A family home in a good suburb costs what a small flat costs further north.
Transport
Short commutes, cheap parking outside the hospital campus, and many households running one car instead of two.
Power
The one that goes the other way. Winter heating in an older house is a real line in the budget, and the item newcomers most consistently underestimate.

What things cost

Indicative figures that change over time. Use them as a shape, not a quote.

The winter power bill

This deserves its own heading because it catches almost everybody. A poorly insulated older house in Dunedin can cost two or three times as much to heat in July as it does to run in February. The same house, properly insulated with an efficient heat pump, costs a fraction of that.

The Healthy Homes standards have raised the floor for rentals considerably, which helps. But if you are buying, treat insulation, double glazing and heating as part of the purchase price rather than a later project. It is the best money you will spend here.

Ask for the numbers
Landlords and vendors can usually produce recent power bills. Ask for a July one specifically, not an average.
Look for the retrofit
Ceiling and underfloor insulation, a heat pump, and double glazing in the main rooms. A villa with all three is a genuinely warm house.
Sun is free heating
A north-facing living room does more for your winter than any appliance. This is why aspect matters so much here.

The student effect

Twenty thousand students distort the rental market in a way worth understanding. Demand peaks sharply in late January and February as the academic year begins, and slumps from November. Family homes in the hill and coastal suburbs are less affected than flats near the university — but if you can time your search for December, you will have more choice and less competition.

Tax, KiwiSaver, banking and sending money home are national topics, covered in Money, tax & banking.

The bill that catches people out

Heating is the Dunedin budget line that surprises people, and it has almost nothing to do with how cold the city is. Dunedin winters are cooler than the North Island but not extreme; the issue is the housing stock.

The city has a large inventory of Victorian and Edwardian villas — beautiful, generously proportioned, and frequently built with single glazing, no wall insulation and high ceilings. A house like that costs several times more to keep warm than a modern equivalent, and the difference shows up on every winter bill for as long as you live there.

The practical advice: when you view a property, ask when it was last insulated, whether it is double glazed, and what the heating is. A well-upgraded villa is a wonderful thing to live in. An un-upgraded one is the single most common regret we hear from people who move here.

Three costs people forget

Flights home
Every long-haul journey from Dunedin involves at least one connection, usually two. Budget both the fare and the extra half-day each way.
Adult dental
Private in New Zealand, as everywhere. A check-up runs into three figures, and work beyond that adds up quickly.
Setting up
Bond and rent in advance, a car, whiteware and furniture — plus, here, whatever it takes to make an old house warm. Most people spend more than they planned in the first two months.
Three costs people forget
  • Flights home. Dunedin Airport has domestic and limited trans-Tasman connections; long-haul means Auckland, Christchurch or Sydney. Add half a day each way.
  • Heating. The single biggest local budget line. An old villa without insulation can cost several times what a modern house does to keep warm.
  • Adult dental is private, as everywhere in New Zealand. Register early rather than in an emergency.
The short version

The same national salary against the lowest housing costs in the country — provided you spend some of the difference on keeping the house warm.

Get today’s numbers, and compare them to home

These are live and updated continuously, which is why we send you to them rather than competing with them. Put the city you are leaving in the other box.

  • Numbeo cost-of-living calculator — the most complete comparison tool, and the one most clinicians already use. Crowd-sourced, so read it as a ballpark rather than a quote.
  • Expatistan — the same idea with a cleaner side-by-side. Worth running as a sanity check when Numbeo looks off.
  • Tenancy Services market rent — official bond-lodgement data by area, and the most reliable rent figure in New Zealand.
  • Stats NZ — official, slower, and the one to trust on the direction of travel rather than any single price.
