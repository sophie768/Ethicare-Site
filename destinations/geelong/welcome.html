Geelong is the city that gives Melbourne a coastline of its own: Victoria’s second city on Corio Bay, home to the only tertiary hospital in the state based outside Melbourne, with the Great Ocean Road starting at its edge. Here it is in nine numbers, then six things nobody tells you until you have moved.

A wool port that became a knowledge city

Around 270,000 people live across Greater Geelong, on Corio Bay about 75 kilometres south-west of Melbourne. It grew rich on wool in the nineteenth century — the reason for the grand civic architecture still standing in the centre — then on manufacturing through the twentieth: car assembly, an aluminium smelter and an oil refinery all operated here in turn. Within the last fifteen years all three closed, and the city rebuilt itself around health, education and the public sector instead. Two state government agencies moved their head offices to the waterfront, Deakin University expanded, and the local health service grew into Victoria’s largest regional provider.

For clinicians the proposition is specific. The region’s principal referral hospital is the only tertiary hospital in Victoria based outside metropolitan Melbourne, drawing a primary catchment of around 350,000 people and extending to roughly 500,000 for some specialist services. You get genuine tertiary breadth in a city of a quarter of a million, with Melbourne itself an hour up the road when you want it.

The honest costs: a specialist bench that, while real, is still smaller than a Melbourne teaching hospital’s; a summer that brings the whole of Melbourne to the local beaches; and a wind off the bay that takes some getting used to. This guide prices all three plainly.

Six things you only learn once you are here

The wind has opinions
Geelong is genuinely breezier than Melbourne, exposed to both the bay and Bass Strait. A hot northerly followed by a cool change can move thirty degrees in an hour.
It is a Cats town
The Geelong Football Club runs deep here. Expect the city to notice, loudly, in September.
“Pako” is a place, not a joke
Pakington Street in Newtown is the café and shopping strip locals just call Pako. You will too, within a month.
The referral role is bigger than the city suggests
Case mix arrives from across the Barwon South West, not from Geelong’s population alone.
Old industry left a waterfront, not a scar
Where wool stores and a refinery once stood is now parkland, apartments and government offices. People still talk about “when Ford was here.”
Summer means visitors
The Bellarine and the Surf Coast are Melbourne’s nearest beach escape, so the roads and coastal towns get genuinely busy from Boxing Day to late January.
A note on Country
  • Geelong sits on the land of the Wadawurrung people, one of the five nations of the Kulin alliance.
  • Acknowledgement of Country opens meetings and events across the region, as it does across Victoria.
