Welcome to Christchurch

Kia ora, and a warm welcome to Aotearoa

Moving to the other side of the world is a big step — and choosing Christchurch means arriving in a city that has remade itself with real heart. Ōtautahi is the South Island’s largest city and its gateway: a flatter, greener, more walkable place than ever, with the Southern Alps, the ski fields and the coast all close by.

You won’t be doing this alone. New Zealanders are known for their warmth and manaakitanga — hospitality and care for others — and Christchurch pairs that with the best value of the big cities, so families get the space they want. Its hospital offers some of the most significant clinical work in the country.

This guide is written to make your move smooth and unhurried — from the health system, IRD numbers and KiwiSaver to the suburbs, the school zones and where to spend your first free weekend. Think of it as the conversation we’d have over a flat white, in your pocket for whenever you need it.

Nau mai, haere mai ki Aotearoa — welcome to New Zealand. We can’t wait to see you settle in.

Section one · Overview

Modern, green and rebuilt with heart

Few cities have remade themselves like Ōtautahi. What followed the earthquakes is a flatter, greener, more walkable city — full of bold new architecture, open spaces and one of the most liveable centres in the country, on the edge of the South Island’s mountains and sea.

Overview · A city reborn

Bold new architecture, heritage alongside

The rebuilt centre is designed around the Ōtākaro Avon, with riverside dining, laneways and public squares. Heritage sits alongside it — the pastel shopfronts of New Regent Street, the restored stone of the Arts Centre, and the historic tram looping through it all.

Ōtautahi is the heartland of Ngāi Tahu, the principal iwi of the South Island, and that story is woven through the city’s parks, place names and public art. Landmark civic buildings like Tūranga, the central library on Cathedral Square, have made the centre a genuinely exciting place again.

The result is a city that feels young and optimistic — proud of what it has rebuilt, and easy to fall for once you’re in it.

Overview · Transport

Getting around

Christchurch is famously flat and easy. The city spreads across the Canterbury Plains, so commutes are short, cycling is a joy, and the whole South Island opens up from here by road, rail and air.

Driving

Flat, grid-like roads make driving simple, and parking is easier than up north. New Zealanders drive on the left.

Cycling & buses

A growing network of cycleways and the Metro bus network (tap on with a Metrocard) make car-free days easy.

Air

Christchurch Airport is the South Island’s main hub, with domestic and international flights.

The South Island

The Alps, the lakes and the coast are all within a day’s drive; the TranzAlpine train is one of the world’s great rail journeys.

Overview · Climate

Four real seasons

Christchurch has a drier, more temperate climate than the North Island, with four distinct seasons: warm, dry summers, crisp autumns, cold clear winters with frosts (and occasional snow), and bright springs. The warm nor’west wind is a local feature.

Section two · Healthcare

A major hospital and tertiary care

Christchurch has the largest healthcare network in the South Island, delivered by Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora — and, for you, some of the most significant tertiary clinical work in the country, serving the whole southern half of New Zealand.

Christchurch Hospital

One of New Zealand’s largest tertiary hospitals — acute, surgical, specialist and emergency care for the wider South Island, within Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora.

Christchurch Women’s

The region’s specialist maternity, neonatal and gynaecology hospital, alongside the main campus.

Burwood Hospital

Nationally significant rehabilitation, spinal and older-persons’ health services.

A private option

Private hospitals in the city offer elective procedures and shorter waits alongside the public system.

Healthcare · Everyday care

GPs, pharmacies & urgent care

Registering with a GP & dentist

Once you settle in, enrol with a local GP. Enrolment means subsidised visits — typically around NZ$50–$70, more as a casual patient. Many practices allow online enrolment once you have an address. Dental care is private (roughly NZ$90–$150 for a check-up).

Pharmacies

Pharmacies are found throughout the city and suburbs. Prescriptions are partly subsidised once you’re enrolled with a GP — commonly a small standard charge per item.

Emergency & after-hours
·Emergency Department — for urgent or critical care; in an emergency, dial 111.
·Urgent care clinics — 24-hour surgery and accident & medical clinics across the city.
·Healthline 0800 611 116 — free 24/7 advice from registered nurses.
Section three · Banking & Finance

Setting up your finances

Getting your finances in order early makes day-to-day life much easier. Most of this can be started before you arrive.

Go deeperSalary & pay in New Zealand healthcare→

Opening a bank account

The major banks are ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank and Westpac. You’ll usually need your passport, work or resident visa, and proof of a NZ address. Many banks let you start online before arrival, then activate in person — ask for online banking and a debit card at the same time.

Tax — your IRD number

Every worker needs an IRD number from Inland Revenue. Apply online once you have a NZ address and bank account; it usually takes around 7–10 working days. Give the number to payroll as soon as it arrives so the correct tax is applied. Wages are commonly paid fortnightly.

Sophie’s tip

Start your bank account application before you fly. Having an account number ready makes your IRD application and payroll setup far smoother in those hectic first weeks.

KiwiSaver & sending money home

KiwiSaver is a government-supported retirement savings scheme. On a work visa you can usually opt in or out. Contributions are a percentage of your pay (commonly 3%, 4%, 6%, 8% or 10%), with employers contributing at least 3%. You can withdraw your funds if you permanently leave New Zealand.

Sending money overseas: banks can transfer internationally, but specialist services such as Wise or OFX are often cheaper and faster. Always compare the exchange rate and the fees before moving larger amounts.

Section five · Housing & Areas

Finding a place to live

Christchurch offers everything from central apartments and leafy heritage suburbs to new family homes and beachside living — much of it newer and better insulated since the rebuild. Where you live shapes your commute, though few here are long; if you have children, check the school zone before you sign.

Most rentals are advertised online and move quickly in the popular suburbs. The main sites are trademe.co.nz/property (NZ’s largest rental marketplace) and realestate.co.nz (nationwide rentals and homes for sale). Many people rent for a few months first to get to know the areas before committing.

A guide to the areas

Central city

Rebuilt apartments and townhouses around the Avon and the laneways — walkable and lively, ideal without a car.

Merivale, Fendalton & Ilam

Leafy, sought-after northwestern suburbs with heritage homes, good schools and the university nearby.

Riccarton & Addington

Central, well-connected and handy suburbs with shopping, dining and easy access to the hospital.

Cashmere & the Port Hills

Elevated suburbs with views over the city and plains, and walking tracks from the doorstep.

Sumner & the coast

Seaside living at Sumner and New Brighton — beaches, cafés and a village feel within reach of town.

Rolleston & Rangiora

Fast-growing satellite towns just out of the city, popular with families for newer homes and value.

A tip on choosing

Because the city is flat and compact, you can choose on schools and lifestyle rather than commute. Ask about heating and insulation — newer, rebuilt homes are warm and dry, which matters in a Christchurch winter. Check any school zone before you sign.

Housing · Everyday services

Utilities, rates & rubbish

Power & gas

Choose from providers like Genesis, Mercury, Meridian and Contact. Compare rates on Powerswitch (powerswitch.org.nz).

Water & rates

Council rates fund local services; if you rent, rates are usually included in your rent.

Heat pumps are standard; fibre broadband is widespread. Spark, One NZ and 2degrees are the main providers.

Section six · Shopping & Food

Where to shop

Christchurch offers a rebuilt central retail quarter, big suburban malls and lively weekend markets. A few of the main ones:

The central city & Riverside

The rebuilt retail precinct and the Riverside Market — fresh food, eateries and independent shops in the heart of town.

Westfield Riccarton

The city’s largest mall, with major stores, dining and a cinema, close to the centre.

The Palms & Northlands

Big suburban malls serving the east and north, plus Hornby and Barrington to the south and west.

Weekend markets

The Riccarton Rotary and Lyttelton Farmers’ markets are weekend institutions for produce and local makers.

Supermarkets & groceries

New Zealand’s supermarkets fall into a few main groups — knowing the difference helps you balance budget and convenience.

Pak’nSaveThe cheapest of the big chains — a no-frills, bulk-buy warehouse format. Best for budget shopping.
WoolworthsMid-range and widespread, with strong online shopping and delivery (formerly Countdown).
New WorldMore upmarket, with a wider fresh and specialty range.
Four Square & FreshChoiceSmaller neighbourhood stores for convenience and top-ups.

Pak’nSave, Woolworths and New World are all across the city, with Costco at Northwood for bulk shopping. The Canterbury Plains are prime farming country, so local produce and meat are superb. Bring your own bags — single-use plastic bags are banned.

A taste of home

Halal & South Asian. Riccarton and the central city have halal butchers and Indian, Middle-Eastern and Sri Lankan grocers; many supermarket lines are halal-processed — check the label.

Asian. Riccarton Road is the city’s Asian food hub, with Chinese, Korean, Japanese and South-East Asian supermarkets and restaurants.

British & European. Specialty importers and the larger supermarkets stock familiar brands from home.

Faith & community · Places of worship

Christchurch reflects New Zealand’s spiritual diversity, with welcoming communities across many faiths — Christian churches of many denominations, several mosques, Hindu mandirs and Sikh gurdwaras, Buddhist temples and an established Jewish community. Most list service times online, and many are among the quickest ways to build a support network when you arrive.

Section eight · Education & Family

Schooling & study

Christchurch offers a full range of early childhood education, schools and tertiary study, including several long-established state and integrated schools. The school year runs late January to mid-December, in four terms with two-week breaks in April, July and October.

Go deeperEducation & schools in New Zealand→

Early Childhood (ECE)

Kindergartens, daycare, Montessori, kōhanga reo and home-based care for ages 0–5. Government 20-hours funding applies for ages 3–5.

Primary & intermediate

State, state-integrated and private schools for Years 1–8 across the city and satellite towns.

Secondary

Years 9–13, working towards NCEA, the national qualification, with several well-known colleges.

Tertiary

The University of Canterbury and Ara Institute of Canterbury offer degree and vocational study in the city.

School zones come first

In New Zealand, where you live can determine which state school your children attend. For many families, the school decision leads the choice of where to rent or buy.

In-zone = guaranteed

Children living inside a school’s enrolment zone are guaranteed a place.

Out-of-zone = ballot

You can still apply, but popular schools run a ballot for limited spots.

Check the address

Zones can be specific — confirm the exact address is in-zone.

The order that works best: shortlist schools you like → check each school’s zone map → search for rentals inside that zone → confirm the address is in-zone before you sign.

Christchurch for families

Beyond school and work, Christchurch is a spacious, green, easy place to raise a family — parks and playgrounds everywhere, beaches and the Port Hills close by, and the mountains for winter weekends. Bigger homes for the money and short commutes give families more time together. Children often settle fastest through school, sport and local clubs.
