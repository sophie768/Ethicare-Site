The honest summary: a car makes life easier, but Geelong is also the most train-connected regional city in Victoria, and some households manage well without a second one.

Inside the city

Most trips across Geelong take ten to twenty minutes. A local bus network covers the suburbs, and the train line itself has local stops that double as inner-city transport. Parking is easier than Melbourne, though not as effortless as a small country town.

Cycling works well along the waterfront and the Barwon River trails, and Geelong’s flat inner geography makes it a genuinely rideable city outside the windiest days.

Getting to Melbourne

By road
About an hour up the Princes Freeway, dual carriageway the whole way. A normal commute for some, and the reason a Melbourne evening is an easy thing to do.
By train
A frequent direct service runs to Melbourne’s Southern Cross Station in roughly sixty to seventy-five minutes — genuinely commutable, and some Geelong residents do it daily.
By air
Avalon Airport sits between Geelong and Melbourne with limited domestic and seasonal international services. For most international travel you will use Melbourne’s main airport, about fifty minutes away.
By coach
Regional coach services connect Geelong to the Surf Coast, the Otways and towns further west, useful if you are without a car.

The car question, answered plainly

Useful, not always essential. If you live centrally and work at the hospital, the train line and local buses can genuinely cover daily life. A car earns its keep on weekends — the Bellarine, the Great Ocean Road and the wine regions are not well served by public transport, and that is where Geelong’s real lifestyle argument lives.

Licence conversion, importing a vehicle, insurance and registration all work the same way across Australia and are covered once in Living in Australia. Nothing about it is specific to Geelong.

First-month practicalities
  • If you plan to live centrally and use the train, confirm your actual commute time at your real start time, not the timetable’s best case.
  • Check whether your accommodation has off-street parking — it varies more in the inner suburbs than further out.
  • If you are commuting from the Bellarine, drive the route once at your real start time before you commit to a lease.
  • Register for the Melbourne rail service early if you expect regular trips — peak services fill.
The short version

Geelong is the rare regional city where you can genuinely manage without a second car. Fifteen minutes to anything local, an hour to Melbourne by train — and the car earns its keep on Bellarine and Great Ocean Road weekends, not the daily commute.
