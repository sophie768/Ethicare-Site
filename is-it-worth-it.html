The second question

Is it worth it?

Nobody asks this out loud very often, but almost everyone is working it out quietly. We understand that: you are weighing a salary you cannot verify against a cost you cannot yet see, for a job you have only read about. Our aim is to give you real numbers and an honest account of the work, so the decision is yours rather than ours — including the version where the answer turns out to be no.

Start with what leaves your account

Pay is the exciting half, but the costs are the half that catches people out — assessment, registration, the visa, flights, shipping and a rental bond tend to land close together, and mostly before your first pay arrives. The calculator uses published fees for your route, shows what an employer might contribute, and keeps the figure so you can come back to it.

What you would earn

Pay you can actually check

Here is something unusual about both countries: in the public systems, pay is set by collective agreement and published. Not negotiated in the dark, not dependent on how well you interview. That means we can show you indicative bands and name the agreement they come from — and it means you can verify us. We keep figures rough on purpose, rounded so neither end overstates, because a precise number against a role we have not seen would be a promise we are not in a position to make.

New Zealand

APEX and the union agreements cover most allied health and technical roles; the ASMS MECA covers senior doctors. Steps are published, so you can usually work out where you would land.

What might not suit you

The things people wish they had known

We would rather you heard these from us now than discovered them in month three. None of them is a reason not to come. All of them are reasons some people decide not to, and that is a perfectly good outcome.

A pay rise is not always a pay rise

If you are coming from a no-income-tax country, the gross figure can look excellent and the net one ordinary. Housing may cost more than you are used to as well. Run both against the cost of living before you get attached to the headline.

Progression is slower and flatter

Public-system scales are transparent, which is the good news. It also means less room to negotiate, and seniority often counts for more than performance. Some people find that fairer; others find it frustrating.

You may be further from family than any map suggests

Flights are long and expensive, and a two-week visit is not a weekend trip. This is the single most common thing people underestimate, and it is worth an honest conversation at home before anything else.

The first year costs more than the calculator says

Setting up a household from nothing — a car, furniture, a deposit — is real money that no relocation package covers in full. Build a buffer, not just a budget.

If any of this gives you pause, that is the page doing its job. Talk it through with us — we would rather have an honest conversation than a placement that unravels.
