Where clinicians live

Geelong Guide · Chapter 5 of 9

Geelong is compact enough that most commutes stay under twenty minutes, which frees you to choose on lifestyle rather than logistics. The real decision is inner city, growth corridor, or the Bellarine.

Make one decision first

Before you look at a single listing: fix your work site. The hospital and university precinct sits close to the CBD, while some services and clinics operate further out — and while nothing here is a long drive by Melbourne standards, the difference between a ten-minute and a thirty-minute commute is worth knowing before you sign a lease.

Then choose your shape of life. Inner suburbs mean cafés and walkability. The southern growth corridor means new housing and more space. The Bellarine means beach life with a genuine commute attached.

The areas, honestly

Newtown & Geelong West
Leafy and established, with Pakington Street’s cafés and shops on the doorstep. Suits professionals without children or those who want to walk to dinner. Least space per dollar.
Herne Hill & Hamlyn Heights
Similar character a little further from the centre, with somewhat better value for a family-sized block.
Highton & Wandana Heights
Established and family-friendly, close to well-regarded schools and a short drive from the hospital precinct.
Belmont & Grovedale
Established southern suburbs near the Barwon River and the Waurn Ponds precinct, popular with families.
Armstrong Creek
The city’s major growth corridor to the south — the newest and most affordable family housing, with a longer commute in.
The Bellarine towns
Ocean Grove, Barwon Heads, Drysdale and Portarlington offer genuine beach life with a twenty-five to forty-minute commute, and are increasingly sought after for exactly that reason.
Corio & Norlane
The city’s more affordable northern suburbs, close to the ring road and undergoing gradual renewal.

Renting first is the sensible order

Almost everyone we place rents for six to twelve months before buying, and Geelong’s market moves with the seasons as much as anything else — competition for Bellarine rentals rises sharply over summer. Arriving with something secured for your first month takes the pressure off the decision that matters more.

How leases, bonds, applications and tenancy rights work is the same across Australia and is covered once in Moving to Australia. Nothing about renting in Geelong is legally different; it is the timing and the local competition that vary.

Practical, in order
  • Fix your work site before you choose a suburb.
  • Decide between inner city, growth corridor, or the Bellarine.
  • Rent for the first six to twelve months, and secure something before you fly.
  • Walk the street at the time of day you would actually be coming home.
  • If you are drawn to the Bellarine, drive the commute at peak on a summer weekend before committing — the traffic is real in January.
The short version

Nothing in Geelong is a difficult commute by Melbourne standards, so choose on the life rather than the drive. Inner city for walkability, the growth corridor for space, the Bellarine for the beach — and rent first, because the Bellarine market moves with the summer season.
