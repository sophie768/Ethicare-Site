## Each one, with the line it came from

### Physiotherapist, New Zealand
> **People can come straight to you.**
> *Under ACC, injury care is funded before the patient arrives. Manageable caseloads, and a ten-step scale that rises each year.*

*Page: "direct access instead of waiting on a referral, earlier intervention, greater
clinical autonomy."*

### Occupational Therapist, New Zealand
> **You won't be fighting for funding.**
> *ACC pays for assessment, equipment and home modifications directly. Autonomy, manageable caseloads, a system that backs rehab.*

*Page: "The clinical skill travels; who pays for the plan does not… the no-fault accident
scheme funds OT assessment and intervention directly."*

### Psychologist, New Zealand
> **Get your scope right before you apply.**
> *Your registered scope, not your job title, decides which roles you can take. Strong demand, real autonomy, meaningful caseloads.*

*Page: "The step that costs people most time is applying under the wrong scope."*

### Sonographer, Australia
> **Sonographers don't register through Ahpra.**
> *ASMIRT first, then ASAR — and one of the world's largest private imaging markets on the other side.*

*Page: "Sonographers don't follow the Ahpra route. Two steps, in order."*

### Sonographer, New Zealand
> **Sonographers can go straight to residence.**
> *Very high demand nationwide, autonomous scanning, and every application assessed on its own.*

*Page: "Sonography is on the Tier 1 Green List, with a Straight to Residence pathway."*

### Nuclear Medicine Technologist, Australia
> **There is no fast track into this one.**
> *Every application is assessed individually — and the skills assessment is run by ANZSNM, not ASMIRT. SPECT/CT, PET/CT and a fast-growing theranostics role.*

*Page: "there is no HCPC or CORU shortcut… getting that wrong costs weeks."*

### Radiographer, Australia
> **Register once. Work in any state.**
> *Strong demand, a very large private market, and the quickest route in if you hold HCPC or CORU.*

*Page: "One national registration covers every state and territory."*

### Radiation Therapist, Australia
> **You need a licence as well as registration.**
> *Registration lets you practise; the state licence lets you use radiation. Modern linac fleets and a growing private oncology sector.*

*Page: "registration lets you practise, the state licence lets you use radiation, and a
skills assessment is only for certain visas."*

### Consultant Radiologist, Australia
> **One assessment shapes your whole first year.**
> *RANZCR decides how comparable your training is, and that finding sets your route. A large public and private market, and mixed appointments that make the week negotiable.*

*Page: "Substantially comparable is the most direct route… A partially comparable finding
adds requirements and months."*

### Allied Health, Australia
> **Half of allied health registers. Half doesn't.**
> *Physiotherapy, occupational therapy and podiatry register through national boards. Speech pathology, dietetics and exercise physiology don't — and which side you're on changes your whole route in.*

## What changed from the first draft

The acronyms came out of the headlines and moved down into the subtitle, where there's
room to explain them — RANZCR, Green List, ASMIRT, "division", "regulator shortcut". The
headline now uses only words someone reads at speed on a phone.
